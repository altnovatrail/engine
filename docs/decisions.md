## The choice rule

**R03. The core of the choice rule is the rule of standalone on the module's skills: a skill is open when its prerequisites are "mastered" by the model; reachable when its easiest task in the pool has P ≥ 0.70; the branches go in the order of standalone — consolidation, probe, the place of repetition, new skill, queue — and each has its own code `ReasonCode`; the task on a skill is in the corridor 0.70–0.85, with the learner's frequent traps, the nearest by P to 0.775. The frequent traps and the interests are not branches but the choice of a task on a skill. The engine does not choose an item within a task. The history of issues the engine gets as a summary from `api`.** The author's decisions of 2026-11-07, in the planning and execution of T01 of RUN.md, are seven, all by the recommendations; the rest is a proposal of the plan, which the author approved. The rule step by step is [architecture.md](architecture.md), subsection 3.1.
- **The split of T01** — into three tasks: the core of the rule in one module for skills with a verdict — T01; several modules and anchor skills — T09; lessons and the author's order — T10 (rule 11 of RUN.md). Repetition is still T02.
- **The order of the branches** is as in standalone: the lesson of an open run (T10), a skill from the map (T09), consolidation, probe, repetition (T02), new skill, queue; when all the reachable ones are mastered — the same queue over all of them, and when none are reachable — "the bottom of the ladder", the skills of the module's first band (SPEC 3.2 standalone). In the module's trial series there is no consolidation and no probe.
- **A prerequisite is mastered** — the model's "mastered" at any band. A prerequisite with `rubric` or `none` is counted by the author's order — T10.
- **An item** the engine does not choose: the choice is a lesson, a task or "nothing to give", as `ChoiceKind` (R99 spec).
- **The summary of issues** is a new input from `api`: for a skill — the time of the last issue and, if the last closed attempt was a probe, whether it was correct; for a module — the number of tasks issued; for a task — when its solution last went to the device. From it come the queue, the turn of a probe, the return from a pause and the pause before a task with a seen solution.
- **A task with a seen solution** is not a candidate for 24 hours, and after that it comes after all the tasks of the skill without a seen solution, first the one whose solution went the longest ago (R82 spec).
- **The double turn of a "lagging" skill** (R300 standalone) is not in the base.
- **A proposal of the plan, not the author's answer:** a mastered skill goes into the queue again when its recommended task is above the mastery band, as in SPEC 2.5 standalone, and the model's "mastered" stays; the frequent traps are chosen among the candidates in the corridor, not after it; the P of a task on a skill is the mean P of its items where this skill is primary; a frequent trap is one that at least two pieces of evidence of the skill found, like the "repeating" of R99 spec; the order of the graph is the tier, then the ID, because the order of the skills in the graph carries no meaning ([format text](../spec/format/README.md), section 12); a trap that AI supposed the rule does not read.

— *Why:*

The engine's types are its own and simple, built around the types of `learner-model`: the graph, the description of a task and the output of reading the engine takes from it as they are, and the candidate with its items' traps, the lesson, the lesson run, the attempt at a task with a rubric closed by "Done", the last attempt, the summary of issues and the choice it keeps as its own. The reason code is a type of its own, `ReasonCode`, with the contract's names and numbers, checked against it by a test. The result of an attempt on a skill comes as facts, and the engine computes "failure". In the input is only what the rules R03–R07 read.** The author's decisions of 2026-11-07 in the planning of T04 of RUN.md — three, all by the recommendations (RUN.md, T04, «Решено автором»); the rest is the plan's proposal, which the author approved. - **From `learner-model`, as they are** (R28 spec; `main` 6a6c843): the module's graph `Graph`, the description of a task `Task` and the output of reading `Readout` for the time of the choice. ## The build for the device

**R10. The engine gives the same choice and the same code on any build of one version of its own and of `learner-model` — by the Go compiler, on any architecture, in WebAssembly too — and is built for the device as it is. - **The checks — from the first code, as in `learner-model`** (R09 learner-model): runs on amd64 with GOAMD64 v1 and v3, with v1 with `GODEBUG=cpu.all=off`, with GOARCH=386 and in js/wasm, the build of wasip1, the test of the rules of code, the differential check of the assembler and a job of its own on `ubuntu-24.04-arm`.

- **The plan's proposal, not the author's answer:**
  - the rules of code — the seven rules of R09 learner-model in what the engine needs ([architecture.md](architecture.md), subsection 3.7): the engine needs no exp and log — P is given by `Readout.Task` — and it does not take `detmath`, and should it need it, by an amendment to this entry;
  - NaN and ∞ in the input are an error, returned as a value, not a choice;
  - the golden vectors of the choice — the input and the choice with its code, the same on all the targets;
  - the job of the gate of `sim` — from the first tag of `sim`, and before it the first version is checked by its own tests (R103 spec).

— *Why:*
- **The engine computes with floating-point numbers** — the P of a task on a skill is the mean of the P of its items, and the recommended task is the one nearest by P to 0.775 (R03). The compiler fuses only a multiplication — with an addition or a subtraction — and a division by a constant with an exact reciprocal it does as a multiplication. The rule as written needs no such operation: the mean is a division by the number of items, a variable, and in an experiment of 2026-11-07 in the container, go1.27.3, `s/float64(n) - 0.775` gave a separate division and subtraction on arm64, ppc64le and riscv64. But let a constant stand in the code: `s*0.5 - 0.775` gave `FNMSUBD` on arm64 and `FMSUB` on ppc64le, `(p1+p2)/2 - 0.775` also on s390x, riscv64 and loong64, and `float64((p1+p2)/2) - 0.775` separate operations on all five. The specification allows this — "An implementation may combine multiple floating-point operations into a single fused operation, possibly across statements" — and an explicit conversion forbids it: "An explicit floating-point type conversion rounds to the precision of the target type, preventing fusion that would discard that rounding" ([architecture.md](architecture.md), section 4). A fused operation rounds once, and a P at the bound of the corridor or the equality of two distances may come out otherwise — and with it the choice. Rule 1 holds this for any code that comes, and the test of the rules and the differential check catch a violation before the merge. The order of addition too: the sum of the items' P is by the item's place, because floating-point addition is not associative.
- **The repeat of a choice** — R07 promises that a repeat by the record gives the same choice, and takes for it the same version of `engine` from the record. The same build it cannot take: `api` builds the engine into its own binary, and between deployments its Go version, architecture or GOAMD64 may change. Without the same bits a repeat would diverge from the record on another build, and the simulator would not see this before the deployment.
- **The same discipline as in `learner-model`** — its proposal to the engine (architecture 3.8 spec), and both libraries are built together. The engine takes P from the model, which already gives the same bits; the engine's own are only means, differences and comparisons, so fewer rules are needed.
- **A port of the test of its own** — the engine's first code does not wait for another repository's task; the test of the rules in `learner-model` is a test file of its module, not a package, and another module cannot call it.
- **Т-10 is open** — how it works offline is still decided by stage 5 (product 11 spec), and it has a path without computation on the device at all: the server chooses a queue in advance, and the device only writes evidence (R09 learner-model).

— *Rejected:* a minimum now — the test of the rules and the differential check, and the runs on the targets and arm64 at stage 5; all the checks of the device at stage 5 — before it nobody would check that a repeat of a choice on another build of `api` gives the same; a shared test of the rules at once — the first code would wait for another repository's task; `detmath` with no need; closing Т-10 now.

## The speed of the choice

**R20. The choice computes what is costly once per call: `ready` builds the pool of tasks with their P and the index "skill → tasks of the pool", and the rule takes a skill's candidates from the index, not by a pass of the whole pool for each skill. It does not sort the list of candidates: it reads its length, the first by `byCorridor`, the first by `byEase` and whether there is a P not below 0.70, and both orders are total — their last key is the ID. The tasks of the pools are ordered by ID once per call — for three checks, the reading of P and the index. The choice, the codes and the errors are the same to the bit.** The author's answer 2026-11-10 in the planning of T38 (RUN.md, T38, «Решено автором»), by the recommendation of the plan; the goal is the answer to В31 («Решено автором» of E4).
- **The index** — by skill, sorted by the skill's ID, and a skill's tasks in it are in the order of their IDs: the order of the pool in the input changes neither the choice nor the lists inside. It is built from a task's primary skills — a superset of what `candidateOf` accepts: a skill primary in an item is among the task's primary skills. The search is over a sorted slice, without a map.
- **`poolTask`** holds a pointer to the candidate in the input and does not copy the items and their P when they are already in place: the rule only reads them.
- **The checks**: `checkIDs` finds a repeated task as a neighbour in the order of IDs — the smallest one is the same; `checkTraps` compares the places of traps pairwise, without copies and sorts, — the same smallest place, — and for a task with traps on more than 16 items sorts them as before, so that a page of many lines does not become quadratic. The order of the checks and the texts of the errors are as before.
- **Small things**: `pause` takes the task's description by pointer; `candidateOf` does not gather a task's traps when the skill has no frequent ones — there is nothing to compare them with; the primary skills of a "Done" task from the pool are taken from the pool; the field `prepared.p` is gone — the rule reads P from the pool.
