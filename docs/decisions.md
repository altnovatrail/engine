# engine decision log

> Why engine is built the way it is. Each entry: what was decided, why, what was rejected, and in its tail "— *Consequence:*" the files, by their paths, that a change of it edits.
>
> **Rule:** do not propose again what was rejected without new facts. If a decision has to change, ask the author and rewrite its entry in its place, and every entry the change touches: an entry is its text in force — what was decided, why, what was rejected and what a change of it edits —, git keeps the old text, and the commit says why. An entry's number stands in its heading alone: an entry cites no other entry and no section, says in words what it rests on, and carries no date of who decided it and no amendment; a claim about the outside world cites its one record of [sources.md](sources.md) or of [measurements.md](measurements.md), or of `spec`'s, as `sources 2.3 spec`.
>
> The repository was set up 2026-10-19 from the template of `spec` v0.1.0; the tag it stands on is the `spec` submodule's, and an entry on a move to a tag says what the tag brings to `engine` and what is changed here. How it comes to differ from the template — tools in the devcontainer, rules in RUN.md — is recorded here as a decision. Next number — R32.
>
> Open questions that concern several repositories — О-, П-, Т- — are in the registers of `spec`, in `spec/docs/product.md`, `spec/docs/privacy.md` and `spec/docs/repositories.md`. What is still to decide in the component itself is in [architecture.md](architecture.md).

## The choice rule

**R03. The core of the choice rule is the rule of standalone on the module's skills: a skill is open when its prerequisites are "mastered" by the model or when it has evidence of its own; reachable when its easiest candidate has P ≥ 0.70; the branches go in the order of standalone — consolidation, probe, the place of repetition, new skill, queue — and each has its own code `ReasonCode`; the task on a skill is in the corridor 0.70–0.85, with the learner's frequent traps, the nearest by P to 0.775. The frequent traps and the interests are not branches but the choice of a task on a skill. The engine does not choose an item within a task. The history of issues the engine gets as a summary from `api`.**
- **The order of the branches**: the lesson of an open run, a skill from the map, consolidation, probe, repetition, new skill, the author's order, queue; when all the reachable ones are mastered — the same queue over all of them, "all mastered"; when none is reachable — "the bottom of the ladder": the open skills with candidates whose lowest band where they are taught is the lowest among such skills, in the first slice the module's first band. A skill without a band where it is taught does not get onto the bottom. On the bottom the queue first takes a skill not mastered for the queue, and once every skill issued there is, it chooses over all of them, as "all mastered" does. The trial series, the turn of a probe and the bottom are each module's own, and in the trial series there is no consolidation and no probe.
- **The codes**: consolidation — `REASON_CODE_CONSOLIDATION`; probe — `REASON_CODE_PROBE`; new skill — `REASON_CODE_NEW_SKILL`; queue — `REASON_CODE_QUEUE`, and for a skill with the model's "mastered" back in the rotation `REASON_CODE_ROTATION`; all mastered — `REASON_CODE_ROTATION`; the bottom — `REASON_CODE_NEW_SKILL` for a skill not yet issued and `REASON_CODE_ROTATION` for one issued. A choice of a task always has a code.
- **Open**: each prerequisite of the module is the model's "mastered" at any band. A skill that has evidence of its own, where it is primary, is open whatever becomes of its prerequisites, of the module's skills and of the anchor skills, and practice on a skill from the map opens it so too. A prerequisite with `rubric` or `none` is counted by the author's order. A prerequisite that nothing in the input can teach does not hold back the skill after it; the count is one step: first "open" without this exception, then the exception reads only the tasks whose every other primary skill is open by that "open", and a passed lesson teaches nothing.
- **The candidates of a skill** are the tasks of the pool where it is primary — an item's own skill, and the task's skill for an item without one and for a task without items, as `learner-model` computes it in `NewGraph` —, that have an item with a verdict on it and whose every other primary skill is open; a skill from the map takes its tasks without the last condition. A closed skill has candidates too, for the map, but only an open one is reachable, and "reachable" reads the candidates of its own pass. **The P of a task on a skill** is the mean P of its items with a verdict where the skill is primary.
- **A failure** is a closed attempt where the skill has fewer than half correct first verdicts, or the solution shown and no correct first verdict; an attempt without first verdicts on the skill and without the solution shown is neither a failure nor correct. A skill whose last closed attempt, a probe or a repetition, is correct is reachable until the next closed attempt.
- **Mastered for the queue**: the model's "mastered" while the skill's recommended task is not above its mastery band; above it the skill goes into the queue again, and the model's "mastered" stays. The recommended task sees no pause.
- **The task on a skill**: in the corridor first the one that catches more of the skill's frequent traps; of those that catch equally many, the one whose caught traps stand higher in the order "number of pieces of evidence, then the time of the last one, then the ID", compared in ascending order; then the nearness to 0.775, the greater P and the ID. With none in the corridor — the nearest to 0.775, of two the easier. A probe takes the easiest; on pause every candidate has P < 0.70, so the easiest is also the nearest. A frequent trap is one that at least two pieces of evidence of the skill found, as an adult's progress counts a repeating trap; a task's traps are those of its items where the skill is primary. A trap that AI supposed the rule does not read (Д6).
- **A task with a seen solution**, a task with a key whose solution went to the device less than 24 hours ago, is not a candidate; later it comes after the skill's tasks without a seen solution, first the one whose solution went the longest ago. The solution of a task with a rubric, open after "Done", does not count, and `api` gives no such time for it: the author's order reads such a task by `RubricDone`. The task of the last closed attempt whose solution went to the device is not a candidate of the next choice, however long ago it went.
- **The second pass**: when the choice gave nothing — no lesson, no task, in no module — the rule goes through once more with the tasks from the pause as candidates, after the others, first the one whose solution went the longest ago, but not the task of the last closed attempt; "nothing to give" comes only after it.
- **The summary of issues**, an input from `api`: for a skill, the time of its last issue and, if its last closed attempt was a probe or a repetition, its result; for a module, the number of its choices; for a task, when its solution last went to the device. From it come the queue, the turn of a probe, the return from a pause and the pause before a task with a seen solution.
- **The turn of a probe**: n is `Choices` of the module's row in the summary, the module's choices before this one, a lesson as one, and 0 without a row; the turn is n > 5 and n mod 6 = 5, first at the module's 12th choice. A probe takes a skill on pause with candidates. "Not issued for the longest" — one never issued first, then by the time of the last issue — is one order for the probe, the queue, "all mastered" and the bottom, and a tie goes by the order of the graph: the tier, then the ID, byte by byte.
- **Consolidation** reads the profile's last closed attempt only when its task is of this module, by the start of its ID, `TrialOver` is set and `LastInTrial` is not, so that neither an error in the trial series nor one of its last answer is worked through; of several failed skills, the first in the order of the graph.
- **One package `engine`**: the rule is its unexported functions, which the import list and the test of the rules hold as they are.

— *Why:*
- **standalone as the base** — the engine starts from the rule of standalone (sources 1.1), whose order passed standalone's simulator, and a change of the rule passes NovaTrail's simulator by a criterion written before the first candidate. The upper bound of the corridor, 0.85, is the "85 % rule"; the lower one standalone takes from Math Garden, whose primary source could not be read (sources 2.1);
- **the model's "mastered"** — a second threshold of the engine would diverge from the skill map and from knowing an anchor skill, which read the same "mastered";
- **open by its own evidence** — otherwise a skill would flicker, open, closed, open again, when two failures in a row take "mastered" off a prerequisite, which comes back into the rotation anyway; an unreachable skill the rule gives only by a probe;
- **the other primary skills open** — otherwise a task chosen for an open skill would give evidence to a closed skill that shares it, and that one would open past its prerequisites; and a prerequisite P and a skill Q after it, primary in one task, would wait for each other forever if "nothing to teach with" counted that task;
- **nothing to teach with** — when the lesson or the tasks of a prerequisite do not suit the learner by age, the skill after it would be closed forever; with AI closed, while it is closed. The price: with AI closed a skill may open before its prerequisite is "mastered". The count in one step is finite and unambiguous, and errs on the side of "does not hold";
- **traps are not a branch** — in standalone the traps went into the posing of a task, not the choice of a topic, and the platform gave them no code of their own; **in the corridor** — after the choice by P they would decide only at an exact equality of P; **their order breaks a tie** — by their number alone the order of traps would decide nothing;
- **without an item** — `ChoiceKind` knows no item, and a task is the unit of an attempt: its check, its evidence and its solution belong to the task as a whole;
- **the summary of issues** — `Read` gives the time of practice, not of issue: a task issued without evidence would not move the queue, and a skill with abandoned tasks would be issued again and again; and only the summary says which task's solution went and when. A summary, not a log: the learner's data in the call are as much as the rule reads;
- **24 hours, and then after the unseen** — on the small pool of the first slice, three tasks per skill and band, a ban forever would soon leave a skill without tasks, and a pause of a week would keep it without tasks needlessly; the model counts a repeat as help anyway and sets no "mastered" by it;
- **not the last task next** — the platform never gives a task with a seen solution as the next one, and the pause of 24 hours held this only while the learner came back the same day: past the pause a skill without another task got the same task again, which the invariant `I4` of the gate of `sim` found;
- **the second pass** — the key and the solution go to the device after a correct answer too, and a lesson of the first slice holds all the tasks of its skill and band: after it they would all be on pause, and the learner would hear "nothing to give" with a pool that is not empty; the last task still does not come back;
- **an attempt without first verdicts** — otherwise a probe the learner left without an answer would return the skill from a pause; **an item without a verdict** gives no evidence on the skill, and with P = 0 it would pull the mean down;
- **the return into the rotation** — "mastered" at the band `grades-1-2` says nothing about the tasks of `grades-3-4`, as in standalone (sources 1.1), and the platform leaves this to the engine's rule;
- **the bottom by the lowest band taught** — "the easiest the catalog has" of standalone (sources 1.3): the rule does not answer "nothing to give" when the open skills have candidates only above the first band; **a tie of the probe by the order of the graph** — a lesson issues all its skills at once, and the rule relies on each tie being broken;
- **without the "lagging" skill** — in standalone a topic where the child is "behind" counts twice (sources 1.3), but "behind" reads the rank, which in NovaTrail belongs to the skill map, and the rule would need the module's level from the model; it is the first candidate for the simulator.

— *Rejected:* a trap as a branch with its own code; frequent traps only at an equality of P, or ordered by their number alone; new skills mixed with the queue by a share; "mastered" at the learner's band, and a threshold of the engine's own; the choice of an item as a new kind of `ChoiceKind`; the queue and the turn of a probe by `Read` alone; a log of the last issues instead of the summary; pauses of N days and a ban forever for a task with a seen solution; the task of the last attempt held back by the 24 hours alone — the rule is about the order of issue, not time; the double turn of the "lagging" skill; a mastered skill out of the rotation forever; the P of a task as the product or the minimum of its items' — for a page of a workbook of ten items it would be almost zero at any preparation; "correct" as all that is not a failure; holding a skill back by a prerequisite with nothing to teach with when AI is closed; a fixed point instead of the one step — more exact in rare chains, but harder to explain and to check; n as the number of this choice, `Choices` + 1; the rule in `internal/rule` — the input types would pass through a second package, and the import list and the test of the rules would grow.
- **The judged orders of the task on a skill.** A, "the corridor before the seen"; B, "after the pause the seen is as the unseen"; C, "A and consolidation only in the corridor": the judgement of `sim`, 1,000 learners a cell, scored them all better than the base — 0.124, 0.130 and 0.127 —, but none held every "not worse": B failed thirty, most `E4.given` on olympiad maths, since a repeat with a seen solution sets no "mastered"; A and C three each, by the model's lag `M3.lag` on `G2`, and C also `E1.corridor` of school maths on `G2`, worse by at most 0.01004 at a tolerance of 0.01 written before the first candidate (sources 6.12).

**R04. On several modules the rule of one module works within each, and the modules go in turn: the turn goes to the module whose issue was the longest ago, and consolidation after an error stays in the module of that attempt. A prerequisite that is an anchor skill holds the skill back at "does not know" with any reason, "no evidence" too, but for two cases: a cycle of prerequisites between modules, and an anchor that nothing in the input can teach. A skill chosen on the map holds until the learner asks for something else, and it gives a lesson or a task while the pool or the lessons hold one on it. The engine reads neither the age category nor the daily goal, and has no short check of an anchor skill at "unknown".**
- **"My subjects"**: the lesson of an open run; consolidation by the profile's last closed attempt, in the engine's module whose full ID its task begins with — an attempt outside the engine's modules leads to none; then the turn of the module whose issue was the longest ago, and in it the rule of one module from the probe down. A module's time of issue is the latest `LastIssued` among the rows of the summary whose full ID begins with the module's ID, so a skill that its new version dropped counts too; the issue of a lesson is the issue of each of its skills. A module without issues goes first, and a tie goes by the module's full ID, byte by byte.
- **An anchor**: `core.<name>` holds a skill back when `Read` gives "does not know" — "not mastered" or "no evidence"; "knows" and "unknown" do not hold. An anchor that a graph of the engine's modules names without the model's reading, or with a status outside the list, is an input error.
- **A cycle between modules** is a strongly connected component of the graph G of the engine's modules, whose edges go from a skill to its prerequisite of the module and to each skill whose `Teaches` names its anchor prerequisite. An anchor whose teaching skill lies in one component with the skill that needs it does not hold that skill back.
- **Nothing to teach with**: "does not know" does not hold either when no teaching skill of the anchor has a task in the pool, with a pause or without, or a lesson. The teaching skills are those of the engine's modules whose `Teaches` names the anchor, read from the graphs, not from `AnchorReadout.Teachers`, so that G sees them too. A skill teaches only if the model can make it "mastered" — `verdict` and a band where it is taught — and only by a task the rule can give it: an item with a verdict where it is primary, every other primary skill open by "open" without this exception; or by a lesson not yet passed of its module whose skills name it. A teaching skill that is closed or unreachable holds; the pause does not enter the count, because it will end.
- **A skill from the map** holds until `ChooseNext` comes without a skill: the record of the choice keeps the skill and carries it into the next one, and `api` calls the engine for `next` and at the end of a run with it, whatever code the choice came out with. It is given closed, on pause and in the trial series too: one with `verdict` — a lesson on the skill or a task by "the task on a skill"; one with `rubric` or `none` — a lesson not yet passed that teaches it, without waiting for its predecessors; a skill outside the engine's modules — "nothing to give". The lesson of an open run in this mode is only a lesson that teaches the skill.
- **The map gives something.** The second pass takes the task of the last closed attempt too, after all the others, after those with a seen solution too. A skill with `verdict` that has neither a lesson on the skill nor a task left gets a lesson not yet passed that teaches it, a lesson of the author's order and one of another band than its recommended task alike, in the order of lessons; on the second pass, after the tasks on pause and the task of the last attempt, the lesson whose last run "passed" ended the longest ago, as a new run. A skill with `rubric` or `none` without such a lesson, a task without "Done" or a passed lesson gets the task with `rubric` whose attempt "Done" closed the longest ago, as a new attempt.
- **Left for something else**: an attempt the learner left by asking for something else — "left unfinished" — leads to no consolidation in any mode, and the run whose segment it was is not resumed this time.
- **No short check of an anchor at "unknown"**: the check is the answers on the very skill that needs the anchor, which the rule leads by consolidation, a pause and a probe; the module that teaches the anchor `api` offers by the module index. The rule, the input and the codes stay.

— *Why:*
- **the modules in turn** — the turns are equal whatever number of skills the author split a module into: olympiad maths has 17, school maths of grade 3 four (sources 3.1). The rule of one module does not change, and its branches have all they need per module already. The time of a module's issue comes from the summary, with no new input. The levels of modules are not compared: their scales are their own and do not add up;
- **consolidation before the turn** — after an error, one more task on the same skill; otherwise it would wait until the turn went round the other modules;
- **"no evidence" holds** — a skill is offered when its prerequisites are mastered, the module that teaches the anchor is connected, and the turn itself leads to it. Not to hold would open the skill only for it to close with the first answers in another module, as `learner-model` found for "unknown" (sources 5.1);
- **a cycle does not hold** — the format checks each module alone and sees no cycle between modules, and without this both sides would wait for each other forever; a component needs no choice of the edge to drop. Within a module there is no cycle, and a skill never rests on an anchor of its own module, so a cycle always goes through several modules; the first slice has none;
- **nothing to teach with** — otherwise the skill would wait for tasks the rule will not give, closed forever; **an input error** — counting the anchor "unknown" would guess about knowing;
- **a skill from the map holds** — so a topic a person chose held in standalone, until reset (sources 1.4). It is a field of the record of the choice, not of the profile: `api` keeps the record anyway, a repeat of the choice needs the skill as an input, and the code and the task cannot restore it — the lesson of an open run goes with `REASON_CODE_OPEN_RUN`, and a primary skill exists only at an item;
- **the map gives something** — the platform requires the skill map to give a lesson or a task on a chosen skill while the pool and the lessons hold one, and such a choice comes out empty only when `api` has limited the pool to tasks without AI: without this a learner whose skill has one task, just solved, or only passed lessons would hear "nothing to give". The task of the last attempt goes after all the others, so that it comes only when there is no other: the learner chose the skill. A lesson not yet passed goes on the first pass, since it gives the new, and a passed one on the second, after the tasks;
- **left unfinished** — otherwise a request for something else would bring back the same skill;
- **without the age category** — `api` gives the engine only the modules and the lessons that suit the learner, and a task only to a learner of its `age_categories`; the learner's data in the call are as much as the rule reads. **Without the daily goal** — it is neither in the profile nor in the contract;
- **no short check** — a task cannot check an anchor: an anchor is only ever a supporting skill of a task, which the model does not move, and in `olympiad-math` 0.1.1 every task with an anchor as a supporting skill is a task of the very skill that needs it, 16 each for `fractions-parts` and `number-divisibility` — the tasks the base gives after a lesson anyway. Holding the skill back for an anchor nothing teaches would close it forever. The price of the base is probes on one skill, and the skills after it without evidence of their own do not open if the learner never learns the anchor; there is no dead end between modules, and the module goes on by other skills. A module is offered by `api`, only of those it holds and that suit the learner; the engine reads no index, `ChoiceKind` knows no module, and `api` gives an anchor "unknown" with its reason and its teachers by the skill map already.

— *Rejected:* one common queue of the skills of all modules — a module's share would follow the number of its skills; modules in blocks of k tasks — one more constant for the simulator without an argument; "no evidence" as "unknown"; breaking one edge of a cycle by the order of ID — the order is arbitrary; a check of cycles by the module index on the platform's side — the first slice has no cycles, and the engine will not get stuck anyway; an anchor without a reading as "unknown"; a choice from the map for one choice; a field of the profile, like standalone's `lesson_topic` — it lives apart from the record of the choice, and a repeat does not see it; the mode by the code of the last choice — the lesson of an open run loses it; a filter by age in the engine; closing a skill with evidence of its own when knowing goes away; asking the platform for an exception to its rule of the map — the map promises the learner that a choice gives something; a passed lesson before a lesson of the author's order — the one not yet passed gives the new; a check task before a lesson on a skill with an anchor "unknown", with a new value of `ReasonCode` — it differs from the base only by the order "lesson, task", and the platform brought in no such value; a check task of a new kind in the format, with evidence for the anchor — work of the format, `module-kit` and `learner-model`; a question to the adult whether the learner knows the anchor — that is `app`, the contract and the learner's data, and a verdict is given by a program; a field of the choice "anchor without a teacher" — `api` knows it from `Read`; `ChoiceKind` "module" — the engine would need the index in its input.

— *Consequence:* `rule.go`, `open.go`, `skills.go`, `lessons.go`, `choose.go` and their tests; [architecture.md](architecture.md).

**R05. A lesson is given on the new: to a skill with a verdict — when it is new or when its recommended task has gone into a band whose lesson the learner has not yet passed; a lesson's band is the set of its tasks' bands. There is no consolidation in a lesson and no early end in the first slice. Lessons whose skills are not all with a verdict go in the author's order — by the graph, and where the graph gives no order, by tier, then by the lesson's ID. When the choice gave nothing, the rule passes a second time with the tasks whose solution was seen less than a day ago, and only then gives "nothing to give".**
- **A lesson on the new.** A lesson is passed when a run of it ended "passed", on any package. When new skill, queue, "all mastered" or the bottom has chosen a skill with a verdict, it gets a lesson instead of a task if a lesson not yet passed, without an open run, has all its skills with `verdict`, this one among them, and the band of the skill's recommended task among its bands; the lesson's code is the branch's. The recommended task is among the skill's tasks in the pool without regard to the pause, and a lesson without tasks suits any band. Outside the map a lesson that names another skill not yet issued, which waits for its lesson of the author's order, is not a lesson on the skill. Consolidation, a probe and repetition are tasks only.
- **Without consolidation in a lesson and without an early end.** A lesson goes in the author's order, and at the end of a segment `api` does not call the engine. An error of a lesson's last task is consolidated by the choice after the run's end: the last closed attempt is its task.
- **The author's order** — lessons whose skills are not all with `verdict`, a mixed one too — is a branch between new skill and queue: the first such lesson not yet passed, without an open run, whose predecessors of the author's order by the graph are all passed — a lesson whose skills all have `verdict` holds none —, with `REASON_CODE_AUTHOR_ORDER`. Lesson A goes after B as the format defines it: a skill of A, through the module's prerequisites, transitively, requires a skill of B that A does not have; anchors order nothing. "The first in the order of lessons" of a set is a lesson that goes after no other of the set, then by tier — the lowest of its skills' — and ID, byte by byte; if each goes after another, as runs on an earlier package may, by tier and ID among all. Lessons of different modules are not ordered, and of the modules' first lessons the first goes by full ID.
- **A skill with `verdict` that a lesson of the author's order not yet passed teaches** is, while not issued, taken neither by new skill, repetition and the queue nor by "all mastered" and the bottom: it comes by this lesson. The price: when the learner left the run of the lesson before it and there is no other, the choice is "nothing to give".
- **A task with `rubric`** — one where the primary skill of an item is with `rubric` — outside lessons, named by no lesson the engine gets, stands in the author's order as a lesson of its primary skills with `rubric`: it is ready when their lessons of the author's order are passed, and those their lesson would go after; it goes after all the ready lessons, then in the order of the graph and by its ID, without a closed attempt "Done", with its primary skills with `verdict` open and the pause of a candidate.
- **A prerequisite with `rubric` or `none` is counted** by a run "passed" whose `Run.Skills` name it, or by "Done" of a task where it is primary whose description is in the pool or in `Lesson.Tasks` of the engine's modules; without a description it does not count. **It is taught** by a lesson not yet passed, or by a task with `rubric` the rule can give — outside the lessons of its module by the author's order, or in its lesson not yet passed — with its primary skills with `verdict` open: a prerequisite with `none` when it is primary in the task, one with `rubric` when it is primary in the task's item with `rubric`. A prerequisite with `verdict` has nothing to teach with when it has no band where it is taught, or neither a lesson not yet passed nor a task of the pool with an item with a verdict on it whose other primary skills are open.
- **The lesson of an open run**: of the open runs whose lesson begins with the ID of one of the engine's modules, the first in the order of lessons by `Run.Skills` on the module's graph — a run may be on an earlier package —, then by the lesson's full ID. No other branch gives a lesson with an open run.
- **On the map** a skill with `rubric` or `none` takes its tasks by the pause before a seen solution, as candidates, then those without "Done" first, the older "Done", the ID; its lessons by how long ago the last "passed" was, then the ID. A lesson on a skill from the map does not need its skills open, and with no recommended task any of its lessons suits.
- **No new checks of the input**: a run outside the engine's modules and "Done" of a task that is not in the input read as "no".

— *Why:*
- **a lesson on the new** — a lesson explains, and a task may be given for practice without an explanation. A lesson of the first slice holds all the tasks of a skill and a band, and on a new skill or band the explanation goes before the tasks; a lesson until it is passed, in every branch, would make a whole lesson of consolidation;
- **without consolidation in a lesson** — the platform keeps a place for it, not an obligation, and the variant judged by `sim` was no better than the base (below). On packages whose pool is the lessons' tasks, consolidation after the run's end is weak: after the lesson its tasks are on pause, and an error of its last task is worked through only after the pause or by the second pass, by a task whose key the learner saw minutes ago; on `olympiad-math` 0.1.1 and later, with tasks outside lessons, it takes such a task at once;
- **a band is the set of the tasks' bands** — a lesson has no band field, and `modules` rejected one; the band of the recommended task, not the start band, keeps the lesson following the learner;
- **the second pass** — without it a lesson left a skill without tasks for a day;
- **the author's order by the graph, tier and ID** — skills with `rubric` and `none` have no learner model, and the order of lessons is set only by the graph. The format computes the tier from the graph — the length of the longest chain of prerequisites —, so it is the graph's order, as for skills; the ID is the one other order the author holds, and the order of files carries no meaning. By ID alone the essays' `paragraphs` would go before `reading-model-essays`;
- **a mixed lesson by the author's order** — in a lesson whose skills are not all with a verdict the engine adds nothing and skips nothing, and `REASON_CODE_AUTHOR_ORDER` names exactly this;
- **a skill waiting for its lesson taken by no branch** — "all mastered" and the bottom would give a skill not yet issued with `REASON_CODE_ROTATION`, "a skill given before";
- **a task with `rubric` waits for the lessons of its skills and theirs** — a skill without a lesson would give a task before the lessons it needs;
- **taught only by a task the rule can give** — "Done" of a task with `rubric` counts a prerequisite with `none` too, and a skill after it would open before the learner did the task; a task no branch will give — of a passed lesson, or one where a skill with `verdict` closed by this same prerequisite is primary — would make them wait for each other, and the rule would give "nothing to give".

— *Rejected:* a lesson until it is passed, in any branch; a lesson once per skill, without a lesson of a new band; a lesson of only one band; a lesson of the start band; a strict "nothing to give" with a non-empty pool; a mixed lesson by new skill with `REASON_CODE_NEW_SKILL`; unordered lessons by ID alone, with the examples fixed; a skill waiting for its lesson held back by new skill alone; a task with `rubric` waiting only for the lessons of its own skill; any task of the pool, or a lesson alone, as the teacher of a prerequisite with `rubric` or `none`.
- **Consolidation in a lesson, judged.** After a failure of a task segment of a lesson whose skills are all with a verdict, `api` would call the engine, and a task on the failed skill, within reach among its candidates without the lesson's tasks, would come before the next segment, with `REASON_CODE_CONSOLIDATION`, which fits it word for word; otherwise the lesson of the same run, which is no new choice in the summary. On the map only for the run of a lesson that teaches the skill from the map, and that skill alone. The criterion was the engine's own on a new step of `sim`; the judgement, 2,000 learners a cell, ended in an exit: the variant fails "not worse" by `M3.lag` on `G2-half` of olympiad maths, worse by at most 0.01812 at a tolerance of 0.01785, and is not better — the score −0.0018 [−0.0047; 0.0010], the corridor −0.0002 [−0.0010; 0.0006]: a consolidation is in the corridor about twice as often as a segment, but the segments after it go less often into the corridor and more often into "hard" (sources 6.10). The variant's code is the commit 0e4680f, its rule in [architecture.md](architecture.md). No second round: the tolerance was written before the first candidate, and a failure by a hair is no argument to shift it. A next variant of consolidation in a lesson, or an early end, comes only with new facts — a measure of `sim`, the pilot or an adult's progress that shows an error in a lesson worked through too late.
- **An early end** — by difficulty, after a failed consolidation, it needs a new end reason, a new field of the output and work of `api` and `app`; by mastery it has almost no cases, since a cautious "mastered" is almost never set after one or two tasks of a lesson. A new value "consolidation in a lesson" of `ReasonCode`, likewise.

— *Consequence:* `lessons.go`, `open.go`, `rule.go`, `skills.go`, `choose.go` and their tests; [architecture.md](architecture.md).

**R06. Repetition is a task on a mastered skill whose date has come, with the code `REASON_CODE_REPETITION`, on the module's turn of repetition — once in three of its choices, — and when there is nothing new, no author's order and no queue, outside the turn too. The task is in the corridor by P with retention. The rule does not read the daily goal.**
- **The turn of repetition**: n > 5 and n mod 3 = 1 — n = 7, 10, 13 and so on —, with the same counter n of the module's choices as the probe, whose turn, n mod 6 = 5, has n mod 3 = 2, so the two never coincide; the end of the trial series is no condition. A turn taken by the lesson of an open run or by consolidation does not come back. When new skill, the author's order and the queue have nothing to give, a skill due for review goes outside the turn too, before "all mastered" and the bottom. There is no day boundary; the 3 and the 1 are constants of the engine beside those of the probe, and only the simulator changes them.
- **Repetition is a task**, not a lesson: a lesson is given on the new.
- **The task** is the first by "the task on a skill": in the corridor by P with retention, and for an unreachable skill, all of whose P are below 0.70, the nearest to 0.775, which is the easiest.
- **A skill due for review** is an open skill with `verdict`, mastered for the queue, with candidates and not waiting for its lesson of the author's order, when the time of the choice is no earlier than `Review.At` or the date is marked `Already`; the date is read only for a skill "mastered" by the model. Of several: "already" first, and of those the one whose last practice is older; then the earliest date; on a tie the order of the graph.
- Skills with `rubric` and `none` are not repeated: they have no model. A failure on repetition goes to consolidation, and two in a row lift "mastered" and return the skill into the queue; a mastered skill whose recommended task went above the mastery band goes into the queue, whatever its date. A correct repetition brings an unreachable skill back from the pause, like a correct probe. There are no new checks of the input.

— *Why:*
- **a turn, not a priority** — in Anki the order of the new and the reviews is a setting, "Whether new cards should be mixed in with review cards, shown before them, or shown after them", but the review limit limits the new too: "By default, the review limit also applies to new cards, and no new cards will be shown when the review limit has been reached", and with a backlog "it is recommended that you stop introducing new cards until you catch up with that backlog" (sources 2.2). For a child with two maths after the holidays this would mean repetitions only, not one new skill. The turn keeps the share of repetitions at a third at most, as the turn of a probe keeps the probes, on the counter there is already;
- **without the end of the series** — the model's series computes the level by the answers on any skill, which repetition does not hinder, and a date at least a day after practice almost never comes in the series;
- **without a day boundary** — a time zone is no input of the engine, and Anki's daily ceiling — "Anki will not show any more review cards for the day, even if there are more waiting" — would need a window of 24 hours in the input. A date comes at least a day after the last practice anyway, but for "already", which comes at once;
- **"already" by the age of practice** — `learner-model` marks "already" when the threshold does not hold even without forgetting, and correct answers do not lift the mark: by the order of the graph alone the first "already" would take every repetition and the second would never be repeated;
- **outside the turn when there is nothing to give** — "all mastered" would give mastered skills without a date, and a skill due for review would wait for its turn;
- **a task in the corridor** — P with retention sees forgetting already, and after a long break the first task is within the learner's powers; a task on the mastery band would check "mastered", which the model holds and lifts itself, by the answers;
- **without the daily goal** — it is neither in the profile nor in the contract, only in the prototype: the rule cannot read it;
- **the same constants for all modules** — constants of their own per subject would put a subject's code in the core, and the language modules, where repetition is required, are not in the first slice.

— *Rejected:* repetition always first while there is a date, as Anki advises; a daily ceiling with a window of 24 hours in the input; repetition by a lesson, when the band's lesson is not passed; a task on the mastery band; a field of the daily goal in the profile and an input of the engine; constants of repetition of their own per subject; waiting for the end of the trial series, as the probe does; "already" by the age of the issue — practice from the map is an issue too, and the difference is small; "already" by the order of the graph alone, with a debt to the simulator; repetition outside the turn after "all mastered" — a skill due for review would wait for its turn; a mastered skill without a date — `Read` gives one to every mastered skill.

— *Consequence:* `rule.go`, `skills.go`, `choose.go` and their tests; [architecture.md](architecture.md).

## Reason codes and the repeat of a choice

**R07. Each branch of the choice rule gives a code from the list `ReasonCode`, and the three places that no earlier code fits — branch 8, the queue at "the bottom of the ladder" and a mastered skill whose task has gone above the mastery band — give its ninth value, `REASON_CODE_ROTATION`, "in rotation". The engine does without randomness: it has no generator and no seed of the choice, and the field "seed" in the record of the choice is empty. The record of the choice also keeps the skill from the map, the age category and the mark of the engine's modules, and the rest the repeat derives from the records of `api`. Golden vectors and a property test hold the codes of the branches and the choice.** The rule and its table of branches and codes are in [architecture.md](architecture.md).
- **The matching.** Exact are: the lesson of an open run — `REASON_CODE_OPEN_RUN`; a skill from the map — `REASON_CODE_SKILL_CHOSEN`; consolidation, by the second pass too, — `REASON_CODE_CONSOLIDATION`; a probe — `REASON_CODE_PROBE`; repetition on the turn, outside the turn and for an unreachable skill — `REASON_CODE_REPETITION`, "the date has come"; a new skill and a lesson on the new, at "the bottom of the ladder" too, — `REASON_CODE_NEW_SKILL`, which does not require reachability and names a skill whose prerequisites the rule counts as met, or which has none: so it holds where the rule opens a skill with a counted, not mastered, prerequisite — an anchor "unknown", a cycle, a prerequisite there is nothing to teach with, a prerequisite with `rubric` or `none` counted by a lesson — and for a skill with its own evidence; the author's order — `REASON_CODE_AUTHOR_ORDER`; the queue of an unmastered reachable skill and the lesson of its new band — `REASON_CODE_QUEUE`, where "the longest" is within its module; the three places above — `REASON_CODE_ROTATION`. "Nothing to give" is without a code. The rule gives each of the nine codes.
- **`REASON_CODE_ROTATION`** = 9 in `cause.proto`: a skill already issued comes again in rotation — first the one not issued for the longest —, though it does not suit the queue `REASON_CODE_QUEUE`: it is mastered or no skill is reachable. It is given by branch 8 — all the reachable ones are mastered and no dates have come —, by the queue of branch 9 and by branch 7 for a skill "mastered" by the model whose recommended task has gone above the mastery band. The meaning of the former codes does not change: `REASON_CODE_QUEUE` says "within reach" and "not mastered", and none of the three places fits it.
- **Without randomness.** The engine takes no generator, and it has no seed of the choice and no places of randomness of its own.
- **The record of the choice** — beside what the platform's record of a choice names, three fields: the skill from the map; the profile's age category at the time of the choice; a mark of which of the digests are the engine's modules, connected and suiting the learner. The field "seed" is empty: only an engine that needs randomness has a seed, and that the rule takes none is seen by the version of `engine` in the same record.

— *Why:*
- **without randomness** — the rule of standalone was deterministic — "the same profile always produces the same brief" (sources 1.1); each tie of the rule is broken by the order of the graph or by ID — in the corridor, in the queue, in the rotation of modules, for lessons and for dates; the variety of tasks comes from templates and the attempt's seed; "the same input gives the same output" the simulator checks without a seed too. The price: learners in one state get one task and one order of unordered lessons; the variety between learners standalone took from the hash of the learner's ID (sources 1.1), which is not among the engine's inputs;
- **one value, not three** — the three places have one meaning for the adult: the skill came again in rotation, not because it is being worked on. A new value is a new code in `spec` and new words in `app` in all the interface languages, and three such values would distinguish what the adult does not need to know;
- **branch 8 stays** — without it a module where everything is mastered and no dates have come would give nothing, and with one module the learner would hear "nothing to give" with a non-empty pool — against the criterion of the simulator;
- **three fields** — without them the repeat will not restore the input: the skill from the map changes the whole choice, by the age category `api` selects the pool and the lessons (sources 4.1), and the category changes at the yearly refinement and at a change of country; connections change at any time, and the record's digests also name the modules of the log, which only `learner-model` gets. The rest the records of `api` already hold: the summary of issues the repeat folds from the records of choices and attempts up to the time of the choice, the last attempt and its trap — from the records of attempts, and the runs and the attempts with a rubric it restores anyway. The rule reads neither interests nor the daily goal, and a record holds no more of the learner's data than the rule reads;
- **the seed is empty** — the repeat takes the same version of `engine` as the choice, so a future version with randomness does not hinder the old choices, and a seed that the rule does not read repeats nothing;

— *Rejected:* randomness in ties by a seed of the choice; three new values; removing branch 8; a snapshot of the whole input in the record — twice as much of the learner's data as the rule reads; only the skill from the map in the record — an old choice would not repeat after a change of the category or of the connections; a seed always, in case of a future version; vectors without the bits of P, and a table in Go; only the vectors; consolidation in a lesson as a branch 0 with the former codes — `sim`'s judgement did not choose it, and the table of branches and the vectors are the base's.

## Types

**R09. The engine's types are its own and simple, built around the types of `learner-model`: the graph, the description of a task and the output of reading the engine takes from it as they are, and the candidate with its items' traps, the lesson, the lesson run, the attempt at a task with a rubric closed by "Done", the last attempt, the summary of issues and the choice it keeps as its own. The reason code is a type of its own, `ReasonCode`, with the contract's names and numbers, checked against it by a test. The result of an attempt on a skill comes as facts, and the engine computes "failure". In the input is only what the rule reads, and an input the rule could read only by guessing is an error.** The types with their fields and the rule at each field are in [architecture.md](architecture.md).
- **From `learner-model`, as they are** (sources 5.3): the module's graph `Graph`, the description of a task `Task` and the output of reading `Readout` for the time of the choice. The graph and the description of a task cover everything that the rules read about skills and tasks. The P of a candidate's items the engine takes from `Readout.Task`, and the marks of the trial series, `TrialOver` and `LastInTrial` of `ModuleReadout`, from `Read` (sources 5.5), as they are.
- **Its own types are built around the description of a task,** as `learner-model` proposed: the candidate — `Task` and the traps of each item by the `trap_values` of its checks, as full IDs; the lesson — the skills and the descriptions of the tasks of its segments; the lesson run — the lesson, its skills, the end reason — zero for an open one — and the time of the end; the attempt at a task with a rubric closed by "Done"; the profile's last closed attempt; the summary of issues; the skill from the map. The output is the kind of choice, the ID and the code.
- **The reason code** is a type of its own, `ReasonCode`, with the same names and numbers as `ReasonCode` in `cause.proto` of the contracts of `spec`, its nine values and the zero, and so are `ChoiceKind` and the run end reason `RunEnd` — by `RunEndReason`. A test on the standard library reads `cause.proto` in the submodule `spec/`, which stands at a release's tag, and checks the names and numbers, and `api` converts the values by number. The check is the job "Contracts" of `ci.yml`: a checkout with the submodule, the stage `toolchain`, the recipe `ci-contracts` with `ENGINE_CONTRACTS=1`, under which a missing file is a red test, not a skip. The shared `go.yml` does not take the submodule, and there, as for a consumer from the module cache, the test is skipped.
- **The result on a skill** is one type, `SkillResult`, for the last attempt and for the summary: how many items of the attempt where the skill is primary got a first verdict, for how many of them it is "correct", and whether the solution was shown. "Failure" and "correct" the engine computes with the same denominator as the model's: the answers, not all the items. The word "outcome" stays for the outcome of an attempt in the platform's vocabulary.
- **What the input lacks** — the seed, the age category, the daily goal and the interests: the format has no setting variants yet, and the field will come with them. Nor is there what the rules do not read: the places of finished segments and the close reasons of an attempt with a rubric other than "Done". The difficulty, the supporting skills and the slots of a task in `Task` stay: the rule does not read them, but by them the model computes P in `Readout.Task`.
- **A version of `learner-model` enters in two steps.** `release-verify` accepts the file of its release, so its tag is tied to a signed release of the shared `release.yml` from the tag's commit, and the Origin of the Go module in `proxy.golang.org` is the same commit (sources 5.7); from then on `go.sum` and sum.golang.org hold the module. This is how "accepted by `release-verify`" reads for a Go module: it is not a release file, but its tag is the tag of a signed release. Dependabot does not raise `learner-model` — `ignore` in the ecosystem `gomod` —: a new version comes with a task with the same check of the release and the gate of `sim`.
- **P is `Readout.Task` for each candidate of the pool of the engine's modules, once per call and before the rule.** A P of an item with a verdict outside [0; 1] is an error, whichever branch runs, and the rule reads ready P.
- **The Go names** are those of [architecture.md](architecture.md), and where it has none: `ItemTraps` — the traps of an item by place, `SkillIssue`, `ModuleChoices` and `TaskSolution` — the rows of the summary, `ReasonUnspecified` … `ReasonRotation`, `ChoiceUnspecified` … `ChoiceNothing` and `RunOpen` … `RunAbandoned`. There is no method `String()`: the rule does not need it, and the names of `cause.proto` are held by the test's table.
- **Errors** are as in `learner-model`: `ErrInvalid` and a wrapper with the detail; the wrapper carries the error of `Readout.Task` beside it, and `errors.Is` finds both the engine's `ErrInvalid` and the model's. An ID in the text of an error is written byte by byte, by a function of its own: the rules of code forbid `strconv.Quote` and its kin, which read the Unicode tables.
- **An input error**, beyond what [architecture.md](architecture.md) names at the fields:
  - a task of the pool, a lesson or a skill of a graph whose full ID does not start with the ID of its module: `Readout.Task` reads a candidate by the module that its ID names, and openness reads the skills of all modules together;
  - a run end outside `RunEnd` and a code of the summary outside `ReasonCode`: a value that the list does not name the rule would read silently, as one of those named or not at all;
  - an open lesson run with a time of the end: for an open one it is zero;
  - a skill, a module or a task twice in the summary, and a skill twice in the results of the last attempt: the rule would depend on which row it finds;
  - the traps of an item twice, and traps at a place where the task has no item: the rule gathers the traps by place, and an extra row would double or vanish silently;
  - a result where `FirstRight` is less than zero or greater than `Answered` — at the last attempt with `HasLast` and at a skill of the summary with its `HasLast`; without the flag the result carries no meaning and is not checked, like the code of the summary;

  The code `ReasonUnspecified` at a skill's last attempt is not an error: `cause.proto` marks with it also an attempt that the learner started anew.
- **The order of the checks** is one: an ID twice — modules, tasks of the pool, lessons; the own module, tasks before lessons; the skills of lessons; the traps of items; lesson runs; the summary; the results; the reading of the modules; the anchors; P. Each check goes by the full ID across all modules, over sorted copies, and names the smallest ID — `example/olympiad-2/…` is less than `example/olympiad/…`, since "-" comes before "/" —, and for lesson runs with an equal lesson, the smallest end and time of the end: a permutation of the lists changes neither the choice nor the error, and the test holds this on permutations by a seed.

— *Why:*
- **the types of `learner-model` as they are** — each library's own types of the graph were rejected for the platform: `api` and `sim` build the graph with one function, `NewGraph`, and the engine reads the same values;
- **a type of its own for the code** — the contracts module `github.com/altnovatrail/spec/contracts` pulls in `google.golang.org/protobuf` and `connect`, while `learner-model` keeps to the standard library alone for the sake of the build for the device: the engine, which is built with it, keeps to the same. The test keeps the types from diverging, and `api` converts the number without a table;
- **the traps in the engine** — `Task` lies in each piece of evidence of the log, and a field that the model does not read would grow in each. Traps by `trap_values` are the program's finding; the traps of the field `traps`, which the AI supposes, the rule does not read;
- **the result as facts** — the rule of "failure" is one, for the model and for the engine, and a mark from `api` would be its second copy;
- **only what is read** — a call holds as much of the learner's data as the rule reads: lesson runs without the places of segments, attempts with a rubric — only "Done";
- **two steps for `learner-model`** — by `go.sum` alone the tag would not be tied to a signed release; **no Dependabot** — its pull request would bypass the check of the release and the gate;

## The build for the device

**R10. The engine gives the same choice and the same code on any build of one version of its own and of `learner-model` — by the Go compiler, on any architecture, in WebAssembly too — and is built for the device as it is. This is held by the same discipline of code as in `learner-model`: the result of a multiplication or a division of floating-point numbers goes at once through an explicit `float64()` unless it is an operand of another multiplication, division or comparison, and the imports are only the standard library by a list and `learner-model`. CI checks it by the shared jobs of `.github` with the input `same-bits` — the runs on the targets, the differential check of the assembler, the job on arm64 and the rules of code. By Т-10, compute on the server, and how it works offline stage 5 decides.** How it is built is in [architecture.md](architecture.md).
- **The checks** are the shared `go.yml` with `same-bits` (sources 7.2, 7.5): "Go / targets", after a matrix of jobs "Go / target (<target>)", one a target — amd64 with GOAMD64 v3, v1 with `GODEBUG=cpu.all=off`, GOARCH=386, js/wasm and wasip1, the last run in `node:wasi` of the stage's Node (sources 7.4); "Go / fma-diff" — the assembler of each package with fusion and without it, six architectures at once, with the shared workflow's own control; "Go / arm64" — `ci-test` and the targets of its host on `ubuntu-24.04-arm`; "Go / rules" — the rules of code by `tools/coderules` of `.github`, with the lists `.github/coderules.json`, by the actual imports.
- **In the container** the recipes `test-targets`, `fma-diff` and `rules` of the `justfile` run the same as the shared jobs, and `same-bits` all three: they run the scripts of the jobs `target` and `fma-diff` of the shared `go.yml` of the commit of `.github` that `ci.yml` pins, with its control, and its `tools/coderules`, not copies of them. They take the commit from `ci.yml`, require one call of `go.yml` and keep the clone and the built tool in the user's cache by SHA, so the network is needed by a commit's first run. `test-targets` runs the targets of the matrix, read from the same `go.yml`, one after another, and on a host other than amd64 without `amd64-v3` and `386`, as the job `arm64` runs. CI does not call them.
- **The rules of code** are `learner-model`'s in what the engine needs: the engine needs no exp and log — P is given by `Readout.Task` — and it does not take `detmath`. The rounding of products and quotients and the list of the functions of `math` hold in the code and in the tests; iteration over a map, the functions of `strings` and `strconv` that read the Unicode tables and the operator `go` are banned in the code; the lists of imports hold everywhere. The engine's tests need no functions of `math` beyond the list. `encoding/binary` and `unicode/utf8` are in the list of code of `learner-model` but not in the engine's. `learner-model` is the only `require`, and only its root package, without `replace`: by it `learner-model` would come other than as the release that `release-verify` accepted gave it, and an import of its `detmath` or of another of its packages is a finding. The tests may also take `compress/gzip` and `encoding/json`, and the code takes neither. The one nested module is the lock file of gopls, `.devcontainer/gopls`. NaN and ∞ in the input are an error, returned as a value, not a choice.
- **The release's packages in `testdata/modules`** lie as they are: the index `module-index.json` of `modules` v0.1.4, the three packages 0.1.0 and `olympiad-math` 0.1.1, `sha256-<digest>.tar.gz`, without the bundles. They entered the repository once `just -f spec/justfile release-verify` accepted them with the bundles of their releases (sources 3.4, 3.5), and from then on the chain of digests holds them: the sha256 of the index is pinned in the test, the index names the package digest — the sha256 of its `inventory.json` — and `inventory.json` names the sha256 of `server.json`. The test checks each link of the chain and takes a package from the index by module and version. A package's archive is gzip of tar of blocks of 512 bytes, and the test reads it itself: the name, the size in octal and the type from the header.
- **The golden vectors** of the choice — the input and the choice with its code — are the same on all the targets, on the packages 0.1.0. **The benchmark of the choice** is on `olympiad-math` 0.1.1: its learner states are synthetic, made in the test's code by the helpers of the scenarios of the vectors and with a real `Read`, and which choice each gives a test holds. The test's conversion reads the field `template` of a task, as the conversion of `sim` does; the reader, the conversion and the input of a scenario are on `testing.TB`; the benchmark is `testing` and `b.Loop`, and the profile is by the flags of `go test`.
- **Т-10 — extended and open:** compute on the server; both libraries are built for the device with the same bits, and CI checks this; how it works offline — computation on the device or a queue that the server chose in advance — stage 5 decides.
- **The gate of `sim`** runs from the first tag of `sim`, and before it the first version is checked by its own tests.

— *Why:*
- **The engine computes with floating-point numbers** — the P of a task on a skill is the mean of the P of its items, and the recommended task is the one nearest by P to 0.775. The compiler fuses only a multiplication — with an addition or a subtraction — and a division by a constant with an exact reciprocal it does as a multiplication. The rule as written needs no such operation: the mean is a division by the number of items, a variable, and `s/float64(n) - 0.775` gave a separate division and subtraction on arm64, ppc64le and riscv64. But let a constant stand in the code: `s*0.5 - 0.775` gave `FNMSUBD` on arm64 and `FMSUB` on ppc64le, `(p1+p2)/2 - 0.775` also on s390x, riscv64 and loong64, and `float64((p1+p2)/2) - 0.775` separate operations on all five (sources 8.1). The specification allows this — "An implementation may combine multiple floating-point operations into a single fused operation, possibly across statements" — and an explicit conversion forbids it: "An explicit floating-point type conversion rounds to the precision of the target type, preventing fusion that would discard that rounding" (sources 8.1). A fused operation rounds once, and a P at the bound of the corridor or the equality of two distances may come out otherwise — and with it the choice. The rule of rounding holds this for any code that comes, and the rules of code and the differential check catch a violation before the merge. The order of addition too: the sum of the items' P is by the item's place, because floating-point addition is not associative.
- **The repeat of a choice** — a repeat by the record gives the same choice, and takes for it the same version of `engine` from the record. The same build it cannot take: `api` builds the engine into its own binary, and between deployments its Go version, architecture or GOAMD64 may change. Without the same bits a repeat would diverge from the record on another build, and the simulator would not see this before the deployment.
- **The same discipline as in `learner-model`** — its proposal to the engine, and both libraries are built together. The engine takes P from the model, which already gives the same bits; the engine's own are only means, differences and comparisons, so fewer rules are needed.
- **The shared jobs** — the same targets and architectures as a check of its own, with one copy for both libraries; **recipes that run the shared scripts** — a copy of a script diverged from the wrapper's and would have to be compared by hand at each move of `.github`, and without recipes the targets and the rules would be seen only in a pull request, while the run on 386 in the container catches what amd64 does not.
- **The packages as they are** — the chain from the test to the signature stays short; **the index v0.1.4** names the same packages 0.1.0 of the three modules, its entries byte for byte, and also `olympiad-math` 0.1.1. The cost is 2.4 MB in the zip of the module `engine`, which `api` and `sim` download: Go puts `testdata` there too.
- **Synthetic states for the benchmark** — a measurement before and after a fix repeats by itself; their representativeness was checked by a run of `sim` in a temporary folder.
- **No `archive/tar`** — it pulls in `os/user`, whose cgo the rules of code cannot check by the sources on 386.

## The speed of the choice

**R20. The choice computes what is costly once per call: `ready` builds the pool of tasks with their P and the index "skill → tasks of the pool", and the rule takes a skill's candidates from the index, not by a pass of the whole pool for each skill. It does not sort the list of candidates: it reads its length, the first by `byCorridor`, the first by `byEase` and whether there is a P not below 0.70, and both orders are total — their last key is the ID. The tasks of the pools are ordered by ID once per call — for three checks, the reading of P and the index. The choice, the codes and the errors are the same to the bit.**
- **The index** — by skill, sorted by the skill's ID, and a skill's tasks in it are in the order of their IDs: the order of the pool in the input changes neither the choice nor the lists inside. It is built from a task's primary skills — a superset of what `candidateOf` accepts: a skill primary in an item is among the task's primary skills. The search is over a sorted slice, without a map.
- **`poolTask`** holds a pointer to the candidate in the input and does not copy the items and their P when they are already in place: the rule only reads them.
- **The checks**: `checkIDs` finds a repeated task as a neighbour in the order of IDs — the smallest one is the same; `checkTraps` compares the places of traps pairwise, without copies and sorts, — the same smallest place, — and for a task with traps on more than 16 items sorts them as before, so that a page of many lines does not become quadratic. The order of the checks and the texts of the errors are as before.
- **Small things**: `pause` takes the task's description by pointer; `candidateOf` does not gather a task's traps when the skill has no frequent ones — there is nothing to compare them with; the primary skills of a "Done" task from the pool are taken from the pool; the rule reads P from the pool.

— *Why:* by the profile, the pass of the pool for each skill was 26% of the choice, `poolOf` 7%, the sorting of candidates 7%, the checks of the input 10% and garbage collection 23% (measurements 2.1). With the index and the pool once per call the choice is about 1.8 times faster and takes about half the memory, the engine's own share about a third of what it was, and the rest is `Readout.Task` of `learner-model` (measurements 2.2). The same to the bit — the golden vectors, the inputs of the benchmark, the choices of `sim`'s learners repeated on their recorded inputs and every number of the gate hold it.

— *Rejected:* P "on demand", for the candidates that a branch has reached — the error of a P outside [0; 1] would depend on the branch, and the gain is small: at the start all primary skills are open for 440–471 tasks of 772, and for "mastered" for all of them; the sorting of candidates — 7% of the choice with no use to the rule: the tests hold the order by sorting a copy; removing the list of candidates altogether — the tests would lose the check of the set; a map instead of the index — a search over a sorted slice gives the same without iterating over maps, which the rules of code keep out of the code.

— *Consequence:* `choose.go`, `skills.go`, `task.go`, `rule.go`, `open.go`, `lessons.go`; `skills_internal_test.go`, `choose_internal_test.go`, `golden_internal_test.go`, `property_internal_test.go`; [architecture.md](architecture.md); [measurements.md](measurements.md).
