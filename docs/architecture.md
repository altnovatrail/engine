## 1. Purpose and bounds

**Does:**
- chooses what comes next for the learner — a new skill, consolidation, a probe or repetition — and gives the choice a reason code (concepts 12 spec; architecture 3.8 spec). An engine choice in the contract is a lesson or a task outside a lesson with its own ID, and when there is nothing to give, "nothing to give", without a code: `ChoiceKind` knows no other kinds (concepts 15 spec; R99 spec). The engine does not choose an item within a task: so the author decided in T01 (R03), and the item left the engine's choices in concepts 12 spec (R104 spec);
- chooses by a rule, without AI: the same input gives the same output, and each choice has a cause (R03 spec; concepts 12 spec). Whether the engine needs randomness it decides itself, by its own rule (the 2026-10-20 amendment to R27 spec), and it decided that it does not: it has no generator and no seed of the choice (R07; subsection 3.5). Should a new version of the rule need randomness, it will have its own seed of the choice and its own names of places, and the generator is the platform's shared generator: PCG, as in Go's `math/rand/v2`, and the stream of a place derived as by the simulator of standalone ([format text](../spec/format/README.md), section 22; R59 spec);
- holds the rules of the choice (concepts 12 spec):
  - from the rule of standalone (SPEC 3 standalone): the success corridor — a task that the learner solves with a probability from 0.70 to 0.85, aiming at 0.775: the probability is given by `learner-model`, and the corridor is the engine's rule (R23 spec); consolidation after an error, while the skill is within reach; probes of a deferred skill; the queue — of the reachable unmastered skills that have already been issued, first the one not issued for the longest; a task on the learner's frequent traps; the setting to the learner's interests — format v0 has no such variants, they will come with a minor version (R51 spec);
  - new in NovaTrail: a skill is offered when its prerequisites are mastered, and a mastered skill comes back for repetition when its review date comes. The date is given by `learner-model`, and when to give a skill whose date has come, how many repetitions a day and how they share the queue is the engine's rule (concepts 12 spec; the 2026-11-02 amendment to R23 spec; R04 learner-model). Repetition is a task in the corridor on the module's turn of repetition, once in three of its choices, and when there is no new skill, no lesson of the author's order and no queue, outside the turn too (R06; subsection 3.4);
- chooses lessons: their order is set by the skill graph, and where the graph gives no order, the engine's rule chooses — by the lesson's ID. To a skill with a verdict a lesson is given on the new — when the skill is new or its recommended task has gone into a band whose lesson has not yet been passed — and a lesson's band is the bands of its tasks. The engine does not reorder a lesson's segments, adds no consolidation to a lesson and does not end a lesson early, and within a lesson it is not called: `api` calls it when a lesson run has ended and when the learner asks for something else (concepts 5 spec; R58 spec; architecture 3.8 spec; R05; subsection 3.3);
- does not block a lesson when a prerequisite is an anchor skill of the shared taxonomy that no module of the learner teaches: "unknown" does not hold a lesson back (concepts 4 spec; R50 spec). The engine has no separate short check — the check of an anchor is the answers on the very skill that needs it — and the module that teaches the anchor is offered by `api` by the module index (R63 spec; the 2026-11-11 amendment to R04; subsection 2.11). "Does not know" holds a skill back even when the skills that teach it have no evidence yet — except for a cycle of prerequisites between modules and an anchor whose teaching skills have neither tasks in the pool nor lessons (R04; subsection 3.2);
- for skills with a rubric and without assessment — `rubric` and `none` — does not adapt to the learner: the order is set by the author, by the skill graph and the order of a lesson's segments, and where the graph gives no order, the rule chooses without the learner model, by the lesson's ID, with the code `REASON_CODE_AUTHOR_ORDER` (concepts 4 spec; R49 spec; R58 spec; R05);
- when there is nothing to give, goes through the rule a second time — with the tasks whose solution was seen less than a day ago, except the last one, and on a skill from the map with it too, after all the others, — and only then returns "nothing to give" (the 2026-11-07 amendments to R03 and R04; subsection 3.3);
- sees a task template as one task, whichever instance comes up (R60 spec), and does not give again at once a task — not a template — whose solution has already gone to the learner's device (R82 spec);
- chooses in the mode it is given: it leads itself — "My subjects", where the connected modules go in turn, — or the learner chose a skill on the skill map, and the choice holds until the learner asks for something else; the third mode — a task from paper — is not in the first slice (concepts 12 spec; product 10 spec; О-11; R04; subsection 3.2);
- there are no constraints in the input: the modules, lessons and tasks are already selected by age category by `api` — the platform offers a module only to learners of its age categories, and a task may name the categories it suits (R46 spec; R51 spec), — and the engine does not read the category; there is no daily goal either in the profile or in the contract, and the rule does not read it. Both left the engine's constraints in concepts 12 spec (R04; R06; R107 spec).

**Does not:**
- does not estimate knowing skills: the estimate, its uncertainty, retention, "mastered" with the mastery band, the review date and knowing an anchor skill are computed by `learner-model`, and the engine reads them (concepts 11 spec; architecture 3.8 spec). The engine neither sets nor passes the threshold "mastered" (the 2026-11-02 amendment to R23 spec);
- does not call an AI model, and a model does not choose for it: R03 spec rejects this. In standalone the chat model could ask for another topic, naming a reason (SPEC 3.4 standalone; sources 1.1), — in NovaTrail it will not be so;
- does not give a verdict — that is `module-kit` (architecture 1 spec);
- does not write tasks: tasks come from the module — a published pool and templates with parameters, and `module-kit` builds a template's instance by the attempt's seed (R60 spec; concepts 9.3 spec);
- does not read module packages or the database: the skill graphs, the lessons, the pool of tasks, the lesson runs, the closed attempts at tasks with a rubric and the learner model are passed to it by `api`, and on the simulator by `sim` (architecture 3.8 spec; concepts 12 spec);
- keeps nothing: the record of the choice is kept by `api` (architecture 1 spec).

**Data and links:**
- it has no data of its own: pure computations without input and output. The time comes from outside, and the engine has no seed: the rule takes no randomness (architecture 3.8 spec; R07);
- at the input (concepts 12 spec; architecture 3.8 spec):
  - the learner model and the skill graphs of the connected modules. For the time of the choice the model gives the probability of a correct answer of an item with retention — for the corridor —, the estimate of a skill with uncertainty and retention, and for a mastered skill also "mastered" with the mastery band, the cautious probability with retention and the review date. For a skill — each trap the program found, with the number of pieces of evidence and the time of the last one, and which of them are frequent the engine chooses; for an anchor skill — "knows", "does not know" or "unknown", with a reason and the skills that teach it (R06 and R07 learner-model); for a module — whether the trial series has ended and whether the last evidence was still in it (R104 spec);
  - the lessons and the pool of tasks, which `api` sets. When the AI that the check needs is closed to the learner, the pool holds only tasks that are checked without AI, the lessons are only of such tasks, and the pool may be empty; the engine does not know the cause (concepts 12 spec; R11 api);
  - the profile's lesson runs — open and ended, with the end reason and the time of the end, without the places of ended segments, — and the attempts at tasks with a rubric closed by "Done": the task and the time (concepts 5 spec; concepts 12 spec; R13 api; R09; subsection 3.6);
  - the summary of issues from `api` — from the records of choices and attempts that it keeps anyway: for a skill, the time of the last issue, for a module, the number of issues, for a task, when its solution last went to the device (concepts 12 spec; R104 spec). With the additions of R107 spec: the issue of a lesson is the issue of each of its skills, for a module the number of choices, a lesson counted as one, for a skill the result of the last closed probe or repetition as facts, not as a mark "correct" (subsection 3.1);
  - the last closed attempt — its task, the result on the primary skills and the lesson of the run if the attempt is its segment, — the skill from the map as the mode, and the time of the choice (concepts 12 spec; R09; subsection 3.6). Interests, the age category, the daily goal and a seed are not in the input: the rule does not read them (R04; R06; R07; R09).

  The learner model, the attempts, the runs, the summary of issues and the skill from the map are the learner's data: on the server only the database of `api` keeps them (architecture 1 spec; privacy 4 spec);
- at the output — the engine choice and the reason code. The code is one value of `ReasonCode`, a closed list of nine values without parameters: a choice has one code — of the branch of the rule that made it ([contracts text](../spec/contracts/README.md), section 15; R100 spec). Only an adult sees the code — in progress, at the attempt, — and it does not go into the answer to the learner; the words for the adult are written by `app` (concepts 12 spec; R100 spec). A branch of the rule that is not in the list the engine asks for with a row «В реестр spec», and it comes in as a new value, and the meaning of the earlier ones does not change (the 2026-11-04 amendment to R29 spec). Frequent traps are not a branch but the choice of a task on a skill, and they need no code of their own (R03; subsection 3.1). There is no separate short check of an anchor skill (the 2026-11-11 amendment to R04; subsection 2.11), and it needs no code. Branch 8 of subsection 3.1 — all the skills are mastered —, the queue at "the bottom of the ladder", branch 9, and a skill "mastered" by the model that came back into the queue by branch 7 give the ninth value, `REASON_CODE_ROTATION`, "in rotation": R07 asked for it, and R107 spec recorded it (subsection 3.5);
- the record of the choice is kept by `api`: the versions of `engine` and `learner-model`, the package digests, the seed and the reason code, the release of `modules`, the number of the log's last evidence by which the choice was made, the time of the choice, the grade by which the model computed the starting point of modules without evidence, and whether the pool was limited to tasks without AI, and by R07 also the skill from the map, the age category and the mark of the engine's modules, with the seed field empty: so `spec` recorded it too (architecture 1 spec; R107 spec; subsection 3.5). The choice is repeated without AI — by the evidence log: the append-only log is the source of truth, and the state of the skills is a cache (architecture 1 spec; R80 spec);
- the types of the graph are simple and not from `module-kit`: so the libraries can be built for the device too. `learner-model` defines them, and the engine takes them from it (R28 spec): the graph is per module, with bands and skills, and the description of a task is one for evidence and for a candidate task from the pool (architecture 3.8 spec; R10 learner-model). Lessons and the pool are types of `engine`, and the types of the input about lessons and about attempts with a rubric are its own and simple, like the types of the graph (architecture 3.8 spec): a candidate is the description of a task and the traps of its items, and the reason code is a type of its own with the names and numbers of the contract (R09; subsection 3.6). The recommendation of Т-10 is to compute on the server and to keep a computation on the device possible, for offline; offline is stage 5 (product 11 spec). The engine gives the same choice on any build of one version, WebAssembly too, and CI checks this from the first code (R10; subsection 3.7);
- at build time it depends only on `learner-model` and the standard library (R09), and `sim` and `api` import it (repositories 2.1 spec; repositories 3 spec). The language is Go, as for all the platform's libraries (R11 spec). The code takes the files of `spec` from its releases (R64 spec): the test of the codes reads `cause.proto` in the submodule at the tag (subsection 3.6), and the Go module `spec` is not among the engine's dependencies. If it comes, its version and the submodule are moved together, by one move to a tag, and the module `spec` is excluded from Dependabot (R64 spec). A file of a NovaTrail release enters the code and CI only once `just -f spec/justfile release-verify` has accepted it (R77 spec);
- at stage 1 the engine runs only on the simulator: `sim` imports it and runs it on virtual learners, and reads the module packages itself — the server part, by the types of the canonical view from `spec`, without `module-kit` (architecture 3.9 spec; R62 spec). From stage 2 the engine is a library in `api`'s process: on the result of an attempt `api` gives the evidence to `learner-model`, then the engine chooses the next lesson or task (architecture 3.5 spec; architecture 4.1 spec);
- the first version of `engine` is the baseline before the first tag of `sim`: its own tests check it, and it comes out signed; each change after it passes the simulator `sim` by a criterion written down before the candidate's first run (architecture 3.8 spec; R163 standalone). The gate is a job of the CI of `engine`: it takes `sim` by the tag and the SHA of its commit and writes `go.work` on the fly, without committing it, — `sim`, the code of the pull request and `learner-model` at the commit from the gate file of `sim`, — and a change that worsens the criterion does not merge (R103 spec; repositories 4 spec). The criterion itself and the tolerance bands are an item of the card of `sim` (architecture 3.9 spec), and the success criterion of the first slice is that the engine on the simulator behaves sensibly on all three graphs: it holds the success corridor where there is a verdict, goes in the author's order where there is none, does not loop and does not declare "mastered" too early (product 10 spec). The first slice is three modules of two subjects: olympiad maths 1–6, school maths of grade 3 on a workbook, and short essays in English (R41 spec);
- stage 1, together with `learner-model` and `sim`: the simulator needs both the learner model and the choice rule (repositories 6 spec; product 11 spec). The starting point is the rule by which standalone chose a task, SPEC 3 (repositories 7 spec).

### 2.9. The base's corridor

The analysis of T29, before the first candidate of E3: which branches and codes give items outside the corridor 0.70–0.85, and why. This is a **rough look**: it decides nothing and does not touch the criterion — the judgement of `sim` decides (section 3.6 sim). The hypotheses below are the basis of the variants of T31, not a decision; the judgement of the variants is at the end of the subsection: the first round ended in an exit.

**The measurement.** The run of T29 — how it was counted, the tables by kind, code and branch of the choice, and the cells whole — is measurements 1; the numbers below are from there.

**Where the gap is.** On olympiad maths the base has 27–28% of items in the corridor, and the "pool ceiling" of `sim` — the best share in the corridor for one task that the rule could give — is, on average over the choices, 0.995–1.000. On school maths 13–19% are in the corridor, but the pool ceiling too is only 0.46–0.78. The paths of the judgement by `E1.corridor` — the share outside the corridor, less is better, the base minus the ceiling — are 0.71–0.72 on olympiad maths and 0.08–0.17 on school maths (the 2026-11-09 amendment to R04 sim, the "new pinning"). The numbers moved from the earlier pinning, by which the description of E3 was written (10–19% in the corridor for the base, 25–33% for the ceiling), but the gap is still on olympiad maths, and it is the rule's.

**Hypotheses and levers.**
- **H1. A task with a seen solution goes after all the unseen ones — and the corridor loses.** In branches 3 and 7 of olympiad maths the skill almost always has a candidate in the corridor (94–97%), but an unseen one only in 37–48% of choices, and the issued task is in the corridor by the rule's P in almost as many (38–51%; the 1–3 points more are seen tasks that got into the corridor by how long ago they were shown). The solution goes to the device after a correct answer too (R82 spec), so the tasks in the corridor that the learner has solved become "seen", and the hard ones stay unseen. `bySolution` puts the unseen ones first, and the corridor is compared only within one group. When there are no unseen ones at all (13–21% of the choices of branches 3 and 7), the seen ones go by how long ago they were shown, without the corridor. Where a skill has many unseen ones in the corridor — branch 7 for a mastered skill, `ROTATION`, — the rule's P is in the corridor in 81–83% of choices. **Lever** — the order of `byCorridor` and `bySolution` (`task.go`; R03): a seen task after a pause of 24 hours is a candidate on a par with the unseen ones by the corridor, or at least a seen one in the corridor comes before an unseen one outside it. R82 spec says only not to give it as the next task, and after how long is decided by `engine` (R107 spec), so this is a change of R03, not of `spec`. **Risk:** a task with a seen solution does not let "mastered" be set (R06 learner-model) — `E4` may move; and a repeat of the seen is the simulator's measure `M8`. A rough upper bound, not a forecast: if branches 3 and 7 on `G0` had as many in the corridor by P* as `ROTATION` of branch 7 (49%), the cell's corridor would rise from 27% to about 39%, and the share below 0.50 would fall from 34% to about 17%.
- **H2. Consolidation after every error keeps the learner on a hard skill.** Branch 3 is 31–40% of the choices of olympiad maths, and it takes the task by the same order as in H1, on the skill where the learner has just made an error. A loop comes out: a hard task — an error — consolidation — a hard one again. **Lever** — the condition of branch 3 (`rule.go`; R03 and R04): consolidate only when the skill has a candidate in the corridor, and otherwise go by the turn of modules; or a target of consolidation easier than the target of the corridor. H1 removes most of the cause, and H2 is worth trying together with it or after it.
- **H3. A lesson on a new skill.** The lessons of branch 6 are 8% of the choices of olympiad maths, but 18–20% of items, and of them 14% are in the corridor by P*; the lessons of branch 7 are another 10–13% of items, 22–31% in the corridor. The tasks of a lesson go in the author's order, not by the corridor (R05). The engine's lever here is narrow — whether to give a lesson, — and the corridor within a lesson is Д4 and outside E3.
- **H4. The model.** Even where the rule's P is in the corridor, P* is in the corridor in 33–68% (`ROTATION` of branch 7 and repetition): a band of 0.15 is narrower than the spread of the estimate. On `G2` of olympiad maths the model's mean P is below the mean P* by 0.08–0.09 for branch 7, and on `G2` of school maths by 0.16 for the queue, and there 54% of its items are above 0.95. - A module is offered to the learner by `api`: «Ученику модуль предлагает `api` — только из тех, что держит сам и что ученику подходят» (R63 spec), — by `teaches` of the index's entries ([format text](../spec/format/README.md), section 26). The engine does not read the index (architecture 3.8 spec), and `ChoiceKind` knows a lesson, a task and "nothing to give" (R99 spec). concepts 4 spec says otherwise — «движок может предложить короткую проверку или модуль», — this is row В35.
- An adult already sees an anchor "unknown": the skill map carries `AnchorSkillState` — `ANCHOR_SKILL_HOLDING_UNKNOWN` with the reason `ANCHOR_SKILL_REASON_NOBODY_TEACHES` or `ANCHOR_SKILL_REASON_ONLY_UNASSESSABLE` and the skills that teach it (`progress.proto`; R99 spec). By which module to learn it the contract does not say: neither `AnchorSkillState` nor `CatalogModule` (`module.proto`) has such a field — row В36.
- There is no value of `ReasonCode` for a check of an anchor: R100 spec did not add one: concepts 12 spec names no such branch of the rule, and «движок может их не ввести».
- The simulator does not see such a learner. In `sim` v0.1.6 the truth knows an anchor that nobody teaches: "an anchor is held when a skill of the learner's modules that teaches it is truly mastered with its R* then, or when none teaches it" (`internal/run/pool.go`). Knowledge of an anchor outside the platform enters the truth only as a shift of the teaching skills of school maths — `TeacherBoost` of the population `A-unpractised`, — so for a learner of olympiad maths alone the P* of the tasks of `fractions-parts` does not depend on the anchor. `M9` reads only "knows" and "does not know" (`internal/measure/model.go`), and the populations `A-` go on both maths. This is row В37.

**What a short check is.** Options:
- (a) there is no separate one: the check is the first answers on the skill that needs the anchor;
- (b) a check task before the lesson: to a skill with an anchor "unknown" without evidence of its own, first its easiest task, and the lesson after, with a new value of `ReasonCode`;
- (c) a new kind of task in the format, and evidence for the anchor in `learner-model`;
- (d) a question to an adult whether the learner knows the anchor, and the answer in the engine's input.

The recommendation and the author's decision — (a). (b) gives the same tasks of the skill that the base gives after the lesson: it differs from the base only by the order "lesson — task", and a lesson on a new skill is R05. (c) is the work of the format, `module-kit` and `learner-model`, not of the engine, and until it is done the engine has nothing to read. (d) is `app`, the contract and the learner's data, which are read against privacy spec, and an adult's words are not evidence: a program gives the verdict (R03 spec).

**Who proposes a module.** Options: (a) `api` by the index, as R63 spec says, and `app` shows the proposal; (b) a field of `EngineChoice` "anchor without a teacher", by which `api` looks for a module; (c) a new `ChoiceKind` "module". The recommendation and the author's decision — (a). (b) repeats what `api` knows anyway from `Read` and gives with the skill map. (c) requires the index in the engine's input, against architecture 3.8 spec, and a module is neither a lesson nor a task.

**When and what holds the skill after an answer.** The rule is as in the base. Here is what it does with a learner of olympiad maths alone who does not know the anchor:
1. The anchor "unknown" does not hold: `fractions-parts` is open when `arithmetic-tricks` is mastered, and comes by branch 6 — a lesson on a new skill, `REASON_CODE_NEW_SKILL` (subsections 3.2 and 3.3; example 5 of subsection 3.2).
2. The tasks of the skill are the check. An error after the module's trial series is branch 3, consolidation, `REASON_CODE_CONSOLIDATION`, while the skill is reachable.
3. Failures drop P: when the easiest task has P < 0.70, the skill is on pause, and the module's turn goes to other skills. On the turn of a probe, once in six choices of the module, branch 4 gives it by its easiest task, `REASON_CODE_PROBE`, if of the skills on pause it is the one not issued for the longest; a correct answer returns it within reach (subsection 3.1).
4. `percent-basic` and `ratio-sharing` without evidence of their own wait while `fractions-parts` is not mastered (subsection 3.1, "Open"), and the other skills of the module go their own way.

The engine cannot do more here: to hold a skill back for an anchor that no task and no lesson in the input teaches is to close it forever, and such an anchor does not hold even at "does not know" (subsection 3.2). The price is probes on one skill, and `percent-basic` and `ratio-sharing` without evidence of their own wait for its "mastered" and, if the learner never learns the anchor, will never open. There is no dead end between modules or within the module's turn: the module goes by other skills, and the probe holds "does not loop" (subsection 3.1). A better outcome is given only by a module that teaches the anchor, and `api` proposes it.

**What the engine needs from `Read`.** There is no new input. Of an anchor the rule reads only `Status`, and it takes the teaching skills from `Teaches` of the graphs of the engine's modules (subsection 3.6). It needs neither `Reason` nor `Teachers`: with both reasons for "unknown" there is equally nothing to teach with, and which module to propose `api` decides.

В35 — concepts 4 spec: «„Неизвестно“ урока не блокирует, и отдельной проверки навыка-опоры нет: опору проверяют ответы ученика на самом навыке, которому она нужна»; `api` proposes a module and gives it on the skill map (R99 spec). **The variant's rule** — a proposal for T52, in the code of the branch of E7 since T52 with the author's decisions of 2026-11-13 on the map and on reach (the 2026-11-14 amendment to R05); whether it enters the rule, the judgement decides.
- **Branch 0, "consolidation in a lesson",** — before branch 1. It fires when all of this holds at once:
  - the last closed attempt is a segment of a lesson, not "left unfinished", and the run of this lesson is open, and in the mode of the map the lesson teaches the skill from the map, as branch 1 reads a run there;

Branch 0 has no second pass with the tasks on pause: a task whose key the learner saw minutes ago is weak consolidation (R05). The code is `REASON_CODE_CONSOLIDATION`, and in the mode of the map `REASON_CODE_SKILL_CHOSEN`: so the contract says for a choice on a skill from the map.

### 3.1. The core of the rule: a task outside a lesson in one module

Decided by R03. Here is a task outside a lesson in one module, for skills with a verdict. Several modules, a skill from the map and anchor skills fit into this rule by R04 — subsection 3.2, — lessons and the author's order by R05, subsection 3.3, — repetition by R06, subsection 3.4. The numbers are as in standalone: the corridor 0.70–0.85 aiming at 0.775 and a probe once in six choices of the module (SPEC 2.3, 3.2 standalone; sources 1.1, 1.2 and 1.3). These are the engine's constants, and only a run of the simulator `sim` changes them (R23 spec; architecture 3.8 spec).

**What the rule reads.**
- From `Read` of `learner-model`, for the time of the choice: for a skill, the number of pieces of evidence, the time of the last practice, "mastered" with the mastery band, and the traps the program found, with the number of pieces of evidence and the time of the last one; from `Readout.Task`, the P of each item of a candidate task, with retention (architecture 3.8 spec; R08 and R10 learner-model).
- The module's skill graph: the skills, the tier, the prerequisites, the assessability and `teaches` (R10 learner-model). On several modules, the graphs of all the engine's modules, and for an anchor skill its state from `Read`: "knows", "does not know" or "unknown", with a reason (R07 learner-model; subsection 3.2). The order of the skills in the graph carries no meaning ([format text](../spec/format/README.md), section 12), so the **order of the graph** here is by tier, and within a tier by the local ID, byte by byte.
- The module's pool of tasks from `api` (concepts 12 spec).
- The summary of issues from `api` — the input that R104 spec recorded, not a log: a fold of the records of choices and attempts that `api` keeps anyway (architecture 1 spec; concepts 12 spec). For a skill, when a task on it or a lesson that teaches it was last issued (R05), and, if its last closed attempt was a probe or a repetition — a choice with `REASON_CODE_PROBE` or `REASON_CODE_REPETITION`, — its result on the skill as facts: the items where the skill is primary and there is a first verdict, how many of them are "correct" and whether the solution was shown, while whether the attempt is correct the engine computes by the rule "Failure" below (R06; R09); for a module, the counter n of its choices, a task and a lesson one each (the 2026-11-07 amendment to R04); for a task, when its solution last went to the device. R104 spec recorded the summary before these additions, and R107 spec with them: the issue of a lesson, the counter of choices, repetition and the result as facts.
- Whether the module's trial series has ended and whether the module's last evidence was still in it: the first five answers in the module live in `learner-model` (R03 learner-model), and `Read` gives these two marks for a module (R104 spec; architecture 3.8 spec).
- The time of the choice (concepts 12 spec). The rule reads no randomness: the engine does without it (R07; subsection 3.5).

**The states of a skill.**
- **Open** — each prerequisite of the module is "mastered" by the model, on any band, or there are no prerequisites. The engine has no second threshold (the 2026-11-02 amendment to R23 spec). A prerequisite with `rubric` or `none` will never get "mastered", and it is credited by the author's order — subsection 3.3; a prerequisite that is an anchor skill — subsection 3.2. A skill that has evidence of its own is open whatever becomes of its prerequisites, and a prerequisite that has nothing to teach with in the input does not hold (the 2026-11-07 amendments to R03; subsection 3.3).
- **The candidates of a skill** — the tasks of the pool where it is primary — for the task or for an item whose skills replace the task's skills (concepts 6 spec), — without those that are not candidates because of a seen solution (below), and without the tasks for which another primary skill is closed: otherwise the evidence would open a closed skill past its prerequisites (the 2026-11-07 amendment to R03). This restriction does not hinder a skill from the map: its candidates are its tasks (subsection 3.2). A template is one task, whichever instance comes up (R60 spec).
- **The P of a task on a skill** — the mean P of its items with a verdict for which this skill is primary: the share of items that the learner will solve correctly. An item without a verdict does not enter the mean, and a task without an item with a verdict on the skill is not its candidate: it will give no evidence on it (the 2026-11-08 amendment to R03). The primary skill is as in `learner-model`: an item's own, and the task's skill for an item without its own.
- **Failure** — a closed attempt where the skill has fewer than half correct first verdicts, as in the model (R06 learner-model). An attempt with the solution shown and without correct first verdicts is a failure too. **Correct** — an attempt with a first verdict on the skill that is not a failure. An attempt without first verdicts on the skill and without the solution shown is neither a failure nor "correct": it leads to no consolidation and does not return the skill from a pause (the 2026-11-08 amendment to R03).
- **Reachable** — open, and the easiest candidate — the one with the highest P — has P ≥ 0.70. A skill whose last closed attempt — a probe or a repetition (R06) — is correct is reachable too: so a correct answer of a probe or of a repetition returns the skill from a pause — a rule of `engine`, not a rise of the model (R06 learner-model), — until the next closed attempt. A skill without candidates is not reachable.
- **On pause** — open, with evidence, not reachable, — including when its P fell from forgetting or the pool narrowed. **Not reached yet** — open, without evidence and not reachable: it will come when the learner grows.
- **Mastered for the queue** — the model's "mastered", as long as the skill's recommended task (below) is not above its mastery band. If it has gone above, the skill is in the rotation again, as in SPEC 2.5 standalone, and the model's "mastered" stays as it is (concepts 12 spec).

**The task on the skill.** When a skill is chosen, the rule chooses a task among its candidates:
- a task — not a template — whose solution went to the device less than 24 hours ago is not a candidate. Later it is a candidate, but after all the candidates without a seen solution, and among such the first is the one whose solution went longest ago (R82 spec). 24 hours is a constant of the engine. In the second pass — when the choice gave nothing — such tasks are candidates again, after the others, except the task of the last closed attempt (the 2026-11-07 amendment to R03; subsection 3.3). The task of the last closed attempt whose solution went to the device is not a candidate of the next choice, however long ago the solution went: R82 spec does not give it as the next task (R107 spec; the second 2026-11-10 amendment to R03);
- if there are candidates with P in the corridor, from 0.70 to 0.85, — among them the one that catches more of the learner's frequent traps on this skill, then the nearest by P to 0.775, and of two equally near the easier one, with the greater P, as the point of standalone (SPEC 2.3 standalone). So frequent traps choose the task without taking it out of the corridor;
- if there is none in the corridor — the nearest by P to 0.775, of two equally near the easier one. For a probe — the easiest;
- a frequent trap is one that at least two pieces of evidence of the skill found, in order of their number and then of the time of the last one. Of the tasks that catch equally many frequent traps, the first is the one whose caught trap is higher in this order, and with an equal number and time — by the trap's ID: the places of the caught traps are compared in ascending order. The traps of a task are the traps of its items where the skill is primary (the 2026-11-08 amendment to R03). This is the threshold "repeating" of an adult's progress — two closed attempts of a profile (R99 spec) —, but on the skill. A trap that AI supposed the rule does not read: it does not go into the model (concepts 9.4 spec). Which traps a task expects is carried by the engine's candidate — the traps of the items by `trap_values` (R09; subsection 3.6);
- then — the order of the task's ID, byte by byte.

The rule does not read the learner's interests yet: format v0 has no variants of setting (R51 spec). When they come, their place is the setting of the task, not a branch, as in SPEC 3.2 standalone.

The engine does not choose an item within a task: the choice is a lesson, a task or "nothing to give", as `ChoiceKind` (R99 spec). So `spec` recorded it too (R104 spec).

4. After the series the learner made an error in `gaps-34-d3-1`. Branch 3: `counting-gaps` is reachable, `REASON_CODE_CONSOLIDATION`; the corridor has already moved toward the easy tasks, and the recommended one is `gaps-34-d1-1`.
5. Two more errors, and the easiest task of `counting-gaps` has P < 0.70: the skill is on pause. At n = 11 — the turn of the probe, branch 4: `gaps-12-d2-1`, the easiest, `REASON_CODE_PROBE`. The answer is correct — the skill is reachable again.
6. `combinatorics-enumeration` has "mastered" on `grades-3-4`. `pigeonhole-basic` opens, branch 6: `REASON_CODE_NEW_SKILL`. When the recommended task of `combinatorics-enumeration` goes to `grades-5-6`, the skill will come back into the queue.

**Example: school maths of grade 3.** The graph (the same commit): `times-tables` is tier 1; `division-with-remainder` and `fractions-of-a-whole` are tier 2, both after `times-tables`; `comparing-fractions` is tier 3, after `fractions-of-a-whole`. There is one band, `grade-3`, with tasks of difficulty 1, 3 and 5 per skill.
1. Only `times-tables` is open: branch 6 — the lesson `times-tables` (subsection 3.3) —, then branches 7 and 3, while it has no "mastered". If even `times-tables-d1` is too hard, there are no reachable skills, and branch 9 gives the same skill, by the easiest task.
2. The solution of `times-tables-d3` is shown: for 24 hours it is not a candidate, and then it goes after `times-tables-d1` and `times-tables-d5`.
3. "Mastered" on `times-tables` — both skills of tier 2 are open: branch 6 gives `division-with-remainder`, then `fractions-of-a-whole` — the order of IDs.
4. "Mastered" on `fractions-of-a-whole` opens `comparing-fractions`. When all four are mastered for the queue, the skills due for review go as repetition outside the turn, and the rest by branch 8, `REASON_CODE_ROTATION` (subsections 3.4 and 3.5).

**What the simulator will check** (product 10 spec; architecture 3.9 spec) — a proposal for the criterion of `sim`, and the criterion itself is an item of the card of `sim`:
- the share of issued tasks with P from 0.70 to 0.85 on skills with a verdict — "holds the corridor";
- each reachable skill is issued no less often than once in so many choices, and a skill on pause comes back by a probe — "does not loop";
- a skill without evidence of its own does not open before "mastered" of its prerequisites (the 2026-11-07 amendment to R03) — "mastered" too early is declared by the model, and the engine does not build on it;
- the same input gives the same choice and the same code, and each choice of a task or a lesson has a code (concepts 12 spec) — subsection 3.5.

### 3.2. Several modules, a skill from the map and anchor skills

Decided by R04. The rule of subsection 3.1 works within a module; here — how it leads through all the engine's modules — connected and suitable for the learner (R07 api) —, how a skill chosen on the map holds, and what an anchor skill does to the state "Open". Lessons and the author's order — subsection 3.3, repetition — subsection 3.4.

**"My subjects".** `ChooseNext` carries no module: the engine leads through all the modules at once ([contracts text](../spec/contracts/README.md), section 15). The first branch that fits makes the choice:
1. Consolidation in a lesson and the lesson of an open run — branches 0 and 1 of subsection 3.1, by subsection 3.3.
2. Consolidation — branch 3 of subsection 3.1 by the profile's last closed attempt, in its module. On the module's turn below, branch 3 does not repeat, even if the last closed attempt of this module is a failure: it has already been consolidated, or other modules went after it. An attempt that the learner left by asking for something else — the outcome "left unfinished" (concepts 11 spec), the close "left" ([contracts text](../spec/contracts/README.md), section 15) — leads to no consolidation in any mode: otherwise asking for something else would bring back the same skill.
3. The module's turn. The time of issue of a module is the latest time of the last issue among its skills from the summary of issues (R104 spec; R107 spec); the issue of a lesson is the issue of each of its skills (R05). The turn goes to the module where it is longest ago, and a module without issues goes first; on a tie — the order of the module's full ID, byte by byte. Within the module branches 4–9 of subsection 3.1 work. The trial series, the counter of the turn of the probe and "the bottom of the ladder" are each module's own, and the rule does not compare the levels of modules: the modules have scales of their own (concepts 11 spec; R03 learner-model). The modules' turns are equal, not their shares of issues: consolidation adds tasks to the module where the learner makes errors, and a module without reachable skills takes its turn by branch 9, "the bottom of the ladder".
4. A module whose branches gave nothing passes the turn to the next one in the same order; if none gave anything — the second pass with the tasks from the pause, and then "nothing to give" (subsection 3.3).

The code of a choice is the code of the branch that made it. For `REASON_CODE_QUEUE` on the module's turn, "not issued for the longest" is within its own module; the meaning of the code is the same, and so `spec` made its comment more precise too (R07; R107 spec).

**A skill from the map** — the mode "skill chosen on the map", branch 2 of subsection 3.1; a task and a lesson on the skill always have the code `REASON_CODE_SKILL_CHOSEN`, and the lesson of an open run `REASON_CODE_OPEN_RUN` (R100 spec). The mode replaces items 2–4 above:
- which skill can be chosen the map decides: only one not closed by its rule "open", and the engine takes this rule as an input (R104 spec; R03 skill-map). Its own reachability of "My subjects" does not get in the way: it gives the skill from the map whatever its state in subsection 3.1;
- the task on the chosen skill — by the rule "The task on the skill" of subsection 3.1: in the corridor, and without candidates in the corridor the nearest by P to 0.775, with the pause before a task with a seen solution. The skill is given when closed, on pause and in the module's trial series too: the learner's choice is stronger, as a topic chosen by a person in SPEC 3.6 standalone, and the model's series computes the module's level by the answers on any skill (R03 learner-model);
- a lesson on the skill and a skill with `rubric` or `none` — subsection 3.3; if the skill has neither a lesson nor candidates — the second pass, and on a skill from the map it takes the task of the last closed attempt too, after all the others (the 2026-11-07 amendment to R04): R104 spec requires giving a lesson or a task on a skill from the map while the pool and the lessons hold at least one, and such a choice is empty only when `api` limited the pool to tasks without AI. To a skill with `verdict` without a lesson on the skill and without tasks the rule gives a lesson of the author's order too, and on the second pass a passed lesson too (subsection 3.3; the 2026-11-09 amendment to R04); if the skill is not in the engine's modules — "nothing to give";
- the lesson of an open run in the mode of the map — only a lesson that teaches the chosen skill, and the other open runs wait; so is consolidation in a lesson (subsection 3.3);
- the choice holds until the learner asks for something else: `ChooseNext` without a skill brings back "My subjects". For this the record of the choice keeps the chosen skill — the full ID of the skill from the map, which is not among its fields (architecture 1 spec), and it cannot be restored from the task: a primary skill may be an item's alone (concepts 6 spec). `api` calls the engine for `next` and at the end of a run with the skill from the last record of the choice, and the new record carries it on, the lesson of an open run too, — whatever code the choice came out with. The repeat of a choice needs the same skill too: the mode is an input of the rule (concepts 12 spec; subsection 2.3). So `spec` recorded it too (R107 spec).

**An anchor skill and "Open".** A prerequisite `core.<name>` holds a skill back when `Read` gives "does not know" — with the reason "not mastered" or "no evidence". "Knows" and "unknown" do not hold it back (R07 learner-model; concepts 4 spec). "No evidence" holds it back because the module that teaches the anchor is connected, and the module's turn will lead to it by itself. There is nowhere to lead when no skill with `verdict` that teaches the anchor in the engine's modules has either a task in the pool — on pause or not: the pause will end — where every other primary skill is open, or a lesson — for example, with AI closed the pool narrowed to tasks without it (concepts 12 spec): then "does not know", with any reason, does not hold the skill back, as "unknown" does not, — otherwise it would wait for tasks that the rule will not give. A teaching skill that is closed or out of reach holds it back: it will come when the learner grows in its module. "Every other primary skill is open" is read here by "open" without this exception, in one step: so the count is finite, and it errs toward "does not hold back" (the 2026-11-08 amendment to R04). The teaching skills are the skills of the engine's modules whose `teaches` names the anchor, with `verdict` and a band taught, and such a skill is taught by a task where it has an item with a verdict, or by a lesson whose skills name it. There is no separate short check at "unknown": a task will not check the anchor — an anchor is only ever its supporting skill —, and the check is the answers on the skill itself. The check is a rule of the engine (R03 learner-model), and the rule decided that there is no separate one. A module cannot be an engine choice — `ChoiceKind` knows a lesson, a task and "nothing to give" (R99 spec) —, and `api` proposes it by the module index, by the field `teaching_modules` of the skill map, which the engine does not read (R63 spec; the 2026-11-11 amendment to R99 spec; the 2026-11-11 amendment to R04; subsection 2.11).
- **A cycle between modules.** The graph G is the skills of the engine's modules; its edges go from a skill to each of its prerequisites of the module and from a skill to each skill of the engine's modules whose `teaches` names its anchor prerequisite. If a skill that teaches `core.X` lies in one strongly connected component of G with the skill s, then `core.X` does not hold the skill s back. Within a module there are no cycles — the format rule `skill-graph.acyclic` —, and `skill-graph.own-anchors` does not let a skill rest on an anchor of its own module (R50 spec): such a component always goes through several modules. In the first slice there are no cycles.
- **Knowing goes away** — two failures in a row took "mastered" off the skill that teaches (R06 learner-model), or the module that teaches no longer gives its graph (R07 learner-model). A skill with evidence of its own — where it is primary — stays open, and a skill without it waits again (the 2026-11-07 amendment to R03). So practice on a skill by a choice from the map opens it too: the learner chose it themself, and "My subjects" will not give a skill out of reach anyway — only by a probe (subsection 3.1).

**Constraints.** The engine does not read the age category: `api` already selects the modules, lessons and tasks by it (R07 api; architecture 3.7 api), and `spec` removed it from the engine's constraints (R107 spec). There is no daily goal either in the profile or in the contract ([contracts text](../spec/contracts/README.md), section 15), and the rule does not read it (R06), and it left the engine's constraints too (R107 spec). A task from paper is stage 5 (О-11): there is no branch for it.

**Example: both maths.** In the first batch of `modules` olympiad maths rests on no anchor skills (sources 3.2), so the example takes its graph from the [format examples](../spec/format/examples/skill-graph/valid/olympiad-math.yaml) — the 17 skills of standalone, where `fractions-parts` needs `arithmetic-tricks` and `core.fractions-of-a-whole` —, and school maths of grade 3 from `modules`, where `fractions-of-a-whole` teaches `core.fractions-of-a-whole`. A learner of grade 3 who has both.
1. The modules alternate: the school one goes first, without issues — `novatrail/grade-3-math` comes before `novatrail/olympiad-math` in the order of IDs —, and the first choice in it is the lesson `times-tables` (subsection 3.3); after the module's trial series an error in its task keeps the next choice in it, by consolidation, and in the series it does not (subsection 3.1).
2. `fractions-parts` waits: the anchor is "does not know" — first "no evidence", then "not mastered", until `fractions-of-a-whole` is mastered.
3. "Mastered" on `fractions-of-a-whole` gives "knows": `fractions-parts` is open and on the turn of olympiad maths comes by branch 6, `REASON_CODE_NEW_SKILL`.
4. Two failures in a row take "mastered" off `fractions-of-a-whole`: the anchor is "does not know" again. The begun `fractions-parts` is open, and `ratio-sharing` — after `fractions-parts` — is open if it has evidence of its own, and waits if it has none and `fractions-parts` is not mastered.
5. The learner has only olympiad maths: no module of the engine teaches the anchor, it is "unknown", and `fractions-parts` is open when `arithmetic-tricks` is mastered.
6. The learner chose `pigeonhole-basic` on the map, still closed: first goes the lesson of `pigeonhole-basic` of the band of its recommended task — in the first batch of `modules` a skill has a lesson for each band (subsection 3.3) —, and then the tasks on it, the nearest to the corridor, all with `REASON_CODE_SKILL_CHOSEN` — after an error and after a correct answer alike —, until the learner asks for something else. If the learner left a task begun wrongly, the next choice is by "My subjects" already, without consolidation, and `pigeonhole-basic` with evidence of its own is open: "My subjects" will give it when it is reachable.
7. A cycle — a synthetic one: the skill a of the module A rests on `core.y` and teaches `core.x`, the skill b of the module B rests on `core.x` and teaches `core.y`. a and b are one component of G, and neither anchor holds them back.

**What the simulator will check** — in addition to the list of subsection 3.1:
- the turn comes to each module in turn, and a module's share of issues beyond its turns comes from consolidation and branch 9;
- a skill without evidence of its own does not open while its anchor is "does not know", except for a cycle and an anchor whose teaching skills have no candidates, — with the measure of a false "knows" from architecture 3.9 spec. For a skill without evidence of its own the same amendment also makes the criterion of subsection 3.1 about the module's prerequisites more precise;
- there is no dead end between modules: each open skill with candidates is issued sooner or later;
- a choice from the map holds until `ChooseNext` without a skill.

### 3.3. Lessons, the author's order and "nothing to give"

Decided by R05, with the 2026-11-07 amendments to R03 and R04. Here — when the choice of subsections 3.1 and 3.2 gives a lesson, how the skills with `rubric` and `none` go, and what the engine gives when there is nothing to give. Repetition — subsection 3.4.

**What the rule reads about lessons.** The lessons of the engine's modules — the lesson's skills and its segments, and for a task segment the description of the task with its band (concepts 12 spec; R10 learner-model); `api` gives only the lessons that suit the learner whole (architecture 3.7 api). The profile's runs — open and ended, with the end reason and the time of the end (concepts 5 spec; R13 api): the rule does not read the places of ended segments — the segments go in the author's order, and `api` leads the lesson on. The closed attempts at tasks with a rubric — of them the rule reads only those closed by "Done", with the task and the time (architecture 3.8 spec; subsection 3.6). The last closed attempt comes with the lesson of its run, if it is a segment of a lesson (subsection 3.6).

**A lesson is passed** when it has a run that ended "passed", on any package. "Unchecked", "not suitable" and "abandoned" do not pass the lesson: it can be given again, by a new run.

**The order of lessons** is by the graph, as the [format text](../spec/format/README.md), section 21, defines it: lesson A goes after lesson B when a skill of A, through the module's prerequisites, transitively, requires a skill of B that A does not have; anchors order nothing. The first in the order of lessons among several is a lesson that does not go after another of them; if there are several such — the lower tier of the lesson, the lowest tier of its skills ([format text](../spec/format/README.md), section 12), then the lesson's ID, byte by byte (the 2026-11-08 amendment to R05). Runs on an earlier package may go one after another by the current graph; then among them — the tier and the ID. The graph does not order the lessons of different modules, and the modules have tiers of their own: of the first lessons of each module the first is the one whose full ID is smaller.

**The band of a lesson** is the set of the bands of its tasks (Д90 in table 4 of RUN.md of `spec`; R107 spec). A lesson suits a skill when the band of the skill's recommended task is in this set. **The recommended task** is the one that "The task on the skill" of subsection 3.1 would choose among the skill's tasks in the pool **without the pause** before a task with a seen solution: otherwise right after a lesson its tasks are on pause, and the recommended one would jump into another band not because the learner grew. A lesson without tasks suits any band.

**A lesson on a skill with a verdict.** When branch 6, 7, 8 or 9 of subsection 3.1 has chosen a skill, the rule looks for **a lesson on the skill** — a lesson not passed, without an open run, where all the skills are with `verdict` and open, this one among them, and which suits the skill by band. If there is one, the choice is this lesson, not a task, with the code of the branch (subsection 3.5): `REASON_CODE_NEW_SKILL` on a new skill, `REASON_CODE_QUEUE` in the queue, when the recommended task of a skill not mastered has gone into a new band, and `REASON_CODE_ROTATION`, "in rotation", for a skill with the model's "mastered" whose task has gone above the mastery band. Of several — the first in the order of lessons. For a skill from the map the lesson's skills may be closed too, as for its tasks (subsection 3.2), and without a recommended task — the skill's pool is empty — any lesson of it suits it: R104 spec requires giving a lesson on a skill from the map while there is one. Outside the map a lesson that names another skill not issued, which waits for its lesson of the author's order, is not given as a lesson on the skill: that skill comes by its own lesson (the 2026-11-08 amendment to R05). Branches 3 and 4 — consolidation and the probe — give no lessons: consolidation is "a task for practice without an explanation" (concepts 5 spec). Repetition gives no lesson: it is a task (R06; subsection 3.4).

**Within a lesson** the engine skips nothing and does not end the lesson early. In a lesson whose skills are not all with a verdict it adds nothing either (R58 spec). In a lesson whose skills all have a verdict the code of the branch of E7 adds consolidation after a failed segment, branch 0 below — a variant before the judgement of `sim` (the 2026-11-14 amendment to R05; subsection 2.12). `api` does not call the engine at the end of a segment yet (architecture 3.7 api); `sim` does, from v0.1.15 (sources 6.9). After the end of a run the last closed attempt is a task of the lesson, and branch 3 of subsection 3.1 works as outside a lesson, but it needs a candidate. In the first slice the pool is the tasks of the lessons (sources 3.3), and after a lesson all its tasks are on pause: an error in the lesson's last task is consolidated only when the pause has ended or when the second pass has worked — that is, no module gave anything. With other modules the turn goes to them, and the error is not worked through for a day. So the author decided (R05): a task whose key the learner saw minutes ago is a weak consolidation, and the model does not set "mastered" by it anyway (R06 learner-model). There is no "early" in E7, and no new value of `ReasonCode` or of the run end reason (subsection 2.12).

**The author's order** — for lessons whose skills are not all with `verdict`, **lessons of the author's order**, mixed ones too — the rule does not read the learner model (concepts 4 spec; R58 spec):
- **branch 6a** — after branch 6 and before branch 7 of subsection 3.1, on the module's turn: the first lesson of the author's order not passed, without an open run, for which all the **lessons of the author's order** after which it goes by the graph are passed — among the lessons the engine gets ([format text](../spec/format/README.md), section 21). Lessons where all the skills are with `verdict` do not hold it back: they are given on the new and by band, and the learner may never get such a lesson. Of the ready ones — the first in the order of lessons: where the graph gives no order — by the lesson's tier, then by the lesson's ID, byte by byte (the 2026-11-08 amendment to R05). The code is `REASON_CODE_AUTHOR_ORDER`;
- a skill with `verdict` that a lesson of the author's order not passed teaches is not taken by branches 6–9 while it is not issued: it comes by this lesson, and after the lesson it is already issued and goes into the queue, by branch 7. Otherwise branches 8 and 9 would give a skill not issued with `REASON_CODE_ROTATION`. The price: if the learner left the run of a predecessor lesson, and there is no other, — this time branches 6–8 have nothing to give, and the module gives what the probe and repetition find on their turn and outside the turn, and then "the bottom of the ladder" of branch 9, if it has a skill that does not wait for a lesson; otherwise — "nothing to give" (the 2026-11-08 amendment to R05);
- tasks with `rubric` outside lessons — branch 6a too, those that have no attempt closed by "Done". A task with `rubric` is one where the primary skill of an item is with `rubric`, and outside lessons — one that no lesson of its module that the engine gets names. It stands as a lesson of its primary skills with `rubric`: it is ready when the lessons of the author's order of these skills are passed and those after which their lesson would go, and it goes after all the ready lessons, then in the order of the skill's graph, then by the task's ID; its primary skills with `verdict` must be open, as for a candidate, and the skills with `rubric` are held by the order of lessons; the pause before a seen solution — as for a candidate (subsection 3.1; the 2026-11-08 amendment to R05). In the first slice there are no such tasks: all the tasks of essays are in lessons;
- a module where the author's order has ended and branches 3–9 have nothing to give passes the turn to the next module (subsection 3.2).

**A prerequisite with `rubric` or `none` is counted** for a skill with `verdict` — in the state "Open" of subsection 3.1 — when a lesson that teaches it is passed, or an attempt at a task with `rubric` where it is primary is closed by "Done": such a skill will never get "mastered" (О-17; R06 learner-model). The lesson is read by the skills of its run, `Run.Skills`: the lesson may have left the lessons the engine gets. The rule reads the attempt's task by its description in the pool or in a lesson of the engine's modules; if there is no description, the attempt does not count the prerequisite (the 2026-11-08 amendment to R05).

**A prerequisite with nothing to teach with does not hold back** (the 2026-11-07 amendment to R03) — like an anchor whose teaching skills have no tasks (subsection 3.2). There is nothing to teach a skill with `verdict` that has in the input neither a lesson nor a task in the pool — on pause or not — where it is primary and every other primary skill of the task is open: a task that has another primary skill closed is not a candidate (subsection 3.1), and does not count — otherwise a prerequisite and the skill after it, primary in one task, would always wait for each other (the 2026-11-07 amendment to R03); nor a skill with `rubric` or `none` that has neither a lesson nor a task with `rubric`. A task teaches a skill with `verdict` when it is the skill's candidate; a skill with `rubric` — a task with `rubric` where it is primary in an item with `rubric`; a skill with `none` — a task with `rubric` where it is primary, as its "Done" counts it. A task with `rubric` teaches only when the rule can give it: outside the lessons of its module or in a lesson of the module not passed, and with its primary skills with `verdict` open — otherwise a prerequisite and the skill after it, primary in one task, would wait for each other (the 2026-11-09 amendment to R05). This happens when a skill's lesson or tasks do not suit the learner by age — then for good —, and with AI closed, while it is closed. The price: with AI closed a skill may open before "mastered" of its prerequisite. The count is in one step, as for an anchor (subsection 3.2): "every other primary skill is open" is read by "open" without this exception, and there is nothing to teach a skill with `verdict` without a band taught, as with a teacher of an anchor that the model will not make "mastered". A passed lesson teaches nothing any more — neither a prerequisite nor a teacher of an anchor: no branch will give it again (the 2026-11-08 amendment to R05).

**Consolidation in a lesson** — branch 0 of subsection 3.1, before branch 1, a variant before the judgement of `sim` (the 2026-11-14 amendment to R05). It fires when the last closed attempt is a segment of a run that branch 1 would read — open, of the engine's modules, not left, and on the map of a lesson that teaches the chosen skill S —, the lesson is among the lessons the engine gets, all the skills of the run, `Run.Skills`, have a verdict, the module's last evidence is past the trial series, and the attempt is a failure on a skill s of the run that is reachable by the rule of subsection 3.1 among its candidates of the first pass without the tasks of this lesson. The skills are the run's, as branch 1 reads them, and the lesson gives its tasks. Of several such skills — the first in the order of the graph; on the map s is S alone: the skill from the map holds (R04). The choice is the task on s among those candidates, by "The task on the skill" of subsection 3.1, with no second pass; the code is `REASON_CODE_CONSOLIDATION`, and on the map `REASON_CODE_SKILL_CHOSEN`. Without the lesson in the input its tasks are not known, and branch 0 gives nothing. The attempt of consolidation is outside the lesson, so after it branch 1 gives the lesson again: one consolidation a segment. Of several open runs, though, it gives the first in the order of lessons, which may be another: the input names no run the consolidation came from (subsection 2.12, "What will change in `spec`").

**The lesson of an open run** — branch 1 of subsection 3.1, the code is always `REASON_CODE_OPEN_RUN` (R100 spec):
- in "My subjects" — an open run of a lesson of the engine's modules, one whose ID begins with the ID of a module of the engine; when the last closed attempt is a segment of one of them, this one — further through the lesson, the next segment (the 2026-11-14 amendment to R05) —, otherwise of several the first in the order of lessons, by the skills of the run, `Run.Skills`, on the module's graph: a run may go on an earlier package (the 2026-11-08 amendment to R05);
- a run from whose segment's attempt the learner has just left — the last closed attempt is "left unfinished" and it is a segment of this run — is not given by branch 1 this time, in the mode of the map too: the learner asked for something else. The other branches never give a lesson with an open run;
- in the mode of the map — only a run of a lesson that teaches the chosen skill S, the run of the segment first, as above; the other open runs wait for "My subjects".

**A skill from the map** (subsection 3.2), the code `REASON_CODE_SKILL_CHOSEN`:
- S with `verdict` — a lesson on the skill, if there is one — not passed, only of skills with a verdict and suiting the band of the recommended task —, otherwise a task; when there is neither, — a lesson not passed that teaches S, a lesson of the author's order and a lesson of another band alike, in the order of lessons, and on the second pass, after the tasks on pause, — the lesson whose last run "passed" ended longest ago, by a new run (the 2026-11-09 amendment to R04);
- S with `rubric` or `none`:
  - first a lesson not passed that teaches S, in the order of lessons — the rule does not wait for its predecessors: the learner chose the skill;
  - otherwise a task with `rubric` on S outside lessons without "Done";
  - otherwise the lesson of S whose last run "passed" ended longest ago — by a new run;
  - otherwise the task with `rubric` on S whose attempt "Done" closed longest ago — by a new attempt (the 2026-11-07 amendment to R04): R104 spec requires giving a lesson or a task on a skill from the map while the pool and the lessons hold at least one;
  - the lessons of the steps go in the order of lessons, and on the third — by how long ago the last "passed" was, then by ID; the tasks — first by the pause before a seen solution, as candidates, then the one without "Done" first, the older "Done", by the task's ID (the 2026-11-08 amendment to R05).

**The issue of a lesson** is the issue of each of its skills in the summary of issues: so a lesson moves the queue and the module's time of issue (subsection 3.2). The counter n of the turn of the probe of a module counts choices — a task and a lesson as one each: the tasks of a lesson do not move it, and the turn of the probe does not skip. R104 spec recorded the summary without the issue of a lesson and counted a module's issued tasks, not its choices, and R107 spec added to it.

**Nothing given.** If the choice — "My subjects" over all the modules or a skill from the map — gave neither a lesson nor a task, the rule passes it a second time, and the tasks whose solution went to the device less than 24 hours ago are candidates again — after the others, the first being the one whose solution went longest ago — except the task of the last closed attempt (the 2026-11-07 amendment to R03); and the task of the last closed attempt with a seen solution is not a candidate in the second pass outside the map either, however long ago the solution went (the second 2026-11-10 amendment to R03). On a skill from the map the second pass takes it too, last (the 2026-11-07 amendment to R04; R104 spec). If the second pass gave nothing either — "nothing to give", without a code; with AI closed `api` adds the cause (R99 spec; R11 api). The key and the solution go to the device after a correct answer too (R82 spec), and a lesson of the first slice holds all the tasks of its skill and band (sources 3.3): without the second pass after a lesson the skill would be left without tasks for a day.

**Example: the three modules of the first slice** (`altnovatrail/modules`, `main` 6d061e9; sources 3.1 and 3.3).
1. **School maths of grade 3** — a lesson per skill, with three pages of difficulty 1, 3 and 5. A new `times-tables` — the lesson `times-tables`, `REASON_CODE_NEW_SKILL`. After the lesson all three pages are on pause: if the last one had an error, branch 3 finds no candidates. If other modules are connected too, the turn goes to them (subsection 3.2), and the error waits for the end of the pause. If the learner has one module, the second pass gives consolidation by the page whose solution went longest ago — not by the last one.
2. **Olympiad maths** — a lesson per skill and band, with three tasks of the band each. A learner of grade 3: `combinatorics-enumeration` — the lesson `combinatorics-enumeration-grades-3-4`, `REASON_CODE_NEW_SKILL`. When the skill's recommended task goes into `grades-5-6`, branch 7 gives the lesson `combinatorics-enumeration-grades-5-6`, `REASON_CODE_QUEUE`. The lesson of `grades-1-2` does not suit such a learner by band and does not hold the order.
3. **Short essays**, an adult learner. The order of lessons: the graph did not order `first-sentences` and `reading-model-essays`, and both are of tier 1: `first-sentences` goes first, by ID; then `reading-model-essays`, of tier 1, before `paragraphs`, of tier 3 (the 2026-11-08 amendment to R05), and `paragraphs`, and of the three lessons after `paragraphs` — `endings`, `improving-an-essay`, `writing-about-a-day`, by ID. All by branch 6a, `REASON_CODE_AUTHOR_ORDER`. `first-sentences` is mixed: the skill `choosing-a-first-sentence` with `verdict` comes by it, and then goes into the queue, but branch 6a is before branch 7, and the lessons go in a row. The lesson `writing-about-a-day` is only for `adult`, and `api` does not give it to a child, and `reading-model-essays` is without tasks, with a skill `none`.
4. **AI closed.** The pool has only tasks answered by tapping, and the lessons are only of such tasks (concepts 12 spec). If there are none of them in any module either, the second pass changes nothing, and the choice is "nothing to give" with a cause from `api`.

**What the simulator will check** — in addition to the lists of subsections 3.1 and 3.2:
- a new skill with a verdict that has a lesson of its band begins with a lesson, and a new band with a lesson of this band;
- the lessons of the author's order go in the order of the graph, and "goes in the author's order" of the criterion of product 10 spec is their share among the issues to skills without a verdict;
- "nothing to give" does not happen while an open skill has a candidate — a task where it is primary and every other primary skill is open — other than the task of the last attempt; except for two cases where branches 8 and 9 have nothing to give: an open skill out of reach has no bands where it is taught — it does not get onto "the bottom" (subsection 3.1) —, and each open skill with candidates waits for its lesson of the author's order, while the learner left the run of a predecessor lesson. On a skill from the map — while there is a lesson or a task on it (R104 spec; the 2026-11-09 amendment to R04). The tasks of skills closed by a prerequisite wait for it;
- in the second pass a task with a seen solution comes no earlier than the others.

### 3.4. Repetition

Decided by R06. Here — when a mastered skill whose review date has come comes back for repetition, and how repetition shares the module's choices with the other branches of subsection 3.1. The sign of repetition and forgetting are the model's: `learner-model` gives the review date, and "time to review" is a time of the choice no earlier than the date; the engine has no second threshold (the 2026-11-02 amendment to R23 spec; R04 learner-model).

**What the rule reads.** From `Read` for the time of the choice: for a mastered skill — the review date, with the mark "already" if the threshold does not hold even without forgetting, and the P of the items with retention — for the corridor (R04 learner-model; architecture 3.8 spec). Repetition needs nothing more from the model. The counter of the module's choices n is from the summary of issues, the same as for the turn of the probe (R03; the 2026-11-07 amendment to R04: a task and a lesson are one choice each).

**A skill due for review** is a skill with `verdict` in a module of the engine, mastered for the queue (subsection 3.1), that has candidates, and the time of the choice is no earlier than its review date or the date is marked "already". A mastered skill whose recommended task has gone above the mastery band is not counted as a skill due for review: it goes into the queue by branch 7, and there by a lesson of the new band (subsection 3.3). Skills with `rubric` and `none` are not repeated: they have no model (concepts 4 spec). The review date is no earlier than τ₀ = 1 day after the skill's last practice (R04 learner-model), so on the day of practice there is no repetition, except for a date marked "already": for such a skill the threshold does not hold even without forgetting, and practice does not remove the mark (R04 learner-model). "Time to review" is a time of the choice no earlier than `Review.At` from `Read`, or the mark `Already`; a skill without the model's "mastered" has no review date, and the rule does not read it. A skill that waits for its lesson of the author's order is not taken by branch 5, as by branches 6–9 (subsection 3.3; the 2026-11-08 amendment to R06).

**Branch 5, "Repetition",** the code `REASON_CODE_REPETITION` (R100 spec):
- **on the turn of repetition** — n > 5 and n mod 3 = 1, that is n = 7, 10, 13 and so on — a skill due for review. The turn does not coincide with the turn of the probe, n mod 6 = 5: that one has n mod 3 = 2. A turn taken by the lesson of an open run or by consolidation — branches 1 and 3, before the rotation (subsection 3.2) — does not come back, as with the turn of the probe. The turn of repetition, unlike the turn of the probe, does not wait for the end of the trial series: the series computes the level by the answers on any skill, and repetition does not get in its way (the 2026-11-08 amendment to R06);
- **outside the turn** — a skill due for review, when branches 6, 6a and 7 of the module gave nothing, before branches 8 and 9: otherwise branch 8 would give mastered skills that are not due, and branch 9 "the bottom of the ladder", and the skill due for review would wait for the turn;
- of several skills due for review — first one marked "already", and of those the one whose last practice is older, otherwise the first in the order of the graph would take every repetition; then the one with the earliest date; on a tie — the first in the order of the graph (the 2026-11-08 amendment to R06);
- the task — by "The task on the skill" of subsection 3.1, and repetition gives no lesson (R05). P with retention already sees forgetting, and after a break the first task is within the learner's strength. A skill due for review that is now out of reach is repeated too: the rule gives the task nearest to 0.775 — the easiest, as for a probe —, and a correct answer brings it back from the pause, as a correct probe does (subsection 3.1, "Reachable"). The second pass of subsection 3.3 works here too.

3 is a constant of the engine, as 6 is for the turn of the probe, and only the simulator `sim` changes it. There is no boundary of the day and no daily ceiling: there is no time zone in the input (concepts 12 spec), and repetitions are no more than a third of the module's choices.

**After repetition.** On a correct answer the model moves the date further off: the stability grows the more, the lower the retention was (R04 learner-model). On a failure consolidation goes next, branch 3, if the skill is reachable; two failures in a row take "mastered" off, and the skill comes back into the queue by branch 7 (R06 learner-model).

**With the other rules.** A mastered skill whose recommended task has gone above the mastery band is not a skill due for review: it goes into the queue by branch 7, whatever its review date (R03). In the mode of the map the code is always `REASON_CODE_SKILL_CHOSEN` (R100 spec; subsection 3.2). On several modules each module has its own turn of repetition, on its turn in the rotation (subsection 3.2). The rule does not read the daily goal (R06), and `spec` removed it from the engine's constraints (R107 spec).

**Example: school maths of grade 3** — one band, `grade-3`.
1. `times-tables` and `fractions-of-a-whole` are mastered, the learner is working on `division-with-remainder`.
2. Four days later the review date of `times-tables` has come. At n = 13 — the turn of repetition: branch 5, `times-tables`, a task in the corridor by P with retention, `REASON_CODE_REPETITION`. At n = 14 and 15 — new skills and the queue again; if `times-tables-d3` is failed, n = 14 is consolidation, branch 3.
3. The review date has come for both mastered skills — the one whose date is earlier goes first; the second waits for the next turn, n = 16.
4. After the holidays all the mastered skills are due, but the new goes on: repetition is once in three choices of the module.
5. All four are mastered — there is no new and no queue: repetitions go in a row, by date, before branch 8, and when no dates have come, branch 8 works.

**What the simulator will check** — in addition to the lists of subsections 3.1–3.3:
- the share of repetitions among the module's choices is no more than a third while there is the new or the queue;
- a skill due for review waits for repetition no longer than so many choices of the module;
- the measures of forgetting of the criterion of `sim` — its detailing decided them (R03–R05 sim) —, a false "mastered" after a break and the share of repetitions over time (architecture 3.9 spec).

### 3.5. Reason codes, the seed and the repeat of a choice

Decided by R07. Here — which code of `ReasonCode` each branch of the rule of subsections 3.1–3.4 gives, why the engine does without randomness, and what the record of the choice needs to repeat it without AI.

**Branches and codes** (R100 spec; [contracts text](../spec/contracts/README.md), section 15). A choice has one code — of the branch that made it, for a lesson as for a task, and in the second pass too (subsection 3.3).

| Branch | Code | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 0 — consolidation in a lesson | `REASON_CODE_CONSOLIDATION` | after an error in the first segment of the lesson `arithmetic-tricks-grades-3-4` — `tri-34-d1-2`, outside the lesson (subsection 3.3) |
| 1 — the lesson of an open run | `REASON_CODE_OPEN_RUN` | the learner came back to the begun lesson `times-tables` |
| 2 — a skill from the map | `REASON_CODE_SKILL_CHOSEN` | a lesson, and then tasks on `pigeonhole-basic`, chosen on the map (subsection 3.2) |
| 3 — consolidation | `REASON_CODE_CONSOLIDATION` | after an error in `gaps-34-d3-1` — `gaps-34-d1-1` (subsection 3.1) |
| 4 — a probe | `REASON_CODE_PROBE` | at n = 11 — the easiest task of `counting-gaps`, on pause |
| 5 — repetition | `REASON_CODE_REPETITION` | at n = 13 — `times-tables`, whose review date has come (subsection 3.4) |
| 6 — a new skill, a lesson on the new and "the bottom of the ladder" | `REASON_CODE_NEW_SKILL` | the lesson `combinatorics-enumeration-grades-3-4` (subsection 3.3) |
| 6a — the author's order | `REASON_CODE_AUTHOR_ORDER` | the lesson `paragraphs` after `first-sentences` and `reading-model-essays` |
| 7 — the queue of a reachable skill not mastered | `REASON_CODE_QUEUE` | `combinatorics-enumeration` was issued before `counting-gaps` |
| 7 — a skill with the model's "mastered" whose task has gone above the mastery band | `REASON_CODE_ROTATION` | the lesson `combinatorics-enumeration-grades-5-6` for a skill mastered on `grades-3-4` |
| 8 — all the reachable skills are mastered, no review dates have come | `REASON_CODE_ROTATION` | school maths: all four skills are mastered, no review dates have come |
| 9 — the queue at "the bottom of the ladder" | `REASON_CODE_ROTATION` | no skill is reachable, `times-tables` was issued before |
| "nothing to give" | without a code (R99 spec) | AI is closed, there are no tasks answered by tapping (subsection 3.3) |

`REASON_CODE_NEW_SKILL` requires no reachability and is exact at "the bottom of the ladder". The rule opens a skill also when a prerequisite is counted rather than mastered — an anchor "unknown", a cycle between modules, a prerequisite with nothing to teach with, a prerequisite with `rubric` or `none` counted by a lesson — and a skill with evidence of its own (subsections 3.1–3.3), and the comment of `cause.proto` says so: "whose prerequisites the engine's rule counts as met, or which has none". `REASON_CODE_QUEUE` requires both reachability and "not mastered", so the three places get the ninth value, `REASON_CODE_ROTATION` = 9: "Rotation: a skill given before comes round again, the one not given for longest first, though it does not fit the queue of `REASON_CODE_QUEUE`: it is mastered, or no skill is within reach". For `REASON_CODE_QUEUE`, "for longest" is "in its module" (subsection 3.2). So R07 asked, and R107 spec recorded it, with these words in `cause.proto` ([contracts text](../spec/contracts/README.md), section 15); the words for the adult are written by `app`, and the meaning of the earlier values did not change (the 2026-11-04 amendment to R29 spec). The rule gives each of the nine codes, and there is no code that it does not give. The golden vectors hold this (subsection 3.7): each scenario has a branch of this table, and the test requires its code, a scenario of each branch and each code at least once, `REASON_CODE_ROTATION` in all three places (the second 2026-11-08 amendment to R07).

**There is no randomness.** The engine takes no generator, and it has no seed of the choice and no names of places of its own (the 2026-10-20 amendment to R27 spec; R59 spec). It rests on facts:
- the rule of standalone was deterministic: "the same profile always produces the same brief" (SPEC 3.1 standalone; sources 1.1);
- each tie of the rule is broken by an order: in the corridor — frequent traps, nearness to 0.775, P and the task's ID; in the queue — the time of issue and the order of the graph; in the rotation of modules — the time of issue and the module's full ID; for lessons — the order of lessons and the ID; for review dates — "already" with how long ago the practice was, the date and the order of the graph;
- the variety of tasks for the learner comes from templates and the attempt's seed, which `api` takes (R59 spec; R60 spec);
- the simulator checks "the same input gives the same output" without a seed too (concepts 12 spec).

The price: learners in one state get one task and one order of unordered lessons. standalone set them apart by a hash of the learner's ID (SPEC 3.2 standalone), and the learner's ID is not among the engine's inputs (concepts 12 spec).

**The record of the choice** is kept by `api` (architecture 1 spec):
- **already there** — the versions of `engine` and `learner-model`, the package digests, the reason code, the release of `modules`, the number of the last evidence, the time of the choice, the grade and whether the pool was limited to tasks without AI;
- **the seed** — the field is empty: the rule takes no randomness, and this is seen from the version of `engine` in the same record. A repeat takes the same version, so a future version with randomness does not get in the way of old choices;
- **three new fields** — the skill from the map: it changes the whole choice, and it cannot be restored from the code and the task (R107 spec); the age category at the time of the choice: by it `api` selects the pool and the lessons (R07 api; architecture 3.7 api), and it changes at the yearly refinement and at a change of country (concepts 13 spec); the mark of which of the digests are the engine's modules, connected and suitable: the digests also name the modules of the log, which only `learner-model` gets (the 2026-11-02 amendment to R07 api), and connections change at any time;
- **the repeat derives** — the summary of issues it builds anew from the records of choices and attempts before this choice — so `spec` recorded it too (architecture 1 spec; R104 spec); the last attempt, its trap and outcome — from the records of attempts; the lesson runs and the closed attempts at tasks with a rubric it restores anyway: their reason and time of the end do not change (architecture 1 spec);
- **does not keep** — interests and the daily goal: the rule does not read them (R06; privacy 1 spec).

So `spec` recorded it too: architecture 1 spec and privacy 4 spec — by R107 spec. The type of the code in Go and the input — subsection 3.6.

**What the simulator will check:**
- each choice of a task or a lesson has a code from the list, and with the new value branches 7, 8 and 9 have one too;
- the same input gives the same choice and the same code — without a seed;
- a repeat by the record of the choice and the log gives the same choice that was made.

### 3.6. Types

**The module and the call.** The Go module `github.com/altnovatrail/engine`, the package `engine`; at build time it depends only on `github.com/altnovatrail/learner-model` and the standard library (R09). Consumers take it as a Go module by the release's tag — `go get github.com/altnovatrail/engine@v0.1.0` —, and the tag is linked to the signed release by the release's file `engine-<version>.tar.gz`, the code without tests, which `release-verify` accepts (R15). An error is a value, without a panic: a module, a task of the pool or a lesson twice by ID, two open runs of one lesson, a skill of a lesson that is not in the graph of its module, a module of the engine without `ModuleReadout`, a P of an item with a verdict from `Readout.Task` outside [0; 1] — NaN and ∞ too (subsection 3.7) — and any other error of `Readout.Task` itself about a task — its description, the record of its module in the model's graphs or of the module's current version: it comes wrapped in `ErrInvalid`. The engine reads the P of the candidates itself: `Readout.Task` for each candidate of the pool of the engine's modules, once per call and before the rule — so a P outside [0; 1] gives an error whichever branch runs, and the rule reads P ready-made. Once per call the tasks of the pools by ID are built too, and the pool with P and the index "skill → tasks": the rule takes a skill's candidates from the index, and of them the first by the total order, without sorting (R20). | `SkillResult` | `Skill`, `Answered`, `FirstRight`, `SolutionShown` | the skill; how many items of the attempt where it is primary got a first verdict; in how many of them it is "correct"; whether the solution was shown | "Failure" and "Correct" (3.1): a failure is `FirstRight` less than half of `Answered`, or the solution shown and `FirstRight` zero, as in the model (R06 learner-model) |
| `Summary` | `Skills[]` — `Skill`, `LastIssued`, `Issued` | when the skill was last issued — by a task on it or by a lesson that teaches it (R05) | a new skill, the queue, the probe, the module's time of issue (3.1, 3.2) |
| `Summary` | `Skills[]` — `LastCode`, `LastResult`, `HasLast` | the code of the choice of the skill's last closed attempt and its result on the skill | the return from the pause after a probe or a repetition (3.1; R06) |
| `Summary` | `Modules[]` — `Module`, `Choices` | n — the module's choices, a lesson as one (the 2026-11-07 amendment to R04) | the turns of the probe and of repetition (3.1, 3.4) |
| `Summary` | `Tasks[]` — `Task`, `SolutionSentAt` | when the task's solution last went to the device | the pause before a task with a seen solution (3.1; R82 spec) |

**The output** is `Choice`: `Kind` — `ChoiceKind` a lesson, a task or "nothing to give"; `ID` — the full ID of the lesson or the task, for a template the template's, and for "nothing to give" empty; `Reason` — `ReasonCode`, for "nothing to give" `REASON_CODE_UNSPECIFIED` (R99 spec; R100 spec). With AI closed `api` adds the cause of "nothing to give" (R11 api). `ReasonCode` and `ChoiceKind` are types of its own with the names and numbers of `cause.proto`, and so is `RunEnd` with `RunEndReason`; a test checks them against the file in `spec/` at the tag (R09). The test runs in CI of `engine`, where the submodule stands at a release's tag, and reads the file from there: the code takes the files of `spec` from its releases, and the submodule moves only by a move to a tag (R64 spec). The file is not part of the engine's Go module, and `go test` from the module cache skips this test — the consumer has no submodule. The job "Go" of the shared `go.yml` skips it too: it does not take the submodule. So the check is held by a job of its own, "Contracts" of `ci.yml` — with the submodule, by the recipe `ci-contracts`, and if there is no file there, it is red, not a skip. The record of the choice is written by `api` (subsection 3.5): it takes the version of `engine` from the build information of its binary.

**What is not in the input:** the seed (R07), the age category (R04), the daily goal (R06) and interests — format v0 has no variants of setting (R51 spec). Nor is what the rules do not read: the places of ended segments, the close reasons of an attempt with a rubric other than "Done", the traps that AI supposed (R03).

**The learner's data** in the input are `Readout`, `MapSkill`, `Runs`, `RubricDone`, `Last` and `Summary`. The engine holds them only in the memory of the call: it has no input and output, and it writes and keeps nothing, and on the server only the database of `api` keeps them (architecture 1 spec; privacy 1 spec; privacy 4 spec).

**Who builds the input:**
- `api` — the graph by the function `learnermodel.NewGraph` from the server part of the package, as for the model; the description of a task by the same rule as for evidence, and the traps of the items from `trap_values` of the checks; the lessons from the package's lessons; the runs, the attempts with a rubric, the last attempt and the summary from its own records; `Readout` by a call of `Read` for the time of the choice (R10 learner-model; R104 spec);
- `sim` — the same from `server.json` by the types of `formatv0.CanonicalView` of the Go module `spec` and from its own virtual learners (R62 spec; R72 spec; architecture 3.9 spec).

Neither `module-kit` nor the Go module `spec` is among the engine's dependencies. Its types are its own, and a new version of the format does not touch them (Д20 in table 4 of RUN.md of `spec`): `api` and `sim` translate into them what comes in a package.

**What the code will check:** the check of `ReasonCode`, `ChoiceKind` and `RunEnd` against `cause.proto`; that the order of the lists in the input does not change the choice; that a module, a task or a lesson twice is an error, not a choice, and two runs of one lesson are not; that P outside [0; 1], NaN and ∞ is an error.

### 3.7. The build for the device and the same bits

Decided by R10. Here — what is kept from the first code so that the engine gives the same choice on any build and can one day choose on the device, and what checks it. The model to follow is `learner-model`, which holds this in its code (R09 learner-model; sources 5.4).

**The promise.** The same input gives the same choice and the same code, bit for bit, on any build by Go's compiler, `gc`, of one version of `engine` and `learner-model`: on any architecture it knows, with any GOAMD64 and GOARM64, on any processor and in any version of Go no older than the one `go.mod` names, WebAssembly too. TinyGo and gccgo are outside the promise. The repeat of a choice stands on it: it takes the same version of `engine` from the record (subsection 3.5), but not the same build — `api` builds the engine into its binary, and between deployments its version of Go, its architecture or GOAMD64 may change (R10).

**Where builds differ.** Addition, subtraction, multiplication, division and square root of IEEE 754 are rounded correctly, and Go allows fusing several operations into one, with one rounding — except those that an explicit conversion separates (sources 8.1). The compiler fuses multiplication with addition or subtraction, and it turns a division by a constant with an exact reciprocal into a multiplication. The engine computes something of its own in three places: the P of a task on a skill — the mean P of its items, the nearness to 0.775 — a difference, and the comparisons with 0.70 and 0.85 (subsection 3.1). The mean is a division by the number of items, a variable, and it is not fused: `s/float64(n) - 0.775` is a separate division and subtraction. But let a constant stand in the code, and the choice will differ: `s*0.5 - 0.775` and `(p1+p2)/2 - 0.775` are fused on arm64 and ppc64le, and the second also on s390x, riscv64 and loong64, while `float64((p1+p2)/2) - 0.775` is fused nowhere (the experiment of 2026-11-07, sources 8.1). The sum of the items' P is by the item's place: floating-point addition is not associative. The engine does not compute the items' P: `Readout.Task` of `learner-model` gives them, with its same bits.

**The rules of code** are the rules of R09 learner-model in what the engine needs:
1. The result of each `*` and `/` on floating-point numbers goes through `float64(…)` at once, if it is not an operand of another `*` or `/` or of a comparison. No `*=` and `/=` on such numbers, no `complex`, no type parameters in their arithmetic.
2. From `math` — only the functions with an exactly defined result, the same list as for `learner-model`: `Sqrt`, `Abs`, `Floor`, `Ceil`, `Trunc`, `Copysign`, `Signbit`, `Frexp`, `Ldexp`, `Float64bits`, `Float64frombits`, `IsNaN`, `IsInf`, `Inf` and the constants. The engine needs no exp and log; should it need them — `detmath` of `learner-model`, by an amendment to R10.
3. A P of an item with a verdict outside [0; 1] — NaN, ∞ or simply outside the interval — is an error as a value, not a choice: a comparison with NaN is always false, and the choice would depend on the order of the checks (subsection 3.6).
4. The time, the counters of choices and the pause are `int64`: 24 hours is 86,400 seconds; the numbers of items in the result of a skill, `Answered` and `FirstRight`, are `int32`, and the rule "failure" compares them widened to `int64`. Floating-point numbers into integers — only with a check of the range.
5. Order — without iterating over maps, sorting with a total order, IDs byte by byte, without Unicode tables: the order of the graph, of lessons and of IDs is a rule, not chance (subsection 3.5).
6. No global mutable state — `api` calls the engine from many requests at once —, and no goroutines of its own.
7. Imports — only from the list below. No cgo, no `.s` files, no `//go:linkname`, no build tags. The rules and the list are for the core, the package `engine`; the glue with the app at stage 5 needs `syscall/js` and the build constraint `js && wasm`, and it will have a list of its own — by an amendment to R10.

**Dependencies** — Go's standard library by the list of `learner-model` and the root package `github.com/altnovatrail/learner-model` (R09). In the code: `math` — only the functions of rule 2 —, `cmp`, `slices`, `errors`, `strconv` and `strings` — only the byte-wise functions. The tests — `bytes`, `crypto/sha256`, `fmt`, `math/rand/v2`, `os`, `path/filepath` and `testing`, and `compress/gzip` and `encoding/json` read the packages of a release of `modules` for the golden vectors — for the tests, not the code (the third 2026-11-08 amendment to R10); `os` and `path/filepath` also read `cause.proto` in the `spec` submodule (subsection 3.6). This is the list of `.github/coderules.json` below: the packages `go/*` and `runtime`, which the list of `learner-model` gives to its test of the rules, went away with the port at `engine` too (the fourth 2026-11-08 amendment to R10). Not taken — the Go module `spec`, `module-kit`, third-party modules, `time`, `math/rand`, `sync`, `unsafe`, `reflect`, `runtime` and encoders, as for `learner-model`. A new package goes into the list by an amendment to R10.

**For the device.** The core builds for the browser, `GOOS=js GOARCH=wasm`, and for WASI, `GOOS=wasip1 GOARCH=wasm`, as it is.
