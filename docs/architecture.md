## 1. Purpose and bounds

**Does:**
- chooses what comes next for the learner — a new skill, consolidation, a probe or repetition — and gives the choice a reason code. An engine choice in the contract is a lesson or a task outside a lesson with its own ID, and when there is nothing to give, "nothing to give", without a code: `ChoiceKind` knows no other kinds. The engine does not choose an item within a task;
- chooses by a rule, without AI: the same input gives the same output, and each choice has a cause. The rule takes no randomness: the engine has no generator and no seed of the choice. A rule that took randomness would have its own seed of the choice and its own names of places, and its generator would be the platform's shared generator: PCG, as in Go's `math/rand/v2`, and the stream of a place derived as by the simulator of standalone, as the module format's [text](../spec/format/README.md) sets it;
- holds the rules of the choice:
  - from the rule of standalone ([standalone's SPEC](https://github.com/mathtrail/mathtrail-standalone/blob/c6cbeb2/SPEC.md) at c6cbeb2): the success corridor — a task that the learner solves with a probability from 0.70 to 0.85, aiming at 0.775: the probability is given by `learner-model`, and the corridor is the engine's rule; consolidation after an error, while the skill is within reach; probes of a deferred skill; the queue — of the reachable unmastered skills that have already been issued, first the one not issued for the longest; a task on the learner's frequent traps; the setting to the learner's interests — format v0 has no such variants, they will come with a minor version;
  - new in NovaTrail: a skill is offered when its prerequisites are mastered, and a mastered skill comes back for repetition when its review date comes. The date is given by `learner-model`, and when to give a skill whose date has come, how many repetitions a day and how they share the queue is the engine's rule. Repetition is a task in the corridor on the module's turn of repetition, once in three of its choices, and when there is no new skill, no lesson of the author's order and no queue, outside the turn too;
- chooses lessons: their order is set by the skill graph, and where the graph gives no order, the engine's rule chooses — by the lesson's ID. To a skill with a verdict a lesson is given on the new — when the skill is new or its recommended task has gone into a band whose lesson has not yet been passed — and a lesson's band is the bands of its tasks. The engine does not reorder a lesson's segments, adds no consolidation to a lesson and does not end a lesson early, and within a lesson it is not called: `api` calls it when a lesson run has ended and when the learner asks for something else;
- does not block a lesson when a prerequisite is an anchor skill of the shared taxonomy that no module of the learner teaches: "unknown" does not hold a lesson back. The engine has no separate short check — the check of an anchor is the answers on the very skill that needs it — and the module that teaches the anchor is offered by `api` by the module index. "Does not know" holds a skill back even when the skills that teach it have no evidence yet — except for a cycle of prerequisites between modules and an anchor whose teaching skills have neither tasks in the pool nor lessons;
- for skills with a rubric and without assessment — `rubric` and `none` — does not adapt to the learner: the order is set by the author, by the skill graph and the order of a lesson's segments, and where the graph gives no order, the rule chooses without the learner model, by the lesson's ID, with the code `REASON_CODE_AUTHOR_ORDER`;
- when there is nothing to give, goes through the rule a second time — with the tasks whose solution was seen less than a day ago, except the last one, and on a skill from the map with it too, after all the others, — and only then returns "nothing to give";
- sees a task template as one task, whichever instance comes up, and does not give again at once a task with a key — not a template — whose solution has already gone to the learner's device; the solution of a task with a rubric, open after "Done", does not count, and such a task the engine reads by `RubricDone`;
- chooses in the mode it is given: it leads itself — "My subjects", where the connected modules go in turn, — or the learner chose a skill on the skill map, and the choice holds until the learner asks for something else; a task from paper is no input, since the learner takes it from their own page and its attempts give no evidence;
- there are no constraints in the input: the modules, lessons and tasks are already selected by age category by `api` — the platform offers a module only to learners of its age categories, and a task may name the categories it suits, — and the engine does not read the category; there is no daily goal either in the profile or in the contract, and the rule does not read it. Neither is among the constraints the platform gives the engine.

**Does not:**
- does not estimate knowing skills: the estimate, its uncertainty, retention, "mastered" with the mastery band, the review date and knowing an anchor skill are computed by `learner-model`, and the engine reads them. The engine neither sets nor passes the threshold "mastered";
- does not call an AI model, and a model does not choose for it: the platform rejects this, and with it standalone's chat model asking for another topic, naming a reason ([standalone's SPEC](https://github.com/mathtrail/mathtrail-standalone/blob/c6cbeb2/SPEC.md) at c6cbeb2);
- does not give a verdict — that is `module-kit`'s;
- does not write tasks: tasks come from the module — a published pool and templates with parameters, and `module-kit` builds a template's instance by the attempt's seed;
- does not read module packages or the database: the skill graphs, the lessons, the pool of tasks, the lesson runs, the closed attempts at tasks with a rubric and the learner model are passed to it by `api`, and on the simulator by `sim`;
- keeps nothing: the record of the choice is kept by `api`.

**Data and links:**
- it has no data of its own: pure computations without input and output. The time comes from outside, and the engine has no seed: the rule takes no randomness;
- at the input:
  - the learner model and the skill graphs of the connected modules. For the time of the choice the model gives the probability of a correct answer of an item with retention — for the corridor —, the estimate of a skill with uncertainty and retention, and for a mastered skill also "mastered" with the mastery band, the cautious probability with retention and the review date. For a skill — each trap the program found, with the number of pieces of evidence and the time of the last one, and which of them are frequent the engine chooses; for an anchor skill — "knows", "does not know" or "unknown", with a reason and the skills that teach it; for a module — whether the trial series has ended and whether the last evidence was still in it;
  - the lessons and the pool of tasks, which `api` sets. When the AI that the check needs is closed to the learner, the pool holds only tasks that are checked without AI, the lessons are only of such tasks, and the pool may be empty; the engine does not know the cause;
  - the profile's lesson runs — open and ended, with the end reason and the time of the end, without the places of ended segments, — and the attempts at tasks with a rubric closed by "Done": the task and the time;
  - the summary of issues from `api` — from the records of choices and attempts that it keeps anyway: for a skill, the time of the last issue — the issue of a lesson is the issue of each of its skills — and the result of the last closed probe or repetition, as facts, not as a mark "correct"; for a module, the number of choices, a lesson counted as one; for a task with a key, when its solution last went to the device;
  - the last closed attempt — its task, the result on the primary skills and the lesson of the run if the attempt is its segment, — the skill from the map as the mode, and the time of the choice. Interests, the age category, the daily goal and a seed are not in the input: the rule does not read them.

  The learner model, the attempts, the runs, the summary of issues and the skill from the map are the learner's data: on the server only the database of `api` keeps them;
- at the output — the engine choice and the reason code. The code is one value of `ReasonCode`, a closed list of nine values without parameters: a choice has one code — of the branch of the rule that made it, as the [contracts text](../spec/contracts/README.md) holds. Only an adult sees the code — in progress, at the attempt, — and it does not go into the answer to the learner; the words for the adult are written by `app`. A branch of the rule that is not in the list the engine asks for with a row «В реестр spec», and it comes in as a new value, and the meaning of the earlier ones does not change. Frequent traps are not a branch but the choice of a task on a skill, and they need no code of their own. There is no separate short check of an anchor skill, and it needs no code. Branch 8 of the rule below — all the skills are mastered —, the queue at "the bottom of the ladder", branch 9, and a skill "mastered" by the model that came back into the queue by branch 7 give the ninth value, `REASON_CODE_ROTATION`, "in rotation";
- the record of the choice is kept by `api`: the versions of `engine` and `learner-model`, the package digests, the seed and the reason code, the release of `modules`, the number of the log's last evidence by which the choice was made, the time of the choice, the grade by which the model computed the starting point of modules without evidence, and whether the pool was limited to tasks without AI, the skill from the map, the age category and the mark of the engine's modules, with the seed field empty. The choice is repeated without AI — by the evidence log: the append-only log is the source of truth, and the state of the skills is a cache;
- the types of the graph are simple and not from `module-kit`: so the libraries can be built for the device too. `learner-model` defines them, and the engine takes them from it: the graph is per module, with bands and skills, and the description of a task is one for evidence and for a candidate task from the pool. Lessons and the pool are types of `engine`, and the types of the input about lessons and about attempts with a rubric are its own and simple, like the types of the graph: a candidate is the description of a task and the traps of its items, and the reason code is a type of its own with the names and numbers of the contract. The recommendation of Т-10 is to compute on the server and to keep a computation on the device possible, for offline; offline is stage 5. The engine gives the same choice on any build of one version, WebAssembly too, and CI checks this;
- at build time it depends only on `learner-model` and the standard library, and `sim` and `api` import it. The language is Go, as for all the platform's libraries. The code takes the files of `spec` from its releases: the test of the codes reads `cause.proto` in the submodule at the tag, and the Go module `spec` is not among the engine's dependencies. If it comes, its version and the submodule are moved together, by one move to a tag, and the module `spec` is excluded from Dependabot. A file of a NovaTrail release enters the code and CI only once `just -f spec/justfile release-verify` has accepted it;
- at stage 1 the engine runs only on the simulator: `sim` imports it and runs it on virtual learners, and reads the module packages itself — the server part, by the types of the canonical view from `spec`, without `module-kit`. From stage 2 the engine is a library in `api`'s process: on the result of an attempt `api` gives the evidence to `learner-model`, then the engine chooses the next lesson or task;
- the baseline, the first version of `engine`, rests on its own tests; each change after it passes the simulator `sim` by a criterion written down before the candidate's first run. The gate runs in the CI of `engine`, [ci.yml](../.github/workflows/ci.yml): it takes `sim` by the tag and the SHA of its commit and writes `go.work` on the fly, without committing it, — `sim`, the code of the pull request and `learner-model` at the commit from the gate file of `sim`, — and a change that worsens the criterion does not merge. The criterion itself and the tolerance bands are `sim`'s, and the success criterion of the first slice is that the engine on the simulator behaves sensibly on all three graphs: it holds the success corridor where there is a verdict, goes in the author's order where there is none, does not loop and does not declare "mastered" too early. The first slice is three modules of two subjects: olympiad maths 1–6, school maths of grade 3 on a workbook, and short essays in English;
- stage 1, together with `learner-model` and `sim`: the simulator needs both the learner model and the choice rule. The starting point is the rule by which standalone chose a task ([standalone's SPEC](https://github.com/mathtrail/mathtrail-standalone/blob/c6cbeb2/SPEC.md) at c6cbeb2).

### 2.8. Found after the first version

Two places where the rule of the first version did not do what `spec` or the rule itself promises; both are fixed in the code, through the gate of `sim`.
- **A skill from the map with `verdict` without tasks in the pool**: its lessons, passed or of the author's order, were not given on the map, and the choice was empty, while `spec` says to give a lesson or a task as long as the pool and the lessons hold at least one. The map gives a lesson of the author's order or of another band, and on the second pass a lesson passed long ago.
- **A prerequisite with `none` or `rubric` and a task with `rubric`**: "Done" of such a task credited the prerequisite, but "nothing to teach with" did not count the task as its teacher, and for a prerequisite with `rubric` a task that no branch would give counted as one. A task teaches a prerequisite only when the rule can give it.

### 2.9. The base's corridor

Which branches and codes of the base give items outside the corridor 0.70–0.85, and why: the hypotheses H1–H5 and their levers. This is a **rough look**: it decides nothing and does not touch the criterion — the judgement of `sim` decides. The numbers by branch and code are measurements 1; the paths of `sim` are the entry on them in its [log](https://github.com/altnovatrail/sim/blob/v0.1.2/docs/decisions.md), and its pool ceiling is [`internal/run/pool.go`](https://github.com/altnovatrail/sim/blob/v0.1.2/internal/run/pool.go), at `v0.1.2`.

**Where the gap is.** On olympiad maths the base has 27–28% of items in the corridor, while the "pool ceiling" of `sim` — the largest share in the corridor of one task of the pool that the rule could give — leaves only 1–2% outside it: the gap is the rule's. On school maths 13–19% are in the corridor, and the pool ceiling too leaves 69–75% outside it ("School maths", below).

**Hypotheses and levers.**
- **H1. A task with a seen solution goes after all the unseen ones — and the corridor loses.** In branches 3 and 7 of olympiad maths the skill almost always has a candidate in the corridor (94–97%), but an unseen one only in 37–48% of choices, and the issued task is in the corridor by the rule's P in almost as many (38–51%; the 1–3 points more are seen tasks that got into the corridor by how long ago they were shown). The solution goes to the device after a correct answer too, so the tasks in the corridor that the learner has solved become "seen", and the hard ones stay unseen. `bySolution` puts the unseen ones first, and the corridor is compared only within one group. When there are no unseen ones at all (13–21% of the choices of branches 3 and 7), the seen ones go by how long ago they were shown, without the corridor. Where a skill has many unseen ones in the corridor — branch 7 for a mastered skill, `ROTATION`, — the rule's P is in the corridor in 81–83% of choices. **Lever** — the order of `byCorridor` and `bySolution` (`task.go`): a seen task after a pause of 24 hours is a candidate on a par with the unseen ones by the corridor, or at least a seen one in the corridor comes before an unseen one outside it. `spec` says only not to give it as the next task, and after how long is decided by `engine`, so this is a change of the engine's rule, not of `spec`. **Risk:** a task with a seen solution does not let "mastered" be set — `E4` may move; and a repeat of the seen is the simulator's measure `M8`. A rough upper bound, not a forecast: if branches 3 and 7 on `G0` had as many in the corridor by P* as `ROTATION` of branch 7 (49%), the cell's corridor would rise from 27% to about 39%, and the share below 0.50 would fall from 34% to about 17%.
- **H2. Consolidation after every error keeps the learner on a hard skill.** Branch 3 is 31–40% of the choices of olympiad maths, and it takes the task by the same order as in H1, on the skill where the learner has just made an error. A loop comes out: a hard task — an error — consolidation — a hard one again. **Lever** — the condition of branch 3 (`rule.go`): consolidate only when the skill has a candidate in the corridor, and otherwise go by the turn of modules; or a target of consolidation easier than the target of the corridor. H1 removes most of the cause, and H2 is worth trying together with it or after it.
- **H3. A lesson on a new skill.** The lessons of branch 6 are 8% of the choices of olympiad maths, but 18–20% of items, and of them 14% are in the corridor by P*; the lessons of branch 7 are another 10–13% of items, 22–31% in the corridor. The tasks of a lesson go in the author's order, not by the corridor. The engine's lever here is narrow — whether to give a lesson, — and the corridor within a lesson is consolidation in a lesson, below.
- **H4. The model.** Even where the rule's P is in the corridor, P* is in the corridor in 33–68% (`ROTATION` of branch 7 and repetition): a band of 0.15 is narrower than the spread of the estimate. On `G2` of olympiad maths the model's mean P is below the mean P* by 0.08–0.09 for branch 7, and on `G2` of school maths by 0.16 for the queue, and there 54% of its items are above 0.95. The model's P and P* here are of one moment, so this is the model's lag (`M3.lag`) — a lever of `learner-model`, not of the engine.
- **H5. The probe and "the bottom of the ladder".** Branches 4 and 9 on olympiad maths are up to 5% of items, below the corridor by definition: a probe is the easiest task of a skill on pause, and branch 9 is skills when there are no reachable ones. The lever is the cadence of the probe, `probeEvery` (`rule.go`), and the gain is small.

**School maths.** The gap is small and rests on the pool: a skill has two candidates, the queue has a candidate in the corridor in only 32–36% of choices, and in 62–67% only above the corridor; branch 9 is 27% of the choices of `G0`. The engine's rule will not move this: it is a matter of the module's content, and the judgement is on the paths of olympiad maths.

**What came of it.** Three variants from H1 and H2 — A, "the corridor before the seen"; B, "after a pause the seen is as the unseen"; C, "A and consolidation only in the corridor" — went to the judgement `engine-1` of `sim`, on 1,000 learners a deciding run ([report](https://github.com/altnovatrail/sim/blob/9cd59ef7a51c0fa2c383686b8f9a2c4e1bee9ecd/judgements/engine-1/report.md)). All three raised the corridor — the score of the group `corridor` is 0.133–0.147 above zero, — and none holds "not worse". B lost coverage, `E4.given` on olympiad maths: "mastered" is not set by a repeat with a seen solution. A and C lost narrowly on the model's lag `M3.lag` on `G2`: a task that the model sees in the corridor is in fact easier — H4. The base stays; A and C on a later base are "The corridor on the new base", below.

### 2.10. The speed of the choice

Where the time of `engine.Choose` goes on a large pool — `olympiad-math` 0.1.1, 772 tasks — and whose time it is, the engine's or `learner-model`'s: measurements 2.1.

**The levers** the profile showed, each without a change of the choice; what the code took of them are the types, under "How it is built":
- **an index "skill → tasks of the pool"**, once per choice: `candidates` goes only through the skill's tasks, not through the whole pool for each skill;
- **`poolTask` once per choice**, not a copy and a sort of the items of all the tasks on each pass of `skills` and in `openSkills`;
- **lists by ID once**, not by both `checkIDs` and `probabilities`;
- **slices of candidates** with the capacity by the index, without the growth of `growslice`.

The choice is about 1.8× faster and the same to the bit (measurements 2.2), and a faster `Readout.Task` of `learner-model` makes it about 1.8× faster again (measurements 2.3). `Readout.Task` is a lever of `learner-model`, not of the engine.

**P lazily is not a lever.** Even at the start the primary skills are open for 440–471 tasks of 772 — for the rest the rule needs no P, — and at "mastered" for all 772: the gain is up to 40% of `Readout.Task` at the start and zero after. And P "on demand", for the candidates that a branch has reached, the log rejects: P outside [0; 1] is an error whichever branch is going, and otherwise the error would depend on the branch. Without new facts this is not proposed.

### 2.11. An anchor skill without a teacher

What the engine does at an anchor "unknown", as spec's glossary now holds it too: there is no separate short check, the rule, the input and the codes do not change, and a module that teaches the anchor is offered by `api`; the log holds the options it rejected. The facts are the package `olympiad-math` 0.1.1 in [`testdata/modules`](../testdata/modules), [`learner-model` v0.1.0](https://github.com/altnovatrail/learner-model/tree/v0.1.0), [`spec` v0.15.0](https://github.com/altnovatrail/spec/tree/v0.15.0) and [`sim` v0.1.6](https://github.com/altnovatrail/sim/tree/v0.1.6): `internal/run/pool.go`, `internal/measure/model.go` and `internal/learner/assumptions.go`.

**What holds.**
- An anchor is "unknown" when no module of the engine teaches it or only skills that cannot become "mastered" teach it: `Read` gives `AnchorUnknown` with the reason `AnchorReasonNoTeacher` or `AnchorReasonNoVerdictTeacher`. Such an anchor does not hold a skill back. For a learner without `grade-3-math`, in `olympiad-math` 0.1.1 this is so for two skills of 19: `fractions-parts` rests on `core.fractions-of-a-whole`, `number-divisibility` on `core.division-with-remainder`. After `fractions-parts` come `percent-basic` and `ratio-sharing`, after `number-divisibility` nothing.
- A task cannot check an anchor. A task's primary skill is only a skill of the module, and an anchor can only be a supporting one, as the module format's [text](../spec/format/README.md) sets it, and the model does not move a supporting skill. The tasks with the anchor as a supporting skill are the tasks of the very skill that needs it, and the format has no check task. The check is the learner's answers on that skill, in the glossary's words: "there is no separate check of an anchor skill: the anchor is checked by the learner's answers on the very skill that needs it".
- A module is offered to the learner by `api`, from the module index by `teaches`, and the skill map shows the modules that teach the anchor beside "unknown", `AnchorSkillState.teaching_modules` in `progress.proto`. The engine reads neither the index nor this field, `ChoiceKind` knows no module, and no value of `ReasonCode` is a check of an anchor.
- The simulator's truth counts an anchor without a teacher as known: a candidate with a check of the anchor or with the proposal of a module needs, before its judgement, a population and a measure of its own.

**How the rule acts** for a learner of olympiad maths alone who does not know the anchor:
1. The anchor "unknown" does not hold: `fractions-parts` is open when `arithmetic-tricks` is mastered, and comes by branch 6 — a lesson on a new skill, `REASON_CODE_NEW_SKILL`.
2. The tasks of the skill are the check. An error after the module's trial series is branch 3, consolidation, `REASON_CODE_CONSOLIDATION`, while the skill is reachable.
3. Failures drop P: when the easiest task has P < 0.70, the skill is on pause, and the module's turn goes to other skills. On the turn of a probe, once in six choices of the module, branch 4 gives it by its easiest task, `REASON_CODE_PROBE`, if of the skills on pause it is the one not issued for the longest; a correct answer returns it within reach.
4. `percent-basic` and `ratio-sharing` without evidence of their own wait while `fractions-parts` is not mastered, and the other skills of the module go their own way.

The engine cannot do more: to hold a skill back for an anchor that no task and no lesson in the input teaches is to close it forever. The price is probes on one skill, and the skills after it without evidence of their own never open if the learner never learns the anchor; there is no dead end between modules or within the module's turn, and the probe holds "does not loop". A better outcome is given only by a module that teaches the anchor, which `api` proposes. Of an anchor the rule reads only `Status`, and it takes the teaching skills from `Teaches` of the graphs of the engine's modules.

## 3. How it is built

Here is the library as its code is: the rule decided above, with what is costly computed once per call, on the version of `learner-model` that `go.mod` requires. There is one call, `Choose(Input) (Choice, error)`, and it keeps nothing between calls. How it chooses, each step a subsection below, in this order:
1. **The mode** — "My subjects" or a skill from the map, and in "My subjects" the modules go in turn; anchor skills and a cycle between modules.
2. **The branches of the rule** within a module, in order: the lesson of an open run, consolidation, a probe, repetition, a new skill, the author's order, the queue, "all mastered" and "the bottom of the ladder"; a task on a skill — in the corridor 0.70–0.85 aiming at 0.775.
3. **A lesson or a task** — a lesson is given on the new: when branch 6–9 has chosen a skill, the rule gives a lesson on it not yet passed that suits the band of its recommended task: so a lesson comes to a new skill and also when its recommended task has gone into a band whose lesson has not yet been passed, — and for skills with `rubric` and `none`, the author's order; when this gave nothing — the second pass, then "nothing to give".
4. **Repetition** — on the module's turn of repetition, once in three of its choices, and outside the turn.
5. **The reason code** — the code of the branch that made the choice; there is no randomness; what the record of the choice keeps.

The types of the input and the output, with the rule at each field, and the discipline of the code, the build for the device and the checks of CI come after them. What the simulator `sim` will check, at the end of each subsection of the rule, is what the engine expects of the criterion; the criterion is `sim`'s own, in its `docs/architecture.md`, and it judges each change of the rule. The first version is the baseline by its own tests, and each change after it passes the gate of `sim`.

### 3.1. The core of the rule: a task outside a lesson in one module

Here is a task outside a lesson in one module, for skills with a verdict. Several modules, a skill from the map and anchor skills, lessons and the author's order, and repetition fit into this rule in the subsections after it. The numbers are as in standalone: the corridor 0.70–0.85 aiming at 0.775 and a probe once in six choices of the module ([standalone's SPEC](https://github.com/mathtrail/mathtrail-standalone/blob/c6cbeb2/SPEC.md), its [`corridor.go`](https://github.com/mathtrail/mathtrail-standalone/blob/c6cbeb2/internal/domain/rating/corridor.go) and [`pause.go`](https://github.com/mathtrail/mathtrail-standalone/blob/c6cbeb2/internal/domain/tutor/pause.go) at c6cbeb2). These are the engine's constants, and only a run of the simulator `sim` changes them.

**What the rule reads.**
- From `Read` of `learner-model`, for the time of the choice: for a skill, the number of pieces of evidence, the time of the last practice, "mastered" with the mastery band, and the traps the program found, with the number of pieces of evidence and the time of the last one; from `Readout.Task`, the P of each item of a candidate task, with retention ([`learner-model`'s architecture](https://github.com/altnovatrail/learner-model/blob/98ba29f/docs/architecture.md) at 98ba29f).
- The module's skill graph: the skills, the tier, the prerequisites, the assessability and `teaches`. On several modules, the graphs of all the engine's modules, and for an anchor skill its state from `Read`: "knows", "does not know" or "unknown", with a reason. The order of the skills in the graph carries no meaning, by the module format's [text](../spec/format/README.md), so the **order of the graph** here is by tier, and within a tier by the local ID, byte by byte.
- The module's pool of tasks from `api`.
- The summary of issues from `api` — an input of the engine's contract, not a log: a fold of the records of choices and attempts that `api` keeps anyway. For a skill, when a task on it or a lesson that teaches it was last issued, and, if its last closed attempt was a probe or a repetition — a choice with `REASON_CODE_PROBE` or `REASON_CODE_REPETITION`, — its result on the skill as facts: the items where the skill is primary and there is a first verdict, how many of them are "correct" and whether the solution was shown, while whether the attempt is correct the engine computes by the rule "Failure" below; for a module, the counter n of its choices, a task and a lesson one each; for a task, when its solution last went to the device. The issue of a lesson, the counter of choices, repetition and the result as facts are the part of the summary that the engine's rule brought into it.
- Whether the module's trial series has ended and whether the module's last evidence was still in it: the first five answers in the module live in `learner-model`, and `Read` gives these two marks for a module.
- The time of the choice. The rule reads no randomness: the engine does without it.

**The states of a skill.**
- **Open** — each prerequisite of the module is "mastered" by the model, on any band, or there are no prerequisites. The engine has no second threshold. A prerequisite with `rubric` or `none` will never get "mastered", and it is credited by the author's order, as lessons below say; a prerequisite that is an anchor skill — as several modules below say. A skill that has evidence of its own is open whatever becomes of its prerequisites, and a prerequisite that has nothing to teach with in the input does not hold.
- **The candidates of a skill** — the tasks of the pool where it is primary — for the task or for an item whose skills replace the task's skills, — without those that are not candidates because of a seen solution (below), and without the tasks for which another primary skill is closed: otherwise the evidence would open a closed skill past its prerequisites. This restriction does not hinder a skill from the map: its candidates are its tasks. A template is one task, whichever instance comes up.
- **The P of a task on a skill** — the mean P of its items with a verdict for which this skill is primary: the share of items that the learner will solve correctly. An item without a verdict does not enter the mean, and a task without an item with a verdict on the skill is not its candidate: it will give no evidence on it. The primary skill is as in `learner-model`: an item's own, and the task's skill for an item without its own.
- **Failure** — a closed attempt where the skill has fewer than half correct first verdicts, as in the model. An attempt with the solution shown and without correct first verdicts is a failure too. **Correct** — an attempt with a first verdict on the skill that is not a failure. An attempt without first verdicts on the skill and without the solution shown is neither a failure nor "correct": it leads to no consolidation and does not return the skill from a pause.
- **Reachable** — open, and the easiest candidate — the one with the highest P — has P ≥ 0.70. A skill whose last closed attempt — a probe or a repetition — is correct is reachable too: so a correct answer of a probe or of a repetition returns the skill from a pause — a rule of `engine`, not a rise of the model, — until the next closed attempt. A skill without candidates is not reachable.
- **On pause** — open, with evidence, not reachable, — including when its P fell from forgetting or the pool narrowed. **Not reached yet** — open, without evidence and not reachable: it will come when the learner grows.
- **Mastered for the queue** — the model's "mastered", as long as the skill's recommended task (below) is not above its mastery band. If it has gone above, the skill is in the rotation again, as in standalone ([standalone's SPEC](https://github.com/mathtrail/mathtrail-standalone/blob/c6cbeb2/SPEC.md) at c6cbeb2), and the model's "mastered" stays as it is.

**The branches, in order.** The first that fits makes the choice, and its code is the code of the choice.

| No. | Branch | When | Skill | Code |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The lesson of an open run | — | — | `REASON_CODE_OPEN_RUN` |
| 2 | A skill from the map | the mode "skill chosen on the map" | — | `REASON_CODE_SKILL_CHOSEN` |
| 3 | Consolidation | the module's last closed attempt is a failure for a primary skill, its evidence is no longer in the trial series, and the skill is reachable | this skill; of several, the first in the order of the graph | `REASON_CODE_CONSOLIDATION` |
| 4 | Probe | the trial series has ended, the counter of the module's choices n > 5, n mod 6 = 5, and there is a skill on pause | the one on pause not issued for the longest; at a tie, the first in the order of the graph | `REASON_CODE_PROBE` |
| 5 | Repetition | the turn of repetition — n > 5, n mod 3 = 1 — and there is a skill due for review; outside the turn — when branches 6, 6a and 7 gave nothing, before branches 8 and 9 | "already" — of them, the one whose practice is older, — then the earliest date; at a tie, the first in the order of the graph | `REASON_CODE_REPETITION` |
| 6 | New skill | there is a reachable skill that has not been issued yet | the first in the order of the graph | `REASON_CODE_NEW_SKILL` |
| 6a | The author's order | there is a lesson of the author's order whose predecessors by the graph are passed | — | `REASON_CODE_AUTHOR_ORDER` |
| 7 | The queue | there is a reachable skill, already issued and not mastered for the queue | the one not issued for the longest; at a tie, the first in the order of the graph | `REASON_CODE_QUEUE`; for a skill with the model's "mastered" that came back into the rotation, `REASON_CODE_ROTATION` |
| 8 | All mastered | all the reachable ones are mastered for the queue | the choice of branch 7 over all the reachable ones | `REASON_CODE_ROTATION` |
| 9 | No reachable ones | branch 8 has nothing to give: no skill is reachable, or each reachable one waits for its lesson of the author's order | branches 6 and 7 over the open skills with candidates whose lowest band where they are taught is the lowest among such skills — "the bottom of the ladder" of standalone ([standalone's SPEC](https://github.com/mathtrail/mathtrail-standalone/blob/c6cbeb2/SPEC.md) at c6cbeb2); in the first slice this is the module's first band. A skill without bands where it is taught does not get to the "bottom", and neither does a skill that waits for a lesson of the author's order. When each issued skill of the "bottom" is mastered for the queue, the choice of branch 7 over all the skills of the "bottom", as branch 8: so no open skill of the "bottom" with candidates is left unissued | branch 6 — `REASON_CODE_NEW_SKILL`; branch 7 and the choice over the whole "bottom" — `REASON_CODE_ROTATION` |

An attempt that the learner left by asking for something else leads to no consolidation. In the trial series there is no consolidation and no probe: in it an error is not worked through, and the series goes by new skills, as in standalone ([standalone's SPEC](https://github.com/mathtrail/mathtrail-standalone/blob/c6cbeb2/SPEC.md) at c6cbeb2). An error in the last answer of the series is not worked through either. A turn of the probe given to consolidation does not come back. The task of a probe is the easiest candidate of the skill, whatever its P. When there are no candidates with branch 9 either, the rule passes a second time, with the tasks from the pause, and then — "nothing to give". The counter n is `Choices` of the module's row in the summary: the module's choices before this choice, zero for a module without a row, so the first turn of the probe is the module's 12th choice. A skill on pause without candidates gives no probe, and the turn goes to the branches below.

**The task on the skill.** When a skill is chosen, the rule chooses a task among its candidates:
- a task with a key — not a template — whose solution went to the device less than 24 hours ago is not a candidate; the solution of a task with a rubric, open after "Done", does not count, and `api` gives no time for it in `Summary.Tasks`. Later it is a candidate, but after all the candidates without a seen solution, and among such the first is the one whose solution went longest ago. 24 hours is a constant of the engine. In the second pass — when the choice gave nothing — such tasks are candidates again, after the others, except the task of the last closed attempt. The task of the last closed attempt whose solution went to the device is not a candidate of the next choice, however long ago the solution went: the platform does not give it as the next task;
- if there are candidates with P in the corridor, from 0.70 to 0.85, — among them the one that catches more of the learner's frequent traps on this skill, then the nearest by P to 0.775, and of two equally near the easier one, with the greater P, as the point of standalone ([standalone's SPEC](https://github.com/mathtrail/mathtrail-standalone/blob/c6cbeb2/SPEC.md) at c6cbeb2). So frequent traps choose the task without taking it out of the corridor;
- if there is none in the corridor — the nearest by P to 0.775, of two equally near the easier one. For a probe — the easiest;
- a frequent trap is one that at least two pieces of evidence of the skill found, in order of their number and then of the time of the last one. Of the tasks that catch equally many frequent traps, the first is the one whose caught trap is higher in this order, and with an equal number and time — by the trap's ID: the places of the caught traps are compared in ascending order. The traps of a task are the traps of its items where the skill is primary. This is the threshold "repeating" of an adult's progress — two closed attempts of a profile —, but on the skill. A trap that AI supposed the rule does not read: it does not go into the model. Which traps a task expects is carried by the engine's candidate — the traps of the items by `trap_values`;
- then — the order of the task's ID, byte by byte.

The rule does not read the learner's interests yet: format v0 has no variants of setting. When they come, their place is the setting of the task, not a branch, as in standalone.

The engine does not choose an item within a task: the choice is a lesson, a task or "nothing to give", as `ChoiceKind`.

**Example: olympiad maths.** The graph of the first batch ([the graphs of `modules` v0.1.0](https://github.com/altnovatrail/modules/tree/v0.1.0/modules)): `combinatorics-enumeration` and `counting-gaps` are tier 1, `pigeonhole-basic` is tier 2, with the prerequisite `combinatorics-enumeration`; the bands are `grades-1-2`, `grades-3-4` and `grades-5-6`, each skill has tasks of difficulty 1, 3 and 5 — `counting-gaps` on `grades-1-2` has 2, 3 and 5. The order of the graph is `combinatorics-enumeration`, `counting-gaps`, `pigeonhole-basic`. A learner of grade 3 starts on `grades-3-4`; the probabilities below are for the example, the model computes them.
1. No evidence, the series is on. Both skills of tier 1 are open, `pigeonhole-basic` is closed. Both are reachable — `enum-12-d1-3` has P above 0.70. Branch 6: `combinatorics-enumeration`. The recommended task is the one with P nearest to 0.775, say, `enum-34-d1-1`, and the skill has a lesson of its band not yet passed — the lesson `combinatorics-enumeration-grades-3-4`. The code is `REASON_CODE_NEW_SKILL`.
2. Branch 6: `counting-gaps`, `REASON_CODE_NEW_SKILL`.
3. There are no new reachable skills. Branch 7: `combinatorics-enumeration` — it was issued earlier. `REASON_CODE_QUEUE`. And so, alternating, to the end of the series: there is no consolidation in it.
4. After the series the learner made an error in `gaps-34-d3-1`. Branch 3: `counting-gaps` is reachable, `REASON_CODE_CONSOLIDATION`; the corridor has already moved toward the easy tasks, and the recommended one is `gaps-34-d1-1`.
5. Two more errors, and the easiest task of `counting-gaps` has P < 0.70: the skill is on pause. At n = 11 — the turn of the probe, branch 4: `gaps-12-d2-1`, the easiest, `REASON_CODE_PROBE`. The answer is correct — the skill is reachable again.
6. `combinatorics-enumeration` has "mastered" on `grades-3-4`. `pigeonhole-basic` opens, branch 6: `REASON_CODE_NEW_SKILL`. When the recommended task of `combinatorics-enumeration` goes to `grades-5-6`, the skill will come back into the queue.

**Example: school maths of grade 3.** The graph ([the graphs of `modules` v0.1.0](https://github.com/altnovatrail/modules/tree/v0.1.0/modules)): `times-tables` is tier 1; `division-with-remainder` and `fractions-of-a-whole` are tier 2, both after `times-tables`; `comparing-fractions` is tier 3, after `fractions-of-a-whole`. There is one band, `grade-3`, with tasks of difficulty 1, 3 and 5 per skill.
1. Only `times-tables` is open: branch 6 — the lesson `times-tables` —, then branches 7 and 3, while it has no "mastered". If even `times-tables-d1` is too hard, there are no reachable skills, and branch 9 gives the same skill, by the easiest task.
2. The solution of `times-tables-d3` is shown: for 24 hours it is not a candidate, and then it goes after `times-tables-d1` and `times-tables-d5`.
3. "Mastered" on `times-tables` — both skills of tier 2 are open: branch 6 gives `division-with-remainder`, then `fractions-of-a-whole` — the order of IDs.
4. "Mastered" on `fractions-of-a-whole` opens `comparing-fractions`. When all four are mastered for the queue, the skills due for review go as repetition outside the turn, and the rest by branch 8, `REASON_CODE_ROTATION`.

**What the simulator will check** — what the engine expects of the criterion of `sim`, which is an item of `sim`'s card:
- the share of issued tasks with P from 0.70 to 0.85 on skills with a verdict — "holds the corridor";
- each reachable skill is issued no less often than once in so many choices, and a skill on pause comes back by a probe — "does not loop";
- a skill without evidence of its own does not open before "mastered" of its prerequisites — "mastered" too early is declared by the model, and the engine does not build on it;
- the same input gives the same choice and the same code, and each choice of a task or a lesson has a code.

### 3.2. Several modules, a skill from the map and anchor skills

The rule above works within a module; here — how it leads through all the engine's modules — connected and suitable for the learner —, how a skill chosen on the map holds, and what an anchor skill does to the state "Open". Lessons and the author's order, and repetition, come after.

**"My subjects".** `ChooseNext` carries no module: the engine leads through all the modules at once, by the [contracts text](../spec/contracts/README.md). The first branch that fits makes the choice:
1. The lesson of an open run — branch 1, as lessons below say.
2. Consolidation — branch 3 by the profile's last closed attempt, in its module. On the module's turn below, branch 3 does not repeat, even if the last closed attempt of this module is a failure: it has already been consolidated, or other modules went after it. An attempt that the learner left by asking for something else — the outcome "left unfinished", the close "left" — leads to no consolidation in any mode: otherwise asking for something else would bring back the same skill.
3. The module's turn. The time of issue of a module is the latest time of the last issue among its skills from the summary of issues; the issue of a lesson is the issue of each of its skills. The turn goes to the module where it is longest ago, and a module without issues goes first; on a tie — the order of the module's full ID, byte by byte. Within the module branches 4–9 work. The trial series, the counter of the turn of the probe and "the bottom of the ladder" are each module's own, and the rule does not compare the levels of modules: the modules have scales of their own. The modules' turns are equal, not their shares of issues: consolidation adds tasks to the module where the learner makes errors, and a module without reachable skills takes its turn by branch 9, "the bottom of the ladder".
4. A module whose branches gave nothing passes the turn to the next one in the same order; if none gave anything — the second pass with the tasks from the pause, and then "nothing to give".

The code of a choice is the code of the branch that made it. For `REASON_CODE_QUEUE` on the module's turn, "not issued for the longest" is within its own module; the meaning of the code is the same, and `spec`'s comment of the code says so.

**A skill from the map** — the mode "skill chosen on the map", branch 2; a task and a lesson on the skill always have the code `REASON_CODE_SKILL_CHOSEN`, and the lesson of an open run `REASON_CODE_OPEN_RUN`. The mode replaces items 2–4 above:
- which skill can be chosen the map decides: only one not closed by its rule "open", and the engine takes this rule as an input. Its own reachability of "My subjects" does not get in the way: it gives the skill from the map whatever its state by the core of the rule;
- the task on the chosen skill — by the rule "The task on the skill" above: in the corridor, and without candidates in the corridor the nearest by P to 0.775, with the pause before a task with a seen solution. The skill is given when closed, on pause and in the module's trial series too: the learner's choice is stronger, as a topic chosen by a person in standalone ([standalone's SPEC](https://github.com/mathtrail/mathtrail-standalone/blob/c6cbeb2/SPEC.md) at c6cbeb2), and the model's series computes the module's level by the answers on any skill;
- a lesson on the skill and a skill with `rubric` or `none` — as lessons below say; if the skill has neither a lesson nor candidates — the second pass, and on a skill from the map it takes the task of the last closed attempt too, after all the others: the engine's contract requires giving a lesson or a task on a skill from the map while the pool and the lessons hold at least one, and such a choice is empty only when `api` limited the pool to tasks without AI. To a skill with `verdict` without a lesson on the skill and without tasks the rule gives a lesson of the author's order too, and on the second pass a passed lesson too; if the skill is not in the engine's modules — "nothing to give";
- the lesson of an open run in the mode of the map — only a lesson that teaches the chosen skill, and the other open runs wait;
- the choice holds until the learner asks for something else: `ChooseNext` without a skill brings back "My subjects". For this the record of the choice keeps the chosen skill — the full ID of the skill from the map, which is not among the record's shared fields, and it cannot be restored from the task: a primary skill may be an item's alone. `api` calls the engine for `next` and at the end of a run with the skill from the last record of the choice, and the new record carries it on, the lesson of an open run too, — whatever code the choice came out with. The repeat of a choice needs the same skill too: the mode is an input of the rule.

**An anchor skill and "Open".** A prerequisite `core.<name>` holds a skill back when `Read` gives "does not know" — with the reason "not mastered" or "no evidence". "Knows" and "unknown" do not hold it back. "No evidence" holds it back because the module that teaches the anchor is connected, and the module's turn will lead to it by itself. There is nowhere to lead when no skill with `verdict` that teaches the anchor in the engine's modules has either a task in the pool — on pause or not: the pause will end — where every other primary skill is open, or a lesson — for example, with AI closed the pool narrowed to tasks without it: then "does not know", with any reason, does not hold the skill back, as "unknown" does not, — otherwise it would wait for tasks that the rule will not give. A teaching skill that is closed or out of reach holds it back: it will come when the learner grows in its module. "Every other primary skill is open" is read here by "open" without this exception, in one step: so the count is finite, and it errs toward "does not hold back". The teaching skills are the skills of the engine's modules whose `teaches` names the anchor, with `verdict` and a band taught, and such a skill is taught by a task where it has an item with a verdict, or by a lesson whose skills name it. There is no separate short check at "unknown": a task will not check the anchor — an anchor is only ever its supporting skill —, and the check is the answers on the skill itself. The check is the engine's to set, and the engine sets no separate one. A module cannot be an engine choice — `ChoiceKind` knows a lesson, a task and "nothing to give" —, and `api` proposes it by the module index, by the field `teaching_modules` of the skill map, which the engine does not read.
- **A cycle between modules.** The graph G is the skills of the engine's modules; its edges go from a skill to each of its prerequisites of the module and from a skill to each skill of the engine's modules whose `teaches` names its anchor prerequisite. If a skill that teaches `core.X` lies in one strongly connected component of G with the skill s, then `core.X` does not hold the skill s back. Within a module there are no cycles — the format rule `skill-graph.acyclic` —, and `skill-graph.own-anchors` does not let a skill rest on an anchor of its own module: such a component always goes through several modules. In the first slice there are no cycles.
- **Knowing goes away** — two failures in a row took "mastered" off the skill that teaches, or the module that teaches no longer gives its graph. A skill with evidence of its own — where it is primary — stays open, and a skill without it waits again. So practice on a skill by a choice from the map opens it too: the learner chose it themself, and "My subjects" will not give a skill out of reach anyway — only by a probe.

**Constraints.** The engine does not read the age category: `api` already selects the modules, lessons and tasks by it ([`api`'s architecture](https://github.com/altnovatrail/api/blob/06dad44/docs/architecture.md) at 06dad44), and it is not among the engine's constraints. There is no daily goal either in the profile or in the contract, the rule does not read it, and it is not among the engine's constraints either. A task from paper is no input of the engine, and there is no branch for it.

**Example: both maths.** In the first batch of `modules` olympiad maths rests on no anchor skills ([its manifest in `modules` v0.1.0](https://github.com/altnovatrail/modules/blob/v0.1.0/modules/olympiad-math/manifest.yaml)), so the example takes its graph from the [format examples](../spec/format/examples/skill-graph/valid/olympiad-math.yaml) — the 17 skills of standalone, where `fractions-parts` needs `arithmetic-tricks` and `core.fractions-of-a-whole` —, and school maths of grade 3 from `modules`, where `fractions-of-a-whole` teaches `core.fractions-of-a-whole`. A learner of grade 3 who has both.
1. The modules alternate: the school one goes first, without issues — `novatrail/grade-3-math` comes before `novatrail/olympiad-math` in the order of IDs —, and the first choice in it is the lesson `times-tables`; after the module's trial series an error in its task keeps the next choice in it, by consolidation, and in the series it does not.
2. `fractions-parts` waits: the anchor is "does not know" — first "no evidence", then "not mastered", until `fractions-of-a-whole` is mastered.
3. "Mastered" on `fractions-of-a-whole` gives "knows": `fractions-parts` is open and on the turn of olympiad maths comes by branch 6, `REASON_CODE_NEW_SKILL`.
4. Two failures in a row take "mastered" off `fractions-of-a-whole`: the anchor is "does not know" again. The begun `fractions-parts` is open, and `ratio-sharing` — after `fractions-parts` — is open if it has evidence of its own, and waits if it has none and `fractions-parts` is not mastered.
5. The learner has only olympiad maths: no module of the engine teaches the anchor, it is "unknown", and `fractions-parts` is open when `arithmetic-tricks` is mastered.
6. The learner chose `pigeonhole-basic` on the map, still closed: first goes the lesson of `pigeonhole-basic` of the band of its recommended task — in the first batch of `modules` a skill has a lesson for each band —, and then the tasks on it, the nearest to the corridor, all with `REASON_CODE_SKILL_CHOSEN` — after an error and after a correct answer alike —, until the learner asks for something else. If the learner left a task begun wrongly, the next choice is by "My subjects" already, without consolidation, and `pigeonhole-basic` with evidence of its own is open: "My subjects" will give it when it is reachable.
7. A cycle — a synthetic one: the skill a of the module A rests on `core.y` and teaches `core.x`, the skill b of the module B rests on `core.x` and teaches `core.y`. a and b are one component of G, and neither anchor holds them back.

**What the simulator will check** — in addition to the list of the core of the rule:
- the turn comes to each module in turn, and a module's share of issues beyond its turns comes from consolidation and branch 9;
- a skill without evidence of its own does not open while its anchor is "does not know", except for a cycle and an anchor whose teaching skills have no candidates, — with the measure of a false "knows" of `sim`'s criterion. For a skill without evidence of its own this narrows the item of the core about the module's prerequisites;
- there is no dead end between modules: each open skill with candidates is issued sooner or later;
- a choice from the map holds until `ChooseNext` without a skill.

### 3.3. Lessons, the author's order and "nothing to give"

Here — when the choice above gives a lesson, how the skills with `rubric` and `none` go, and what the engine gives when there is nothing to give. Repetition comes after.

**What the rule reads about lessons.** The lessons of the engine's modules — the lesson's skills and its segments, and for a task segment the description of the task with its band; `api` gives only the lessons that suit the learner whole ([`api`'s architecture](https://github.com/altnovatrail/api/blob/06dad44/docs/architecture.md) at 06dad44). The profile's runs — open and ended, with the end reason and the time of the end: the rule does not read the places of ended segments — the segments go in the author's order, and `api` leads the lesson on. The closed attempts at tasks with a rubric — of them the rule reads only those closed by "Done", with the task and the time. The last closed attempt comes with the lesson of its run, if it is a segment of a lesson.

**A lesson is passed** when it has a run that ended "passed", on any package. "Unchecked", "not suitable" and "abandoned" do not pass the lesson: it can be given again, by a new run.

**The order of lessons** is by the graph, as the module format's [text](../spec/format/README.md) defines it: lesson A goes after lesson B when a skill of A, through the module's prerequisites, transitively, requires a skill of B that A does not have; anchors order nothing. The first in the order of lessons among several is a lesson that does not go after another of them; if there are several such — the lower tier of the lesson, the lowest tier of its skills, then the lesson's ID, byte by byte. Runs on an earlier package may go one after another by the current graph; then among them — the tier and the ID. The graph does not order the lessons of different modules, and the modules have tiers of their own: of the first lessons of each module the first is the one whose full ID is smaller.

**The band of a lesson** is the set of the bands of its tasks. A lesson suits a skill when the band of the skill's recommended task is in this set. **The recommended task** is the one that "The task on the skill" above would choose among the skill's tasks in the pool **without the pause** before a task with a seen solution: otherwise right after a lesson its tasks are on pause, and the recommended one would jump into another band not because the learner grew. A lesson without tasks suits any band.

**A lesson on a skill with a verdict.** When branch 6, 7, 8 or 9 has chosen a skill, the rule looks for **a lesson on the skill** — a lesson not passed, without an open run, where all the skills are with `verdict` and open, this one among them, and which suits the skill by band. If there is one, the choice is this lesson, not a task, with the code of the branch: `REASON_CODE_NEW_SKILL` on a new skill, `REASON_CODE_QUEUE` in the queue, when the recommended task of a skill not mastered has gone into a new band, and `REASON_CODE_ROTATION`, "in rotation", for a skill with the model's "mastered" whose task has gone above the mastery band. Of several — the first in the order of lessons. For a skill from the map the lesson's skills may be closed too, as for its tasks, and without a recommended task — the skill's pool is empty — any lesson of it suits it: the engine's contract requires giving a lesson on a skill from the map while there is one. Outside the map a lesson that names another skill not issued, which waits for its lesson of the author's order, is not given as a lesson on the skill: that skill comes by its own lesson. Branches 3 and 4 — consolidation and the probe — give no lessons: consolidation is "a task for practice without an explanation" in `spec`'s glossary. Repetition gives no lesson: it is a task.

**Within a lesson** the engine adds nothing and skips nothing, and does not end the lesson early — in all lessons, those whose skills are all with a verdict too. Consolidation after a failed segment did not pass the judgement of `sim` (the entry on the judgement `engine-lesson-1` of `sim`'s [log](https://github.com/altnovatrail/sim/blob/b171f6f/docs/decisions.md) and its [report](https://github.com/altnovatrail/sim/blob/b171f6f/judgements/engine-lesson-1/report.md) at b171f6f). `api` does not call the engine at the end of a segment; `sim` does, in a lesson whose skills all have a verdict, and its learner has one open run, so the base answers with the lesson of this run by branch 1, the next segment, and on the base the step gives the same bits (the entry on the step within a lesson of `sim`'s [log](https://github.com/altnovatrail/sim/blob/v0.1.15/docs/decisions.md) at `v0.1.15`). After the end of a run the last closed attempt is a task of the lesson, and branch 3 works as outside a lesson, but it needs a candidate. In the first slice the pool is the tasks of the lessons (the folders `lessons/` and `tasks/` of [`modules` v0.1.0](https://github.com/altnovatrail/modules/tree/v0.1.0/modules)), and after a lesson all its tasks are on pause: an error in the lesson's last task is consolidated only when the pause has ended or when the second pass has worked — that is, no module gave anything. With other modules the turn goes to them, and the error is not worked through for a day. The rule accepts this: a task whose key the learner saw minutes ago is a weak consolidation, and the model does not set "mastered" by it anyway. On `olympiad-math` 0.1.1 and 0.3.0 a skill has tasks outside its lessons, and there branch 3 consolidates the error of the last segment at once. There is no early end of a lesson, and no value of `ReasonCode` or of the run end reason for one.

**The author's order** — for lessons whose skills are not all with `verdict`, **lessons of the author's order**, mixed ones too — the rule does not read the learner model:
- **branch 6a** — after branch 6 and before branch 7, on the module's turn: the first lesson of the author's order not passed, without an open run, for which all the **lessons of the author's order** after which it goes by the graph are passed — among the lessons the engine gets. Lessons where all the skills are with `verdict` do not hold it back: they are given on the new and by band, and the learner may never get such a lesson. Of the ready ones — the first in the order of lessons: where the graph gives no order — by the lesson's tier, then by the lesson's ID, byte by byte. The code is `REASON_CODE_AUTHOR_ORDER`;
- a skill with `verdict` that a lesson of the author's order not passed teaches is not taken by branches 6–9 while it is not issued: it comes by this lesson, and after the lesson it is already issued and goes into the queue, by branch 7. Otherwise branches 8 and 9 would give a skill not issued with `REASON_CODE_ROTATION`. The price: if the learner left the run of a predecessor lesson, and there is no other, — this time branches 6–8 have nothing to give, and the module gives what the probe and repetition find on their turn and outside the turn, and then "the bottom of the ladder" of branch 9, if it has a skill that does not wait for a lesson; otherwise — "nothing to give";
- tasks with `rubric` outside lessons — branch 6a too, those that have no attempt closed by "Done". A task with `rubric` is one where the primary skill of an item is with `rubric`, and outside lessons — one that no lesson of its module that the engine gets names. It stands as a lesson of its primary skills with `rubric`: it is ready when the lessons of the author's order of these skills are passed and those after which their lesson would go, and it goes after all the ready lessons, then in the order of the skill's graph, then by the task's ID; its primary skills with `verdict` must be open, as for a candidate, and the skills with `rubric` are held by the order of lessons; the pause before a seen solution — as for a candidate. In the first slice there are no such tasks: all the tasks of essays are in lessons;
- a module where the author's order has ended and branches 3–9 have nothing to give passes the turn to the next module.

**A prerequisite with `rubric` or `none` is counted** for a skill with `verdict` — in the state "Open" — when a lesson that teaches it is passed, or an attempt at a task with `rubric` where it is primary is closed by "Done": such a skill will never get "mastered" (О-17). The lesson is read by the skills of its run, `Run.Skills`: the lesson may have left the lessons the engine gets. The rule reads the attempt's task by its description in the pool or in a lesson of the engine's modules; if there is no description, the attempt does not count the prerequisite.

**A prerequisite with nothing to teach with does not hold back** — like an anchor whose teaching skills have no tasks. There is nothing to teach a skill with `verdict` that has in the input neither a lesson nor a task in the pool — on pause or not — where it is primary and every other primary skill of the task is open: a task that has another primary skill closed is not a candidate, and does not count — otherwise a prerequisite and the skill after it, primary in one task, would always wait for each other; nor a skill with `rubric` or `none` that has neither a lesson nor a task with `rubric`. A task teaches a skill with `verdict` when it is the skill's candidate; a skill with `rubric` — a task with `rubric` where it is primary in an item with `rubric`; a skill with `none` — a task with `rubric` where it is primary, as its "Done" counts it. A task with `rubric` teaches only when the rule can give it: outside the lessons of its module or in a lesson of the module not passed, and with its primary skills with `verdict` open — otherwise a prerequisite and the skill after it, primary in one task, would wait for each other. This happens when a skill's lesson or tasks do not suit the learner by age — then for good —, and with AI closed, while it is closed. The price: with AI closed a skill may open before "mastered" of its prerequisite. The count is in one step, as for an anchor: "every other primary skill is open" is read by "open" without this exception, and there is nothing to teach a skill with `verdict` without a band taught, as with a teacher of an anchor that the model will not make "mastered". A passed lesson teaches nothing any more — neither a prerequisite nor a teacher of an anchor: no branch will give it again.

**The lesson of an open run** — branch 1, the code is always `REASON_CODE_OPEN_RUN`:
- in "My subjects" — an open run of a lesson of the engine's modules, one whose ID begins with the ID of a module of the engine; of several — the first in the order of lessons, by the skills of the run, `Run.Skills`, on the module's graph: a run may go on an earlier package;
- a run from whose segment's attempt the learner has just left — the last closed attempt is "left unfinished" and it is a segment of this run — is not given by branch 1 this time, in the mode of the map too: the learner asked for something else. The other branches never give a lesson with an open run;
- in the mode of the map — only a run of a lesson that teaches the chosen skill S; the other open runs wait for "My subjects".

**A skill from the map**, the code `REASON_CODE_SKILL_CHOSEN`:
- S with `verdict` — a lesson on the skill, if there is one — not passed, only of skills with a verdict and suiting the band of the recommended task —, otherwise a task; when there is neither, — a lesson not passed that teaches S, a lesson of the author's order and a lesson of another band alike, in the order of lessons, and on the second pass, after the tasks on pause, — the lesson whose last run "passed" ended longest ago, by a new run;
- S with `rubric` or `none`:
  - first a lesson not passed that teaches S, in the order of lessons — the rule does not wait for its predecessors: the learner chose the skill;
  - otherwise a task with `rubric` on S outside lessons without "Done";
  - otherwise the lesson of S whose last run "passed" ended longest ago — by a new run;
  - otherwise the task with `rubric` on S whose attempt "Done" closed longest ago — by a new attempt: the engine's contract requires giving a lesson or a task on a skill from the map while the pool and the lessons hold at least one;
  - the lessons of the steps go in the order of lessons, and on the third — by how long ago the last "passed" was, then by ID; the tasks — first by the pause before a seen solution, as candidates, then the one without "Done" first, the older "Done", by the task's ID.

**The issue of a lesson** is the issue of each of its skills in the summary of issues: so a lesson moves the queue and the module's time of issue. The counter n of the turn of the probe of a module counts choices — a task and a lesson as one each: the tasks of a lesson do not move it, and the turn of the probe does not skip.

**Nothing given.** If the choice — "My subjects" over all the modules or a skill from the map — gave neither a lesson nor a task, the rule passes it a second time, and the tasks whose solution went to the device less than 24 hours ago are candidates again — after the others, the first being the one whose solution went longest ago — except the task of the last closed attempt; and the task of the last closed attempt with a seen solution is not a candidate in the second pass outside the map either, however long ago the solution went. On a skill from the map the second pass takes it too, last. If the second pass gave nothing either — "nothing to give", without a code; with AI closed `api` adds the cause. The key and the solution go to the device after a correct answer too, and a lesson of the first slice holds all the tasks of its skill and band (the folders `lessons/` and `tasks/` of [`modules` v0.1.0](https://github.com/altnovatrail/modules/tree/v0.1.0/modules)): without the second pass after a lesson the skill would be left without tasks for a day.

**Example: the three modules of the first slice** ([`modules` v0.1.0](https://github.com/altnovatrail/modules/tree/v0.1.0/modules), its graphs and lessons).
1. **School maths of grade 3** — a lesson per skill, with three pages of difficulty 1, 3 and 5. A new `times-tables` — the lesson `times-tables`, `REASON_CODE_NEW_SKILL`. After the lesson all three pages are on pause: if the last one had an error, branch 3 finds no candidates. If other modules are connected too, the turn goes to them, and the error waits for the end of the pause. If the learner has one module, the second pass gives consolidation by the page whose solution went longest ago — not by the last one.
2. **Olympiad maths** — a lesson per skill and band, with three tasks of the band each. A learner of grade 3: `combinatorics-enumeration` — the lesson `combinatorics-enumeration-grades-3-4`, `REASON_CODE_NEW_SKILL`. When the skill's recommended task goes into `grades-5-6`, branch 7 gives the lesson `combinatorics-enumeration-grades-5-6`, `REASON_CODE_QUEUE`. The lesson of `grades-1-2` does not suit such a learner by band and does not hold the order.
3. **Short essays**, an adult learner. The order of lessons: the graph did not order `first-sentences` and `reading-model-essays`, and both are of tier 1: `first-sentences` goes first, by ID; then `reading-model-essays`, of tier 1, before `paragraphs`, of tier 3, and `paragraphs`, and of the three lessons after `paragraphs` — `endings`, `improving-an-essay`, `writing-about-a-day`, by ID. All by branch 6a, `REASON_CODE_AUTHOR_ORDER`. `first-sentences` is mixed: the skill `choosing-a-first-sentence` with `verdict` comes by it, and then goes into the queue, but branch 6a is before branch 7, and the lessons go in a row. The lesson `writing-about-a-day` is only for `adult`, and `api` does not give it to a child, and `reading-model-essays` is without tasks, with a skill `none`.
4. **AI closed.** The pool has only tasks answered by tapping, and the lessons are only of such tasks. If there are none of them in any module either, the second pass changes nothing, and the choice is "nothing to give" with a cause from `api`.

**What the simulator will check** — in addition to the lists above:
- a new skill with a verdict that has a lesson of its band begins with a lesson, and a new band with a lesson of this band;
- the lessons of the author's order go in the order of the graph, and "goes in the author's order" of the first slice's success criterion is their share among the issues to skills without a verdict;
- "nothing to give" does not happen while an open skill has a candidate — a task where it is primary and every other primary skill is open — other than the task of the last attempt; except for two cases where branches 8 and 9 have nothing to give: an open skill out of reach has no bands where it is taught — it does not get onto "the bottom" —, and each open skill with candidates waits for its lesson of the author's order, while the learner left the run of a predecessor lesson. On a skill from the map — while there is a lesson or a task on it. The tasks of skills closed by a prerequisite wait for it;
- in the second pass a task with a seen solution comes no earlier than the others.

### 3.4. Repetition

Here — when a mastered skill whose review date has come comes back for repetition, and how repetition shares the module's choices with the other branches of the core of the rule. The sign of repetition and forgetting are the model's: `learner-model` gives the review date, and "time to review" is a time of the choice no earlier than the date; the engine has no second threshold.

**What the rule reads.** From `Read` for the time of the choice: for a mastered skill — the review date, with the mark "already" if the threshold does not hold even without forgetting, and the P of the items with retention — for the corridor ([`learner-model`'s architecture](https://github.com/altnovatrail/learner-model/blob/98ba29f/docs/architecture.md) at 98ba29f). Repetition needs nothing more from the model. The counter of the module's choices n is from the summary of issues, the same as for the turn of the probe: a task and a lesson are one choice each.

**A skill due for review** is a skill with `verdict` in a module of the engine, mastered for the queue, that has candidates, and the time of the choice is no earlier than its review date or the date is marked "already". A mastered skill whose recommended task has gone above the mastery band is not counted as a skill due for review: it goes into the queue by branch 7, and there by a lesson of the new band. Skills with `rubric` and `none` are not repeated: they have no model. The review date is no earlier than τ₀ = 1 day after the skill's last practice, so on the day of practice there is no repetition, except for a date marked "already": for such a skill the threshold does not hold even without forgetting, and practice does not remove the mark. "Time to review" is a time of the choice no earlier than `Review.At` from `Read`, or the mark `Already`; a skill without the model's "mastered" has no review date, and the rule does not read it. A skill that waits for its lesson of the author's order is not taken by branch 5, as by branches 6–9.

**Branch 5, "Repetition",** the code `REASON_CODE_REPETITION`:
- **on the turn of repetition** — n > 5 and n mod 3 = 1, that is n = 7, 10, 13 and so on — a skill due for review. The turn does not coincide with the turn of the probe, n mod 6 = 5: that one has n mod 3 = 2. A turn taken by the lesson of an open run or by consolidation — branches 1 and 3, before the rotation of modules — does not come back, as with the turn of the probe. The turn of repetition, unlike the turn of the probe, does not wait for the end of the trial series: the series computes the level by the answers on any skill, and repetition does not get in its way;
- **outside the turn** — a skill due for review, when branches 6, 6a and 7 of the module gave nothing, before branches 8 and 9: otherwise branch 8 would give mastered skills that are not due, and branch 9 "the bottom of the ladder", and the skill due for review would wait for the turn;
- of several skills due for review — first one marked "already", and of those the one whose last practice is older, otherwise the first in the order of the graph would take every repetition; then the one with the earliest date; on a tie — the first in the order of the graph;
- the task — by "The task on the skill" of the core of the rule, and repetition gives no lesson. P with retention already sees forgetting, and after a break the first task is within the learner's strength. A skill due for review that is now out of reach is repeated too: the rule gives the task nearest to 0.775 — the easiest, as for a probe —, and a correct answer brings it back from the pause, as a correct probe does. The second pass works here too.

3 is a constant of the engine, as 6 is for the turn of the probe, and only the simulator `sim` changes it. There is no boundary of the day and no daily ceiling: there is no time zone in the input, and repetitions are no more than a third of the module's choices.

**After repetition.** On a correct answer the model moves the date further off: the stability grows the more, the lower the retention was. On a failure consolidation goes next, branch 3, if the skill is reachable; two failures in a row take "mastered" off, and the skill comes back into the queue by branch 7.

**With the other rules.** A mastered skill whose recommended task has gone above the mastery band is not a skill due for review: it goes into the queue by branch 7, whatever its review date. In the mode of the map the code is always `REASON_CODE_SKILL_CHOSEN`. On several modules each module has its own turn of repetition, on its turn in the rotation. The rule does not read the daily goal, and it is not among the engine's constraints.

**Example: school maths of grade 3** — one band, `grade-3`.
1. `times-tables` and `fractions-of-a-whole` are mastered, the learner is working on `division-with-remainder`.
2. Four days later the review date of `times-tables` has come. At n = 13 — the turn of repetition: branch 5, `times-tables`, a task in the corridor by P with retention, `REASON_CODE_REPETITION`. At n = 14 and 15 — new skills and the queue again; if `times-tables-d3` is failed, n = 14 is consolidation, branch 3.
3. The review date has come for both mastered skills — the one whose date is earlier goes first; the second waits for the next turn, n = 16.
4. After the holidays all the mastered skills are due, but the new goes on: repetition is once in three choices of the module.
5. All four are mastered — there is no new and no queue: repetitions go in a row, by date, before branch 8, and when no dates have come, branch 8 works.

**What the simulator will check** — in addition to the lists above:
- the share of repetitions among the module's choices is no more than a third while there is the new or the queue;
- a skill due for review waits for repetition no longer than so many choices of the module;
- the measures of forgetting of the criterion of `sim`, a false "mastered" after a break and the share of repetitions over time.

### 3.5. Reason codes, the seed and the repeat of a choice

Here — which code of `ReasonCode` each branch of the rule above gives, why the engine does without randomness, and what the record of the choice needs to repeat it without AI.

**Branches and codes**, by the [contracts text](../spec/contracts/README.md). A choice has one code — of the branch that made it, for a lesson as for a task, and in the second pass too.

| Branch | Code | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — the lesson of an open run | `REASON_CODE_OPEN_RUN` | the learner came back to the begun lesson `times-tables` |
| 2 — a skill from the map | `REASON_CODE_SKILL_CHOSEN` | a lesson, and then tasks on `pigeonhole-basic`, chosen on the map |
| 3 — consolidation | `REASON_CODE_CONSOLIDATION` | after an error in `gaps-34-d3-1` — `gaps-34-d1-1` |
| 4 — a probe | `REASON_CODE_PROBE` | at n = 11 — the easiest task of `counting-gaps`, on pause |
| 5 — repetition | `REASON_CODE_REPETITION` | at n = 13 — `times-tables`, whose review date has come |
| 6 — a new skill, a lesson on the new and "the bottom of the ladder" | `REASON_CODE_NEW_SKILL` | the lesson `combinatorics-enumeration-grades-3-4` |
| 6a — the author's order | `REASON_CODE_AUTHOR_ORDER` | the lesson `paragraphs` after `first-sentences` and `reading-model-essays` |
| 7 — the queue of a reachable skill not mastered | `REASON_CODE_QUEUE` | `combinatorics-enumeration` was issued before `counting-gaps` |
| 7 — a skill with the model's "mastered" whose task has gone above the mastery band | `REASON_CODE_ROTATION` | the lesson `combinatorics-enumeration-grades-5-6` for a skill mastered on `grades-3-4` |
| 8 — all the reachable skills are mastered, no review dates have come | `REASON_CODE_ROTATION` | school maths: all four skills are mastered, no review dates have come |
| 9 — the queue at "the bottom of the ladder" | `REASON_CODE_ROTATION` | no skill is reachable, `times-tables` was issued before |
| "nothing to give" | without a code | AI is closed, there are no tasks answered by tapping |

`REASON_CODE_NEW_SKILL` requires no reachability and is exact at "the bottom of the ladder". The rule opens a skill also when a prerequisite is counted rather than mastered — an anchor "unknown", a cycle between modules, a prerequisite with nothing to teach with, a prerequisite with `rubric` or `none` counted by a lesson — and a skill with evidence of its own, and the comment of `cause.proto` says so: "whose prerequisites the engine's rule counts as met, or which has none". `REASON_CODE_QUEUE` requires both reachability and "not mastered", so the three places get the ninth value, `REASON_CODE_ROTATION` = 9: "Rotation: a skill given before comes round again, the one not given for longest first, though it does not fit the queue of `REASON_CODE_QUEUE`: it is mastered, or no skill is within reach". For `REASON_CODE_QUEUE`, "for longest" is "in its module". These are the words of `cause.proto`; the words for the adult are written by `app`, and a new value does not change the meaning of the earlier ones. The rule gives each of the nine codes, and there is no code that it does not give. The golden vectors hold this: each scenario has a branch of this table, and the test requires its code, a scenario of each branch and each code at least once, `REASON_CODE_ROTATION` in all three places.

**There is no randomness.** The engine takes no generator, and it has no seed of the choice and no names of places of its own. It rests on facts:
- the rule of standalone was deterministic: "the same profile always produces the same brief" ([standalone's SPEC](https://github.com/mathtrail/mathtrail-standalone/blob/c6cbeb2/SPEC.md) at c6cbeb2);
- each tie of the rule is broken by an order: in the corridor — frequent traps, nearness to 0.775, P and the task's ID; in the queue — the time of issue and the order of the graph; in the rotation of modules — the time of issue and the module's full ID; for lessons — the order of lessons and the ID; for review dates — "already" with how long ago the practice was, the date and the order of the graph;
- the variety of tasks for the learner comes from templates and the attempt's seed, which `api` takes;
- the simulator checks "the same input gives the same output" without a seed too.

The price: learners in one state get one task and one order of unordered lessons. standalone set them apart by a hash of the learner's ID ([standalone's SPEC](https://github.com/mathtrail/mathtrail-standalone/blob/c6cbeb2/SPEC.md) at c6cbeb2), and the learner's ID is not among the engine's inputs.

**The record of the choice** is kept by `api`:
- **already there** — the versions of `engine` and `learner-model`, the package digests, the reason code, the release of `modules`, the number of the last evidence, the time of the choice, the grade and whether the pool was limited to tasks without AI;
- **the seed** — the field is empty: the rule takes no randomness, and this is seen from the version of `engine` in the same record. A repeat takes the same version, so a future version with randomness does not get in the way of old choices;
- **three fields for the engine** — the skill from the map: it changes the whole choice, and it cannot be restored from the code and the task; the age category at the time of the choice: by it `api` selects the pool and the lessons ([`api`'s architecture](https://github.com/altnovatrail/api/blob/06dad44/docs/architecture.md) at 06dad44), and it changes at the yearly refinement and at a change of country; the mark of which of the digests are the engine's modules, connected and suitable: the digests also name the modules of the log, which only `learner-model` gets, and connections change at any time;
- **the repeat derives** — the summary of issues it builds anew from the records of choices and attempts before this choice; the last attempt, its trap and outcome — from the records of attempts; the lesson runs and the closed attempts at tasks with a rubric it restores anyway: their reason and time of the end do not change;
- **does not keep** — interests and the daily goal: the rule does not read them.

`spec` holds the record, in `spec/docs/architecture.md` and `spec/docs/privacy.md`.

**What the simulator will check:**
- each choice of a task or a lesson has a code from the list, and with `REASON_CODE_ROTATION` branches 7, 8 and 9 have one too;
- the same input gives the same choice and the same code — without a seed;
- a repeat by the record of the choice and the log gives the same choice that was made.

### 3.6. Types

Here — what the engine takes and gives, with the fields and the rule that reads each. The names are the Go names of the code; the comments of the code are in English. Time is whole Unix seconds, UTC, as in `learner-model`. A field that may be absent by its meaning is a value with a flag beside it, without pointers, as in that library.

**The module and the call.** The Go module `github.com/altnovatrail/engine`, the package `engine`; at build time it depends only on `github.com/altnovatrail/learner-model` and the standard library. Consumers take it as a Go module by the release's tag — `go get github.com/altnovatrail/engine@<version>` —, and the tag is linked to the signed release by the release's file `engine-<version>.tar.gz`, the code without tests, which `release-verify` accepts. `learner-model` comes the same way, at the version `go.mod` requires, the lowest with a fast `Readout.Task`: its tag is linked to the signed release through `release-verify` of its file, and the module is held by `go.sum` and sum.golang.org ([`learner-model` v0.1.0](https://github.com/altnovatrail/learner-model/releases/tag/v0.1.0) and [v0.1.6](https://github.com/altnovatrail/learner-model/releases/tag/v0.1.6); `go.sum`). Dependabot does not raise it: a new version is brought by a task with the same check, and after the baseline by the gate of `sim`. The call is `Choose(Input) (Choice, error)`. An error is a value, without a panic: a module, a task of the pool or a lesson twice by ID, two open runs of one lesson, a skill of a lesson that is not in the graph of its module, a module of the engine without `ModuleReadout`, a P of an item with a verdict from `Readout.Task` outside [0; 1] — NaN and ∞ too — and any other error of `Readout.Task` itself about a task — its description, the record of its module in the model's graphs or of the module's current version: it comes wrapped in `ErrInvalid`. Beyond this — a task of the pool or a lesson not of its own module by ID, a run end outside `RunEnd`, an open run with a time of the end, a skill, a module or a task twice in the summary, a code of the summary outside `ReasonCode`, traps of an item twice or at a place where the task has no item, a skill twice in the results of the last attempt, a result where `FirstRight` is less than zero or greater than `Answered` — for the last attempt and for the result of a skill in the summary —, a skill of a graph not of its own module by ID, and an anchor that a graph of a module of the engine names without a reading of the model or with a status outside the list. The engine reads the P of the candidates itself: `Readout.Task` for each candidate of the pool of the engine's modules, once per call and before the rule — so a P outside [0; 1] gives an error whichever branch runs, and the rule reads P ready-made. Once per call the tasks of the pools by ID are built too, and the pool with P and the index "skill → tasks": the rule takes a skill's candidates from the index, and of them the first by the total order, without sorting. The checks go in one order, and each names the smallest ID that broke it: a permutation of the lists does not change the errors either. Runs of one lesson and attempts "Done" of one task repeat by right: a lesson is given again by a new run, and a task with `rubric` by a new attempt. The order of the lists in the input does not change the choice: the rule orders by itself — by the order of the graph, of lessons and of IDs.

**What the engine takes from `learner-model` as it is** ([`types.go`](https://github.com/altnovatrail/learner-model/blob/v0.1.6/types.go) and [`readout.go`](https://github.com/altnovatrail/learner-model/blob/v0.1.6/readout.go) of `learner-model` v0.1.6):

| Type | Field | Who reads it |
|---|---|---|
| `Graph` | `Module` — the module's full ID | the module's turn, the order of modules |

### 3.7. The build for the device and the same bits

Here — what the code keeps so that the engine gives the same choice on any build and can one day choose on the device, and what checks it. The model to follow is `learner-model`, which holds this in its code ([`learner-model`'s architecture](https://github.com/altnovatrail/learner-model/blob/6a6c843/docs/architecture.md) and [decisions](https://github.com/altnovatrail/learner-model/blob/6a6c843/docs/decisions.md) at 6a6c843).

**The promise.** The same input gives the same choice and the same code, bit for bit, on any build by Go's compiler, `gc`, of one version of `engine` and `learner-model`: on any architecture it knows, with any GOAMD64 and GOARM64, on any processor and in any version of Go no older than the one `go.mod` names, WebAssembly too. TinyGo and gccgo are outside the promise. The repeat of a choice stands on it: it takes the same version of `engine` from the record, but not the same build — `api` builds the engine into its binary, and between deployments its version of Go, its architecture or GOAMD64 may change.

**Where builds differ.** Addition, subtraction, multiplication, division and square root of IEEE 754 are rounded correctly, and Go allows fusing several operations into one, with one rounding — except those that an explicit conversion separates ([the Go specification of go1.27](https://github.com/golang/go/blob/go1.27.4/doc/go_spec.html)). The compiler fuses multiplication with addition or subtraction, and it turns a division by a constant with an exact reciprocal into a multiplication. The engine computes something of its own in three places: the P of a task on a skill — the mean P of its items, the nearness to 0.775 — a difference, and the comparisons with 0.70 and 0.85. The mean is a division by the number of items, a variable, and it is not fused: `s/float64(n) - 0.775` is a separate division and subtraction. But let a constant stand in the code, and the choice will differ: `s*0.5 - 0.775` and `(p1+p2)/2 - 0.775` are fused on arm64 and ppc64le, and the second also on s390x, riscv64 and loong64, while `float64((p1+p2)/2) - 0.775` is fused nowhere (measurements 12). The sum of the items' P is by the item's place: floating-point addition is not associative. The engine does not compute the items' P: `Readout.Task` of `learner-model` gives them, with its same bits.

**The rules of code** are the rules of `learner-model`'s code in what the engine needs:
1. The result of each `*` and `/` on floating-point numbers goes through `float64(…)` at once, if it is not an operand of another `*` or `/` or of a comparison. No `*=` and `/=` on such numbers, no `complex`, no type parameters in their arithmetic.
2. From `math` — only the functions with an exactly defined result, the same list as for `learner-model`: `Sqrt`, `Abs`, `Floor`, `Ceil`, `Trunc`, `Copysign`, `Signbit`, `Frexp`, `Ldexp`, `Float64bits`, `Float64frombits`, `IsNaN`, `IsInf`, `Inf` and the constants. The engine needs no exp and log; should it need them — `detmath` of `learner-model`, by a change of these rules.
3. A P of an item with a verdict outside [0; 1] — NaN, ∞ or simply outside the interval — is an error as a value, not a choice: a comparison with NaN is always false, and the choice would depend on the order of the checks.
4. The time, the counters of choices and the pause are `int64`: 24 hours is 86,400 seconds; the numbers of items in the result of a skill, `Answered` and `FirstRight`, are `int32`, and the rule "failure" compares them widened to `int64`. Floating-point numbers into integers — only with a check of the range.
5. Order — without iterating over maps, sorting with a total order, IDs byte by byte, without Unicode tables: the order of the graph, of lessons and of IDs is a rule, not chance.
6. No global mutable state — `api` calls the engine from many requests at once —, and no goroutines of its own.
7. Imports — only from the list below. No cgo, no `.s` files, no `//go:linkname`, no build tags. The rules and the list are for the core, the package `engine`; the glue with the app at stage 5 needs `syscall/js` and the build constraint `js && wasm`, and it will have a list of its own — by a change of these rules.

**Dependencies** — Go's standard library by the list of `learner-model` and the root package `github.com/altnovatrail/learner-model`. In the code: `math` — only the functions of rule 2 —, `cmp`, `slices`, `errors`, `strconv` and `strings` — only the byte-wise functions. The tests — `bytes`, `crypto/sha256`, `fmt`, `math/rand/v2`, `os`, `path/filepath` and `testing`, and `compress/gzip` and `encoding/json` read the packages of a release of `modules` for the golden vectors — for the tests, not the code; `os` and `path/filepath` also read `cause.proto` in the `spec` submodule. This is the list of `.github/coderules.json` below: the packages `go/*` and `runtime`, which the list of `learner-model` gives to its own test of the rules, are not in it, since the test of the rules is the tool of `.github`. Not taken — the Go module `spec`, `module-kit`, third-party modules, `time`, `math/rand`, `sync`, `unsafe`, `reflect`, `runtime` and encoders, as for `learner-model`. A new package goes into the list by a change of these rules.

**For the device.** The core builds for the browser, `GOOS=js GOARCH=wasm`, and for WASI, `GOOS=wasip1 GOARCH=wasm`, as it is. Neither port is first-class in Go — `"FirstClass": false` in `go tool dist list -json` of the Go that `.devcontainer/Dockerfile` pins —, and the build of the smallest Go program for wasm is about 2 MB, after gzip about 500 KB ([Go Wiki: WebAssembly](https://github.com/golang/wiki/blob/a5e3edeb62a592ab9e6cdb2b64b75b8179a5d61d/WebAssembly.md) at a5e3ede); the engine with the model will be larger, and by how much the build at stage 5 will show.
